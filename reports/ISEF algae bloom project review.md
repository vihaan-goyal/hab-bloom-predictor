# Close the loop to break the ceiling

No harmful-algal-bloom forecasting or detection project found in the ISEF database from 2016 to 2026 has won a First Award, Best of Category or a top award. The ceiling for forecasting projects has been a **Second Award** ($2,000-$2,400) plus agency special awards: Schweinfurth 2021, Chen 2024, Ma 2024 and Choudhury 2026 all stopped there. The highest verified HAB result of any kind is a **First Award** in Microbiology for a programmed-cell-death algicide in 2019 ([ISEF abstract](https://abstracts.societyforscience.org/Home/FullAbstract?projectId=17934)). Most top environmental awards from 2022 to 2026 went to a **built device with one hard percentage**. Examples are 84-94% microplastic removal (2024 Gordon E. Moore Award), 94% detection accuracy from an AI robot turtle tested in 10 bodies of water (2026 Moore), and 80% pollutant removal by a plant-waste sponge (2026 Young Scientist Award) ([SfS 2024](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/high-schoolers-win-more-than-9-million-at-regeneron-isef-2024/), [SfS 2026](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-2026-grand-awards/)). Our project is already more rigorous than every HAB forecaster in the catalog. It is the only one with AUC and confidence intervals, lift over a base rate, pre-registration, a label audit and transfer to a second estuary. It is also the only one found where a forecast automatically switches a treatment on and off. The one thing it lacks, and the thing top winners have, is a measured physical outcome. So the tank experiment matters more than any further modeling. It should show that "forecast-triggered treatment beats late treatment by X% in chlorophyll, at Y% of the treatment dose", as a single number. Several compliance issues could also sink the project regardless of quality: the generative-AI rules, Form 7 for continuation work, and SRC pre-approval if the "non-toxic algae" are cyanobacteria.

A note on evidence. Award placings, titles, methods and rules below are **verified** on the cited Society for Science, ISEF-database, agency or news pages. Explanations of *why* a project placed are **inferences**: Society for Science does not publish judges' scoring or caucus reasoning, and no first-hand judge interviews were found. Coaching-site advice is treated as secondary.

The pattern in this table is the most useful single finding. **Headline accuracy did not predict placing.** The 96.65% and >95% projects won nothing. Li placed while openly reporting 63% skill on bloom events, and went on to STS finalist. Chen placed with R² 0.59. What the placers share (inferred) is completeness: a baseline beaten, a partner, a tool or piece of hardware, and a stated mechanism. That favors us, because our AUC of 0.80 at a 6.3% base rate is honest, and honest numbers look weaker than inflated accuracy unless the base rate is explained up front. The Schweinfurth, Ma and Ye/Wang "accuracy" figures almost certainly benefit from class imbalance (inference). A judge who has seen them will need to be told why lift 5.0 at a 6.3% base rate is the stronger claim.

## Top environmental awards go to a built object with one hard number

At the top tier, the pattern is strong and consistent. The 2024 Moore Award (Huang & Ou, ultrasonic microplastic filter) won on **lab-only single-pass tests** and a stated scale-up plan. So field data helps but is not required. The 2026 Moore (Evan Budz) reused his earlier robot turtle and added holographic AI detection across 10 water bodies. Lakshmi Agrawal climbed from 2025 EAEV First (foam fractionation) to a 2026 Young Scientist Award (6PPD-quinone sponges, framed around salmon in Puget Sound) ([SfS 2025](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-2025-full-awards/), [SfS 2026](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-2026-grand-awards/)). Every one pairs an object judges can see with a single removal or detection percentage, a named victim or place, and usually a multi-year build. ML wins at the top only when it is embodied in hardware or imaging: Estrada's robotic drought camera (2021 Moore) and Puri's drone thermal poacher detector (2022 Scripps) ([SfS 2021](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/2021-regeneron-isef-top-winners/), [SfS 2022](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/2022-regeneron-isef-top-winners/)). A strong AI and IoT wildfire sensor network won only a Second Award in Embedded Systems in 2025 ([SfS 2025](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-2025-full-awards/)).

Closed-loop "sense and act" projects exist at category level but are partial. XinYan Chen's formaldehyde monitor-plus-photocatalyst won ENEV First in 2025. Aryan Mago's wearable VOC detection and mitigation won ENEV First in 2023. Tremsin's multivariable wildfire early warning won EAEV Best of Category in 2018 ([SfS 2025](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-2025-full-awards/), [SfS 2023](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-full-awards-2023/), [SfS 2018](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/intel-international-science-and-engineering-fair-2018-grand-award-winners/)). The inference is that judges reward "detect, then act" when the act is demonstrated. None of these used a forecast to trigger water treatment.

Measured against this, we **match** several winning patterns. We have a named place and stakeholder (Long Island Sound, CT DEEP, which confirmed the CTD change and advised using lab data). We have a built and verified artifact: the Arduino replayed 3,287 days with 0 mismatches against the Python controller. We have an honest baseline (lift over climatology), multi-year depth and statistical rigor well beyond any HAB precedent. We **miss** four things: a single physical outcome number, any test on real water or a real bloom organism, a cost figure, and non-target safety data. Non-target safety is the one check Muro reported and judges will ask about for peroxide and curcumin. There is also a risk of **diffuseness**. Two models, five treatments, a Monte Carlo simulation and a 256-paper scoreboard read as breadth. Winners present one object and one number (inference). The Anantapantula meta-analysis, which found most field treatments ineffective, is a threat as well as a precedent. Our kelp, oyster and aeration arms need to answer why a small-scale timing effect would survive where field trials failed.

## The rubric rewards the interview, and three rules can disqualify

Both ISEF tracks are scored out of 100 ([Grand Award Criteria](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/)):

| Criterion | Points | Science track asks | Engineering track asks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Research Question / Problem | 10 | Testable question | Practical need, criteria and constraints |
| Design & Methodology | 15 | Variables and controls | Alternatives explored, prototype built |
| Execution | 20 | Systematic data, reproducibility, appropriate statistics | Prototype tested "in multiple conditions/trials" |
| Creativity & Potential Impact | 20 | Same for both | Same for both |
| Presentation: poster | 10 | Same for both | Same for both |
| Presentation: interview | 25 | Same for both | Same for both |

The **25-point interview** outweighs every other line. It explicitly scores understanding of the limitations, how independently the students did the work, and, for teams, "contributions to and understanding of project by all members" ([criteria](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/)). ISEF 2027 interviews run in 15-minute slots on May 12, 2027 ([Grand Award Judging](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/)). Judges hold a PhD or equivalent experience, so ML judges will probe for leakage, temporal splits, baselines and test-set reuse ([Lones, arXiv 2108.02497](https://arxiv.org/html/2108.02497v5)). Our label rebuild (precision falling from 0.50 to 0.32 after the fluorometer audit) is interview gold, but only if both students can tell it unprompted (inference). Because ENEV is defined as engineering processes that solve water and pollution problems ([ENEV definition](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/categories-and-subcategories/environmental-engineering/)), the loop fits ENEV under the engineering track. That track requires stated criteria and constraints (lead time, false-alarm budget, dose, cost), alternatives considered, and testing across multiple conditions.

**Generative AI** is the highest-stakes rule for this project. The Society for Science table allows AI-written code only "with explicit citation stating which portions of the code were AI generated and with a log of the prompts". It allows AI help choosing statistical tests only with a log, and "interpretation of data must be done by the student". It marks as **"Never acceptable"** using AI to initially write the research plan, abstract, paper or poster, to produce conclusions or future steps, or to produce a starter bibliography or supporting citations ([Generative AI Use Table](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2026/Rules/Generative-AI-Use-Table.pdf)). Much of this project's code, notes, pre-registration text and the 256-paper evidence scoreboard were produced with Claude (inference from the repository). Three consequences follow. The scoreboard cannot be presented as the students' bibliography unless they rebuild or re-verify it themselves. The research plan and pre-registered hypotheses shown to the SRC must be written by the students. And every AI-generated code file needs an attribution and a prompt log. Affiliated fairs such as CSEF may be stricter.

**Continuation rules** limit what counts. Only work in 12 continuous months, starting no earlier than January 2026, is judged. Form 7 must be displayed. "Repetition of previous experimentation with the same methodology and research question, even with an increased sample size" is an unacceptable continuation. Earlier raw data cannot appear on the board, and judges "focus ONLY on the current year's work" ([Rules for All Projects](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/rules-for-all-projects/), [criteria](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/)). The project start date is when data collection begins ([Rules FAQ](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/faq/)). If any forecasting work predates January 2026, it has to be framed as prior work, and the new variables (the lab-label rebuild, the controller and the tank arms) as this year's.

**Biological and chemical rules** shape the tank design. Cyanobacteria are listed as potentially hazardous microorganisms. Culturing them needs SRC pre-approval and a lab setting, and home culturing is prohibited. Eukaryotic "non-toxic algae" may fall under the lighter protist route, which the rules page does not state explicitly, so ask the SRC. Students may not collect water during an active HAB, and releasing non-native species is prohibited ([PHBA rules](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/potentially-hazardous-biological-agents/)). Peroxide and curcumin need Form 3, SDS review and an EPA-compliant disposal plan written before experimentation ([Hazardous Chemicals](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/hazardous-chemicals-activities-or-devices/)). Oysters are invertebrates and fall outside the vertebrate rules ([Vertebrate rules](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/vertebrate-animals/)). A two-person team is within ISEF's limit of three, and membership is frozen once the project competes ([Rules for All Projects](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/rules-for-all-projects/)).

On special awards, NOAA gave **no** special award at ISEF 2026 ([NOAA](https://www.noaa.gov/office-education/outreach-communication/international-science-and-engineering-fair)). The 2026 environmental special awards came from Aramco, KACST and NTSEC, alongside AAAI, ACM and AMS ([ISEF 2026 Special Awards](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-2026-special-awards-ceremony/)). The U.S. Stockholm Junior Water Prize caps teams at **two**, which our team meets. Its state deadline is April 15, and it weights the written paper heavily ([WEF SJWP](https://www.wef.org/membership--community/students--young-professionals/sjwp/sjwp-eligibility-and-entry-requirements/)). Three HAB students in this catalog converted to STS or SJWP recognition (Li, Chen, Schweinfurth), so a strong paper is a second path.

## Conclusion

The HAB category at ISEF has a clear ceiling, and the precedents explain it. Forecasters have been rewarded for rigor and partnerships but capped at Second Award. Treatment projects climbed higher only by touching real water with a working object. Our project is the first found to sit on both sides of that divide, and its forecast is already stronger than the whole catalog on the dimensions judges probe: base-rate-aware metrics, a label audit and a transfer test. The upside therefore depends almost entirely on a single experimental result. The project will be judged on whether the tank shows that *timing*, not just treatment, changes the outcome, not on the forecast. A null result from a properly controlled, pre-registered test is still a defensible project. An unapproved or AI-authored one is not.

The less obvious implication is that the project's greatest risk is procedural rather than scientific. The same AI-assisted workflow that produced the rigor also produced documents the rules forbid students to submit as their own. Fixing that, and moving the SRC approval ahead of the tank build, costs little and protects everything else.
