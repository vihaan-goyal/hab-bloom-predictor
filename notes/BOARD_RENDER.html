On the table

Question

Can a coastal station forecast its own harmful algal bloom a week ahead, and what sets the limit?

Harmful blooms close shellfish beds and beaches with days of warning at best. Every monitoring station already logs chlorophyll, oxygen, temperature and salinity. Is the run-up to a bloom visible in that record, and if so, why are forecasts in some bays sharp and in others blunt?

Hypotheses

  1. H1 A 7-day bloom-onset probability can be forecast from a station's own recent record: AUC above 0.7, precision above the base rate.
  2. H2 What limits precision is bloom rarity, not model class, features, data volume or sampling frequency. Pre-registered 2026-09-01. Outcome 2026-09-23: mostly rarity, not only.
  3. H3 A model trained in Narragansett Bay transfers to other water bodies with no retraining, after rescaling chlorophyll to the site's own distribution.
  4. H4 Skill holds on forecasts issued before the outcome is known. Protocol frozen 2026-09-06.

Data

  • Long Island Sound (CT DEEP): 50 stations, 1993–2025, boat visits every 2–3 weeks, 11,447 station-days, lab chlorophyll.
  • Narragansett Bay (RIDEM sondes): 18 stations, 2005–2023, a reading every 15 minutes, 4.5 million rows aggregated to 42,315 station-days.
  • Transfer set: six foreign networks (144,475 station-days) and 87 public ERDDAP sonde sites on three continents.
87 sites the exported model runs on today

Method

  • Label: daily chlorophyll above 10 µg/L (or the site's own 75th percentile) within the next 7 days; 21 days for boat data.
  • Features: the station's own history only. Chlorophyll lags and rolling means, trend, anomaly vs climatology, oxygen, temperature, salinity, month.
  • Models: logistic regression and gradient boosting; trained on early years, tested on years never touched during development.
  • Scoring: onset rows only, so persistence cannot inflate skill. Precision always beside its base rate. Lift = precision ÷ base rate. 95 % CIs by station-year clustered bootstrap.
  • Every test pre-registered; negative results kept.

1 · It works

Narragansett: onset precision 0.66 [0.62, 0.69] over nine held-out years, lift 2.5.

Beats every trivial rule. Long Island Sound with the same recipe: AUC 0.825, but precision 0.117 at 0.744 recall (lab-scale label). The ranking skill is real in both bays. The precision is not.

2 · Why the Sound is capped

Far rarer, and a little less separable.

Project every test day onto the model's decision axis. Bloom and no-bloom classes overlap by 0.62 in the Sound and 0.52 in Narragansett: the Sound separates somewhat worse. And blooms are 2.6 % of onset days there against 35 %, so any cut that catches most Sound blooms also catches hundreds of quiet days. Precision 0.055 vs 0.64: mostly rarity, not only.

3 · It transfers

One bay's model, 74 sites, no retraining.

Exported once, chlorophyll rescaled to each site's own distribution. Median lift 1.58, AUC 0.74; the interval clears 1.0 at 67 sites across the Gulf of Mexico, California, the Great Lakes, Alaska, British Columbia and the Pacific islands.

4 · What a manager gets

Half the boat trips per bloom found.

At eight station visits a month, one confirmed bloom costs 17.8 calendar visits in the Sound and 9.7 alert-directed; 3.8 versus 1.4 in Narragansett. The forecast is a triage tool, and that is the unit to sell it in.

In a bloom-rare system, no sampling cadence and no model class produces high-precision alerts, because precision is bounded mostly by rarity. The actionable quantity is lift over the calendar, and it is 2 to 3× in both bays.

Prospective test

Conclusions

  1. Bloom onset is forecastable a week ahead from a station's own record; AUC 0.77–0.83 in the Sound, 0.84 in Narragansett.
  2. Alert precision is set mostly by bloom rarity, not only: the Sound also separates the classes less well (overlap 0.62 against 0.52). Model, features, data volume and cadence were each tested and excluded.
  3. The Sound's old "2014 cliff" was mostly a CTD sensor scale change (SeaBird to YSI EXO2 around 2009/2010), confirmed by CT DEEP; the label now uses DEEP's lab scale. The lab record shows a real, temporary low in 2012–2017, not a 2014 step.
  4. The precursor signature exports: 67 of 74 sites gain from a model that never saw them.
  5. The unit for a manager is visits per confirmed bloom; the forecast roughly halves it.

Limitations

  • Sonde fluorescence reads 1.3–1.6× above lab chlorophyll; both bays use the same 10 µg/L cut.
  • Correlational: low oxygen marks bloom-prone water, it does not cause blooms.
  • Sound decision-value numbers rest on 43 blooms.
  • Nothing is prospective until the ledger fills.
