# Reviewer 2 report on device design v2

Lens: capture physics, chemistry of the magnetic clay in seawater, internal consistency of the run protocol, statistics, controller logic, field realism, compliance. Numbers below were computed from v2's own stated values (70 um floc, 45 wt% magnetite, 0.3 T face field falling to 0.17 T at 10 mm, 2 cm/s rake, settling 0.3-1 mm/s).

## A. Findings

### Blocking

**1. The rake chases flocs that are no longer in the water column, and the sleeve does not fit the magnets.** At v2's own settling velocity (0.3-1 mm/s, v2 s1) a floc crosses the 16 cm depth in 160-530 s. The paddle stops at T16 and the rake runs T16-T26, so by pass 2-4 most of the dose is on the floor; the ten water-column passes at 2 cm/s are sweeping a thinning suspension, and the "floor sweep" as written is a static 5 min lay-down, not a sweep. The capture physics itself holds: a 70 um floc at 5-16% solids (Stokes-consistent with 0.3-1 mm/s) carries 13-44 ng magnetite; at Ms 60-80 A m2/kg for co-precipitated ~10 nm Fe3O4 (MDPI Materials 2013; ACS Omega 2022) and a pole-face gradient of ~25 T/m, the magnetic force is 2-6e-8 N against Stokes drag at 2 cm/s of 1.3e-8 N and buoyant weight of 2-7e-10 N; magnetophoretic velocity is 3-10 cm/s inside 5 mm, 1-4 mm/s at 20 mm. So a 5 mm shell is realistic in front of the two 20 mm pole faces, not on the 10 mm sides or in the wake (rod Re ~550), and a moving cylinder carries flocs around itself on streamlines. Geometry: a 40x20x10 block has a 22.4 mm diagonal; 3/4 in Sch 40 PVC has ID 20.9 mm (ASTM D1785), so the stacks do not fit. 1 in Sch 40 (ID 26.6, OD 33.4 mm) does, with the capture surface ~5 mm from the pole (~0.28 T). Fix: (i) 1 in sleeves, shell from the Hall map; (ii) invert the sequence: 15 min quiescent settle after T16, then a **floor raster** (rods dragged along the bottom at ~1 cm/s in overlapping lanes, pole faces down, 2-3 rasters), then 2-3 water-column passes; (iii) usable pad area is pole faces only, ~256 cm2, so 120-240 mL of pad is 5-9 mm thick with outer layers at 8-14 mm from the pole where holding force equals drag; withdraw at <1 cm/s or add two stacks (+$32).

**2. The three-arm comparison is not a comparison, and the jar sampling volumes do not exist.** The magnetic arm is 20 L under a paddle at G ~30 s-1; the two controls are 2 L jars on a magnetic stirrer at "matched tip speed" (v2 s3), which does not give matched G (a short bar at high rpm gives high local shear and a different Gt). Then at T26 the protocol pulls 1 L for Chl from each 2 L jar, plus count, DO, Fe and Al, and repeats at 2, 5 and 24 h, and reserves 1 L for regrowth: that is >4 L drawn from a 2 L vessel, and "just below the surface" in a jar that is half-emptied at the first draw. Fix: put the three-arm test in matched vessels on a common rig (three 2 L jars under three paddles on one shaft, or three $25 aquaria), and use the 20 L tank for device metrics only (recovery fraction, video, Chl-in-pad). Table every draw per vessel; at 1e4 cells/mL Chl is already conceded as noise after 90% removal (v2 weakness 2), so drop the 1 L draws and take Chl (250 mL) at t0 and 5 h only.

**3. D90 at 1e4 vs 1e5 cannot be resolved with n = 2 and may sit below the dose series.** Log-linear interpolation between five doses with a count-derived RE error of +/-5-7 points (Poisson at 400 cells plus jar variance) on a slope of ~20 RE points per 0.5 log10 g/L gives +/-0.17 log10 on each D90, i.e. a factor 1.5; the difference of two such D90s carries +/-0.24 log10, so only a ratio >2.5-3x would be detectable. The literature gives no assurance the ratio is that large in this range: kaolinite-PAC removed 100% of *A. minutum* within 2 h at every dose from 0.1 g/L and at both 1e4 and 2e4 cells/mL (PMC12390390, five replicates per cell), so the 1e4 D90 may be **below 0.1 g/L** and unresolvable with the 0.1-1.0 series. Fix: doses 0.025, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.8 g/L (half-log), n = 3 per dose and density, fit a two-parameter logistic to RE vs log dose and bootstrap D90; with SD(log10 D90) ~0.12 at n = 3, power is ~0.8 for a 2x ratio at alpha 0.05 (about 0.5 at n = 2). Count only t0 and 5 h. Minimum defensible design: 6 doses x 2 densities x 3 reps x 2 clay arms + 3 x 2 untreated = 78 jars, ~156 counts; do the plain-PAC-kaolinite arm at 1e5 only (Chl works there) if time is short.

### Major

**4. The magnetic clay is a mixture, not a bonded composite; state it and measure it.** NaOH is over-specified: 98 g FeCl3.6H2O + 45 g FeCl2.4H2O need 1.54 mol OH- (62 g NaOH); 80 g is 2.0 mol and leaves 0.46 mol excess in 5.2 L, pH ~12.9, not 11; titrate by the pen, do not weigh in. Fe3+ limits Fe3O4 to 42 g (45.6 wt% of ~92 g product, v2's number), but co-precipitated magnetic kaolinite in the literature shows composite Ms as low as 6 emu/g (Applied Water Science 2021) against 60-80 emu/g for the particles, i.e. ~10% effective loading, because much of the Fe3O4 nucleates free. The magnet-decant keeps free magnetite, so the product is kaolinite plus magnetite aggregates; in seawater (I ~0.7 M) both collapse and PAC bridges them, so the *floc* is magnetic either way, but "recovery of dosed clay" then depends on kaolinite riding with magnetite, and a low recovery would be misread as rake failure. Fix: (a) a detachment test before and after PAC: 1 g product in 1 L filtered seawater at G ~30 for 15 min, hold on a magnet, dry and weigh the non-magnetic residue; (b) calibrate the pull test against the Plan B magnetite powder to report wt% magnetite; (c) acid-digest 0.1 g and read Fe with the kit.

**5. The balance pull test as written is unreliable on the school balance.** Electromagnetic-force-compensation balances are surrounded by a residual field; a magnet on the pan gives "stable but non-repeatable" readings that depend on its position and orientation (Sartorius white paper, 2020). The recommended remedies are distance (interaction falls quadratically) or below-balance weighing. Fix: sample in a vial on the pan on a >=10 cm plastic riser, magnet on a fixed stand above at a set gap; record the magnet-only zero; or hang the magnet below the pan. Also the SS49E saturates at ~0.1 T, so the 0 and 5 mm map points are unreadable; map 10-40 mm and fit the block formula (R1) back to the face.

**6. PAC mass basis is ambiguous by 3x.** The BOM buys "PAC ~30% liquid"; the recipe is 5:1 clay:PAC by mass on a solid basis (IOCAS, CLAY s1). Using liquid mass under-doses PAC 3x and shifts the product-basis dose. Fix: dry a 10 g aliquot to get solids, or buy PAC powder (28-30% Al2O3), and state the basis in the dose definition.

**7. Controller: the density bands cannot fire, the lockout has an edge, and pre-emption in a flushed cell is unexamined.** Both chl bands (<=0.5 x p75, and p75/2 to p75) lie under the onset gate (chl_today <= p75), so the "high-density D90" from the 1e5 jars is never used; the 1e5 series is the counterfactual for the board, not a controller input. A 7 d lockout against a 7 d issuance cadence turns on >= vs > and on poll time; define it as "skip the next issue_date" instead. VERIFY has no pass criterion. Deeper: the claim is that removing cells at 1e4 today prevents a bloom forecast within 7 d; regrowth suppression (30-80 d, CLAY s2) is a beaker result, and in a slip that exchanges every tide the seed population is replaced from outside. Fix: bands at 3e3 and 1e4 cells/mL (add a third low jar density or drop bands); lockout by issue_date; VERIFY = count at 24 h < 0.3 x C0 or re-flag; say in s5 that pre-emption is an enclosed-cell or embayment concept, not a marina-slip one.

**8. Field concept violates curtain practice and would deposit the missed floc outside the cell.** Vendor and DOT guidance for tidal water is a 1-2 ft clearance above the bed at mean low water and never a skirt that touches or folds, because it fans sediment, snags and loads up (Elastec; SCDOT SC-M-815-22); curtains "slow water movement, not stop it." A 4.5 m folding skirt on a 2-4.2 m cell is an open-bottomed pen. At 0.3-1 mm/s the floc reaches a 2 m bed in 0.5-2 h, long before a 12.5 h drum run, so most floc is on the bed, not at a floor intake, and what the drum misses leaves under the skirt on the next flood/ebb and settles outside: the deposition outcome the design exists to avoid. Tracer: Rhodamine WT at ~10 ppb (20 g in 2,000 m3), a borrowed fluorometer (0.1 ppb detection; YSI/Turner), inside and outside samples over >=2 tidal cycles, a pre-dye background, and DEEP sign-off since dye is a discharge. Fix: size the cell to one slack window (600 m3, one drum), add a bed-magnet drag survey inside and outside the curtain as the deposition metric, and report "fraction of dosed magnetite recovered."

**9. Counting time is the hidden critical path.** At 1e3 cells/mL (90% removal at 1e4), >=400 cells means ~400 uL, i.e. ~400 Sedgewick-Rafter grids, 1-2 h per sample. Jars (finding 3) plus tanks at four time points x three arms x four runs are 150-250 counts, or 100-200 microscope hours between Nov 2 and Dec 21. Fix: count t0 and 5 h only; use Chl for the 1e5 series; recruit a second counter and report inter-counter agreement.

### Minor

**10. ISEF paperwork is incomplete.** Protists are exempt from SRC pre-review "but require a Risk Assessment Form 3" (Society for Science PHBA rules); hazardous chemicals also need Form 3 and a Designated Supervisor on Form 1, with Form 1B approved before experimentation. Say which adult signs.
**11. Chl reporting threshold.** A >= 0.02 corresponds to RE <= ~70%; say Chl cannot report the 90% endpoint at 1e4.
**12. Rod wake contradicts the floor sweep.** Re ~550 shedding resuspends settled floc; raster the floor first, water column last.
**13. Synthesis vessel.** 10 M NaOH into 60 C FeCl3 in a plastic bucket: use HDPE or borosilicate and add over 10 min.

## B. Fixes from review 1 that v2 did not fully land

- R1 #1 (capture): rods adopted, but no capacity check per pole face (256 cm2 usable, 5-9 mm pad), the sleeve size is wrong, and the settle-before-rake timing was not thought through (finding 1).
- R1 #7 (mixing): tank G is now stated, but the control jars' "matched tip speed" is not matched G (finding 2).
- R1 #8 (controls): arms exist but in unmatched vessels with impossible draw volumes (finding 2).
- R1 #4 (pull test, Hall map): both added, both flawed as instruments (finding 5).
- R1 #5 (NaOH): switched, but the mass is 30% over and pH target unreachable by weight (finding 4).
- R1 #9e (lockout): 7 d against a 7 d cadence is an edge case (finding 7).
- R1 #12 (dose basis): product basis stated, but the PAC solid/liquid basis is not (finding 6).
- R1 #14 (ISEF): Form 3 named; Designated Supervisor and Form 1B not (finding 10).

## C. What v2 gets right

The force budget supports the rake in principle: near a pole face the magnetic force exceeds drag at 2 cm/s by 2-6x and exceeds the floc's buoyant weight by 100x, so a 5-10 mm capture shell over the pole faces is physically sound and the Hall-map-plus-formula approach is the right way to bound it. Dropping the pipe, dropping DAF for 20-30 ppt tidal water, keeping plain PAC-kaolinite as the comparison arm, defining dose on total product, moving to Sedgewick-Rafter counts and extracted Chl, and control-normalised RE are all correct. Plan B (co-dosed magnetite) is a genuine fallback and the same powder doubles as a magnetisation standard. The seawater 3x dose factor and the 1 g/L cap are right. The budget is honest about borrows, the culture timeline is realistic, and the weakness list now leads with the failure modes that matter.

## D. Three highest-value changes for v3

1. Re-sequence capture as settle, floor raster, then water-column passes, in 1 in sleeves, with capacity sized on pole-face area, and verify it on the clay-only blank before any culture is dosed.
2. Move the three-arm comparison into matched 2 L vessels on one paddle rig with a written draw-volume table, use the 20 L tank only for device metrics, and extend the jar dose series down to 0.025 g/L with n = 3 and a fitted logistic for D90.
3. Fix the instruments that gate Plan B and the field map (riser-based pull test calibrated to magnetite powder, a seawater detachment test, Hall map from 10 mm out) and restate the field concept as a one-slack 600 m3 cell with a bed-magnet deposition survey instead of a folding skirt.
