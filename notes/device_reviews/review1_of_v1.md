# Reviewer 1 report on device design v1

## A. Findings (ranked)

### Blocking

**1. Capture geometry cannot hold the dose.** A 1.5 in Sch 40 clear pipe has ID ~40 mm. Using the standard block-magnet field formula with Br = 1.45 T (N52 40x20x10), the axial field is ~0.41 T at the face, ~0.33 T at 3 mm, ~0.17 T at 10 mm, ~0.07 T at 20 mm and ~0.017 T on the far wall (40 mm). Catalog values for this size (4,000-5,200 G face, suprememagnets/couragemagnet) agree. So the "0.5 T" is only in the bottom few mm, and the force (proportional to gradient) on flocs in the upper three-quarters of the bore is negligible; in a 12 s pass (60 cm at 5 cm/s) they reach the wall only by settling. Worse, capacity: 8 blocks x 40 mm = 32 cm x 2 cm = 64 cm2 of magnet footprint, and 18-22 g of PAC-clay floc at 5-10% solids is 200-400 mL of pad, i.e. 3-6 cm deep on 64 cm2, which is deeper than the useful field and would fill the bore. Realistic single-pass capacity is ~5 mm of pad = ~3 g solids, about 15% of the dose. Fix: replace the pipe with a geometry in which every floc passes within ~5 mm of a magnet face and the area is 5-10x larger: (a) a flat magnetic tray: a 60 x 10 cm acrylic channel with the blocks under a 2-3 mm floor and a 5 mm deep sheet flow (classic wet low-intensity separator); or (b) magnet-rods-in-sleeves in the tank: blocks stacked inside capped 1 in PVC sleeves (~250 cm2 per rod), pull the magnets out to release. Either also solves release (v1's "slide magnets off and flush 500 mL" will strand floc). Put the pump intake at the tank floor where 70 um floc (settling ~0.3-1 mm/s) collects.

**2. The three numbers are not measurable at 1e4 cells/mL with the listed instruments.** (i) 665/750 in-vivo absorbance: S. costatum holds ~0.4 pg Chl-a per cell (Springer, Mar. Biol.), so 1e4 cells/mL = ~4 ug/L; in-vivo absorption ~0.015 m2/mg gives A ~ 3e-4 in a 1 cm cuvette, far below a school spectrophotometer's ~0.005 A noise floor. Fix: filter 250-500 mL onto GF/F, extract in 5 mL 90% acetone, read 664/750 with the 87.67 L/g/cm coefficient (UMCES SOP): A ~0.035-0.09, usable, and cheap ($15 acetone). (ii) Hemocytometer: chamber volume ~0.9 uL, so 1e4 cells/mL gives ~9 cells per chamber (+/-33% Poisson) and Skeletonema chains make it worse. Fix: Sedgewick-Rafter chamber (1 mL, ~$60; ANACC/CSIRO method) with Lugol, count >=400 cells. (iii) DFRobot SEN0189: DFRobot's own FAQ says it is qualitative, +/-0.3 V on a 4.1 V clear-water output; it cannot see algae at any density used here and only crudely tracks clay at 0.2-0.6 g/L. Keep it as a "floc present/absent" logger, not a metric. Recovery fraction by dry mass is fine on the school 0.001 g balance (18 g scale).

**3. Field concept ignores the tide.** Milford Harbor mean range is ~2.0-2.4 m (NOAA 8466442 via tides4fishing), comparable to the 2 m slip depth. A "full-depth curtain, skirt to 2 m" grounds at low water and is a metre short at high; cell volume, dose and pump time swing ~2x over a cycle, so the 0.4 t / 5 t wet / 12.5 h arithmetic has no fixed volume. Also slurry logistics: 400 kg clay at 40 g/L is 10 m3 = ten IBC fills, 8.3 h at 20 L/min. Fix: define the cell at a stated tide stage with a floating (not fixed) curtain of skirt >= max depth; use 100-200 g/L slurry (2-4 IBC); size sludge storage to 5 totes, not one.

### Major

**4. Retrieval choice is justified, but the "1,500 Oe strips magnetite" frame is wrong.** 1,500 Oe = 0.15 T. PubMed 26609948 (Chlamydomonas, bare Fe3O4 nanoparticles) achieved detachment deliberately, at pH 7 with solution density and viscosity raised, as a nanoparticle-recovery step; it is not a threshold above which a wall field strips flocs. In v1 the magnetite is co-precipitated on kaolinite, so the whole clay particle is magnetic; the only stripping risk is hydrodynamic shear, and laminar wall shear at 5 cm/s in a 40 mm pipe is ~0.01 Pa, an order below typical floc break-up. Drop weakness 3, keep the Chl-in-pad vs Chl-remaining measurement as the honest test, and add a $3 linear Hall sensor (e.g. SS49E, range ~0.1 T, usable beyond ~10 mm; DRV5055 to 0.2 T) on the ESP32 to map the field and check the formula; a "magnet on the balance" pull test gives quantitative sample magnetism without a gaussmeter.

**5. Chemistry: ammonia volume and batch count.** Raising 1 L containing 0.31 mol OH- demand (19.6 g FeCl3.6H2O + 9.0 g FeCl2.4H2O) to pH 10.5 in an NH3/NH4+ buffer (pKa 9.25) needs ~5-6 mol NH3: ~1-2 L of 5-10% household ammonia (household ammonia is 2-10%, often with surfactant; Sciencing/Hunker), doubling or tripling the reaction volume in the hood. Fix: 28% ACS NH4OH (~140 mL to pH 10, ~400 mL to 10.5) or, simpler, NaOH (~16 g, 40 mL of 10 M) to pH 11 with the pH pen; NaOH co-precipitation is standard. The Fe3+:Fe2+ ratio 1.6:1 is Fe2+-rich; oxidation on 65 C air drying gives maghemite (74-84 emu/g vs 92) which is still fine. Batch count: jar tests (~15 g) + 4 tank runs (72 g) + blanks need ~100 g product = 5-6 of the 18 g batches, not one; make a single 50 g-clay batch in a 5 L bucket, and order 2 x 100 g FeCl3.

**6. PAC on magnetite: probably survives, but test it on day one and buy the fallback.** 10% PAC is pH 2.2-2.8, 1% is pH 3.5-5 (PWTAG/Elchemy); Fe3O4 is attacked mainly by chelating acids (citric, acetic) and slowly by dilute mineral acid (Colloids Surf. A 2014). At a 5:1 clay:PAC paste the pH will be ~3-4 for 24 h; expect minor Fe loss, not demagnetisation. Fixes: (a) pilot at 1 g scale in week 1 with the balance pull test before and after; (b) make the PAC-clay in seawater (buffers) and stop ageing at pH >= 5; (c) buy 500 g of synthetic magnetite powder (~$15-25) in the first order as Plan B, either blended with plain PAC-kaolinite or co-dosed (Hu 2013, 120 mg/L naked Fe3O4 on a marine alga [RET s1]). v1 has no fallback and a failed batch costs three weeks, not one.

**7. Mixing: the numbers exist, use them.** G = sqrt(P/(mu V)). A 15 cm paddle at 30 rpm (tip 0.24 m/s, drag form P = C_D A rho v^3/2) gives ~0.02-0.04 W in 30 L, G ~ 25-35 s-1, Gt over 15 min ~2-3e4, inside the 20-70 s-1 and 1e4-1e5 design window (CE370 notes; Sci. Rep. 2022). So 30 rpm is defensible; state it. But "rapid mix at pump-jet velocity" is not: a 60 mL/min peristaltic jet gives G < 10 s-1 in 30 L, and dosing takes 7.5 min, contradicting a 60 s rapid mix. Rapid mix needs ~3 W (G ~ 300 s-1); fix by injecting the slurry into the suction side of the 4 L/min recirculation loop (high local shear in the pump) or a drill-driven stirrer for 60 s after dosing.

**8. No control arms.** One tank, n = 4, no simultaneous untreated control: Skeletonema chains sink ~0.5-1 m/d, so 5-24 h "settling" removals of 10-30% will appear in the control too. Also no plain PAC-kaolinite arm, which is the comparison the whole premise (retrieval vs deposition at equal removal) rests on. Fix: second 10 gal tank ($25) or paired 2 L jars; arms = untreated, PAC-kaolinite, magnetic PAC-kaolinite.

**9. Controller and ledger contract.** Confirmed against `LEDGER_COLS` in the fork's `src/deploy/prospective_forecast.py` and PROSPECTIVE_PROTOCOL.md: `alert = bloom_prob >= pack["threshold"]` (0.50), status values ok/stale/warmup/feed_down, `days_old > 3` -> stale (so v1's rule is redundant but harmless). Gaps: (a) keys are (`issue_date`, `site_id`, `station`) and only the highest `revision` counts; v1 keys on station alone and ignores `revision` and `site_id`. (b) The forecast is P(exceeding the station's own p75 within `horizon_days` = 7), and the protocol's primary stratum is `onset_row = True` (chl today <= p75); a "pre-emptive" device must gate on `onset_row` too, otherwise it doses reactively on an already-elevated day. (c) Issuance is weekly; "no successful poll in 24 h -> hold" must mean endpoint unreachable, not "no new row". (d) The probability-to-dose map conflates probability with expected biomass; dose should be the jar-test D90 for the density implied by `chl_today`, with probability only as go/no-go. (e) Add a re-dose lockout of one horizon (7 d) plus a post-dose verification.

**10. BOM is ~$150-250 light and missing items.** Verified: clear 1.5 in Sch 40 is $5.6-10.6/ft (Spears list, RHFS) so pipe + clear fittings + valve is $40-60, not $12 (moot if finding 1 is adopted). GF/F 25 mm 100/pk ~$100 (Lab Unlimited); Swinnex holders ~$34 each; use 47 mm GF/F on the school's Buchner or a $10 plastic holder. DO pens: Amazon budget units are ~$60-100, mainstream $350-800; $45 is optimistic. LaMotte Al kit 3569-01 is 0-0.5 ppm ECR method, ~$70-90; seawater samples will need dilution and it is prone to interference. Missing: f/2 medium, 20 L carboy and grow light (~$60-90), Sedgewick-Rafter, acetone, a second tank, ACS ammonia/NaOH, magnet holders. Magnets at ~$3-6 each in packs are fine. Corrected total is ~$650-700 unless the pipe and the DO pen are cut (DO can be borrowed; the Chl extraction replaces the DO pen's role in the removal metric).

### Minor

**11. Timeline.** NCMA states "at least two weeks" from order for standard strains, overnight only, no Friday shipping; order Sep 21 -> earliest arrival ~Oct 6, not Sep 28. Non-profit price $210. The 14 C worry is overstated: S. marinoi grows fine at 18-20 C; culture at room temperature under a cool LED and drop the wine cooler. Scale-up 250 mL -> 20 L is ~3 weeks, consistent with 1e5 tank runs from mid-November.

**12. Dose accounting.** "0.6 g/L (18 g)" counts magnetic kaolinite only; with 3.6 g PAC the product dose is 0.72 g/L. State which mass is the dose.

**13. Skeletonema mass.** 50 pg dry/cell is reasonable (Menden-Deuer-Lessard diatom C for 100-300 um3 cells is ~12-30 pg C, dry ~2-3x), and Chl 0.4 pg/cell means 1e4 cells/mL = 4 ug/L, below the 20 ug/L "bloom" used in the field balance; the balance itself (0.4-1.2 t clay for ~0.5 kg N) is arithmetically right.

**14. Safety gaps.** N52 blocks pinch, shatter and will snap together in the alternating array (use a printed holder with spacers); keep the SD card and phones away. Tank effluent contains dissolved Al from PAC; check the school's drain policy (bleach then dilute). ISEF: hazardous chemicals (FeCl3, NH3/NaOH, PAC) and a home-built electrical device need Form 3; protists are exempt from PHBA rules but confirm with the SRC.

**15. Known-weaknesses list misses** findings 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 8 and 9 above, i.e. the two that could zero the project (capture capacity; unmeasurable removal at 1e4) and the control-arm gap.

## B. What v1 gets right

The DAF rejection for 20-30 ppt tidal water is correctly sourced (IRL 21% Chl-a, 0% TN) and the non-deposition premise is the right differentiator. Choosing CCMP1332 first (LIS isolate, non-toxic, floc-friendly diatom) and deferring Prorocentrum is sound. The seawater-made-up 3x dose warning and the 1 g/L series cap are correct. Reading the real ledger columns and status codes, holding on non-ok status, and logging every poll is the right controller skeleton. The hybrid fallback (settling removal as the guaranteed metric) and the honest nutrient-credit disclaimer in the field section are good science-fair hygiene. The wall field vs. bore falloff was at least flagged as a gaussmeter gap.

## C. Three highest-value changes for v2

1. Replace the 1.5 in pipe with a thin-sheet magnetic tray or in-tank magnet-rods-in-sleeves so every floc passes within 5 mm of a magnet face and the capture area is ~10x, with a Hall sensor to map the field.
2. Replace in-vivo 665/750 and the hemocytometer with acetone-extracted Chl-a on GF/F (250-500 mL) and a Sedgewick-Rafter count, and add untreated and plain PAC-kaolinite control arms.
3. De-risk chemistry in week one: NaOH or 28% NH4OH instead of household ammonia, one 50 g batch, a 1 g PAC-compatibility pilot with the balance pull test, and 500 g of magnetite powder ordered as Plan B; gate the controller on `onset_row` and key doses on (`issue_date`, `site_id`, `station`, max `revision`).
