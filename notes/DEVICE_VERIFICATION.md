# Scientist verification of design v4 (magnetic PAC-kaolin flocculation with in-tank magnetic retrieval)

Lens: does the process work as claimed, and can it run in real water. Verdicts: WORKS (cited evidence at comparable conditions), PLAUSIBLE (mechanism sound, no direct evidence at these conditions), UNSUPPORTED (evidence against, or none). Independent checks this pass (19 web calls, three primary PDFs read in full): the WHOI/EPA ECOHAB final report; Yu et al. 2016 on PAC-clay in seawater; the CoMag magnetite-ballast process; the Bayferrox 318 M data sheet; Golding 2015 marine Al guideline; EPA 2018 Al criteria; Beaulieu 2005 floc resuspension; Vaudrey 2016 embayment N loads; the CT DEEP coastal permit overview; Hu 2013, Cerff 2012, and Fe3O4@SiO2@PAC magnetic seeds. My calculations are marked [calc]; unverifiable items [unverified].

## 1. Flocculation at low density in seawater: PLAUSIBLE, leaning WORKS; one recipe detail works against it

**Encounter rate is not limiting at G ~30 s-1 [calc].** At 0.2 g/L, ~2 um EPK kaolin is ~1.8e7 particles/mL, ~1,800 per cell at 1e4 cells/mL. Orthokinetic collisions of an 8 um cell with clay run at (G/6)(d1+d2)^3 n ~ 0.09 s-1, ~80 per cell over the 15 min slow mix; perikinetic is ~3e-4 s-1, 300x lower, so stating G was right. Clay-clay collisions scale with n^2, so the clay self-flocculates within minutes and cells are mostly taken by sweep: ~3e9 flocs/m3 of 50-100 um settling at 0.3-1 mm/s sweep 5-6 tank volumes in 15 min, giving 70-95% removal at collision efficiencies of 0.2-0.5. This is the "sweep floc" model the EPA/WHOI report adopts.

**Cell density.** The EPA/WHOI report: for K. brevis "eighty percent or higher cell removal was not seen until cell concentration exceeded approximately 1,000 cells/mL", the same rising trend for Heterosigma, A. tamarense and A. sanguinea, and RE correlated with predicted clay-cell collision frequency. 1e4 is 10x above that threshold. In the Corpus Christi bloom mesocosms (530 L), 4 ppm PAC + 0.10 g/L clay gave RE >81%, and "K. brevis, Bacillaria sp., and Skeletonema sp. were removed at about the same level": the only Skeletonema-in-seawater number found, at bloom density. **Chains:** Yu 2017 and the EPA report both say chain diatoms floc readily; no study isolates chain length, so logging it (v4) is the right hedge.

**The seawater penalty is a stock-preparation penalty, and v4 prepares the stock in seawater.** Yu et al. 2016 (PubMed 26763322): PAC-modified clay suspended in seawater dropped to +5.9 mV, aggregated clay-to-clay in the stock, and "larger flocs were observed when PAC-MC suspension prepared by deionized water was added to the algae culture than PAC-MC suspension prepared by seawater"; this is the 0.6 vs 0.2 g/L factor in CLAY s2. The EPA report saw the extreme: PAC-pretreated clay "immediately formed large, baseball-sized aggregates, which immediately sank to the bottom, leaving behind most of the cells in suspension"; diluting PAC into the seawater first and adding clay 20 min later gave a 2-5x RE gain. v4 pastes kaolin, PAC and magnetite in seawater, ages 24 h and holds a 100 g/L seawater stock: the condition both sources say makes small flocs. IOCAS modifies in fresh water, dries, and disperses in seawater only at dosing. R1 chose seawater ageing to buffer acid attack on magnetite; the trade was never examined. **Minimum evidence:** week-1 pilot on one 1e4 culture, three stocks (DI-aged then seawater-dispersed at dosing; seawater-aged as v4; PAC pre-diluted 20 min before plain clay), n = 2, 5 h counts: one afternoon, six jars. If DI-aged wins by >15 points, change the recipe before Nov 6.

**PAC at pH 8.** 0.2 g/L blend is 33 mg/L PAC powder = 5.1 mg/L Al [calc], consuming ~0.3-0.5 of seawater's ~2.3 meq/L alkalinity; pH falls a few tenths, not a process issue. At pH 8 Al precipitates as Al(OH)3 (sweep) with aluminate as the dissolved residual (Golding 2015): an environmental issue (section 3), not a flocculation one. Magnetite at 37 wt% of the blend and its effect on floc formation: [unverified anywhere].

## 2. Magnetic capture in seawater: PLAUSIBLE; strong industrial precedent for the floc, none for the raster

**Kaolin and cells riding with magnetite.** The commercial CoMag process (Evoqua/Xylem) adds finely ground magnetite to metal-hydroxide coagulation; magnetite is "infused into the metal hydroxide floc", flocs settle ~10x faster, and drum magnets recover ">99% of the magnetite" (after a deliberate shear-detachment step, in freshwater wastewater). Seawater analogues: Fe3O4@SiO2@PAC magnetic seeds on ballast-water sediments (IWA WST 2024), where PAC-coated seeds raised algae removal ~20 points; Cerff 2012 (silica-coated magnetic particles, >95% separation of marine P. tricornutum and N. salina by HGMS, 30-77 g algae/g); Hu 2013 (naked Fe3O4 at 120 mg/L, >95% of N. maritima in 4 min, aggregation at alkaline pH helped). Suga 1982's >=10 g Fe/L was direct adsorption without coagulant, not comparable. In seawater (I ~0.7 M) 0.2 um magnetite coagulates with a perikinetic half-life of ~40 s [calc], so during the 60 s drill mix it hetero-coagulates into whatever is present; segregation is unlikely, and the detachment test is the correct gate. WORKS in freshwater; PLAUSIBLE here.

**Pigment properties.** Bayferrox 318 M TDS: Fe3O4 >96.8%, spherical, ~0.2 um, density ~4.6, pH 4-8, not classified hazardous, food-contact listed. No magnetic data on the TDS; patents for the pigment class give Ms 50-91 emu/g and coercivity 20-330 Oe. Ms of the grade actually bought: [unverified]. It does not affect the headline (recovery is magnetite-equivalent against the same powder) but the remanence means the stock self-agglomerates into multi-micron clusters before dosing, which helps capture.

**Free fines are not captured by a moving raster [calc].** A free 0.2 um particle in 25 T/m moves at ~0.02 mm/s (0.5 um: ~0.14 mm/s), minutes to cross 5 mm against ~4 s of dwell under a 4 cm face at 1 cm/s. Only floc-bound or agglomerated magnetite is swept; so magnetite-equivalent recovery measures floc incorporation, which is what you want, and column passes cannot rescue a shortfall.

**>70% from a 16 cm floor.** R2's force budget rechecks [calc]. Gaps: 13 mm lanes between 20 mm faces on 33 mm centres are covered only by the 3 cm overlap; each release cycle strands pad on the tube; 3 mm skids plus a 3.2 mm wall put settled floc 6-8 mm from the pole, the outer edge of the 8-14 mm holding shell; and the raster resuspends the floor (section 3). No data exist for any hand raster; the clay-only blank is the right and only gate. Add a torch photo of the floor after the last pass and a pull test of the tank rinse, so a miss is attributed (left on floor, stranded on tube, or never in floc).

**Tidal-exchange failure mode.** Beaulieu et al. 2005: a 2 mm PAC-clay layer settled 3 h resuspends at ~0.06-0.07 Pa, ~0.09 Pa after 9-24 h; the EPA report adds PAC makes the layer "more fluffy and thus easier to resuspend". A marina ebb of 10-20 cm/s gives ~0.03-0.12 Pa [calc, Cd 0.003], so a fresh layer under a skirt hung 0.3-0.6 m above the bed leaves on the first flood; magnetite ballast should raise the threshold (CoMag settling) but nobody has measured it. Two modes the reviewers missed: uncaptured 0.2 um pigment disperses as a colloid beyond any transect; and LIS bed sediment is glacially sourced with detrital magnetite (heavy-mineral analyses of regional sediment remove magnetite with a magnet; USGS reports susceptibility anomalies from heavy-mineral layers in Chesapeake cores), so the bed sled collects natural magnetic sand and needs a pre-dose baseline on every transect plus a way to tell pigment from grains. [Natural magnetite abundance at the site: unverified.]

## 3. Environmental effects of the blend vs sunk clay: benefit UNSUPPORTED; Al addition is real

**Burial and bottom oxygen.** There is something to remove: the EPA limnocorrals found all brevetoxin in the treated pens in the trapped floc and still there at 96 h; clay raised settled solids 1,426%, volatile solids 527%, BOD rate 31%, with a DO drop on day 2. But no study compares sunk against retrieved floc for DO, sediment nutrients or fauna (RET s5; confirmed none this pass). The counter-evidence is the EPA clam work: fully sedimented clay gave "no significant differences in shell or tissue growth" for juvenile Mercenaria, while clay kept suspended at 0.25 g/L for 2 weeks cut growth ~90%. The documented harm is resuspension, not burial; a raster that scrapes the bed is a resuspension event, and incomplete capture converts a benign layer into suspended fines. "Retrieved is better for benthos": UNSUPPORTED. Minimum evidence: DO and turbidity 2 cm above the tank floor before, during and 1 h after the raster (the diagram already has a DO probe at K), and the tank's T51 count set against the jar count to quantify resuspension.

**Dissolved Al.** Total Al added is 5.1 mg/L (15 mg/L at 3x) [calc]. After PACl coagulation the dissolved residual "increases greatly as pH increases to 8" (PubMed 23422138); tens to a few hundred ug/L is typical, <20 ug/L only with tailored high-basicity PACl. EPA has no estuarine or marine Al criterion (2018: "not enough data"). Golding et al. 2015: marine guideline 24 ug total Al/L; diatom IC10 18 ug/L, mussel embryo EC10 250, oyster embryo EC10 410; toxic forms are aluminate and Al(OH)3(0). Golding 2018 extends this to 18 species as "very high reliability" [values unverified; fetch timed out]. Pacific oyster D-larvae show abnormal development from 150 ug Al/L (ESPR 2023). So treated water is likely above the 95%-protection guideline for hours and within ~2x of bivalve-larva thresholds; Mote's mesocosm clams took up 5x Al without mortality (RET s5). Retrieval does not change dissolved Al, which is set by PAC dose and pH; it removes particulate Al(OH)3 that could re-dissolve slowly. The LaMotte ECR kit cannot resolve this; a partner-lab ICP Al on one 5 h supernatant per arm is the single most valuable environmental number the MVP can add.

**Iron oxide and bycatch.** 54 mg/L particulate Fe per treatment [calc]; the pigment is inert and non-hazardous (TDS); the concern is turbidity from uncaptured fines. Copepods and bivalve larvae are floc-sized and will be enmeshed whether the floc sinks or is captured; no study counts either [unverified, not measurable in the MVP].

## 5. Energy, time, labour vs the forecast lead and settling time [calc]

Bench: <0.05 kWh per run (drill 8 Wh, paddle ~0.5 Wh, ESP32 ~24 Wh/day); hands-on ~1 h for T0-T51, ~1.5 h pad workup, three 35 min counts, a 24 h count and a 7 d regrowth read: 4-5 h per tank run plus revisits. Consistent with the calendar.

Field concept: 600 m3 at 160 m3/h needs ~10-15 kW for 3.75 h (40-55 kWh); a low-intensity drum at 0.05-0.1 kWh/m3 adds 30-60 kWh; ~100 kWh per treatment, dwarfed by 2-3 people for 6-8 h, 0.8-2.4 m3 of 150 g/L slurry (13-40 L/min in the hour before slack) and 1.5-4.5 t of wet sludge. The sequence is not physically consistent as written: flocs reach a 2 m bed in 0.5-2 h, so with dosing 1 h before low slack the layer forms as the flood starts, and the bed vacuum then runs 1.6-3.75 h under 10-20 cm/s at or above the Beaulieu threshold, inside a skirt deliberately open at the bed. The 7-day lead is not the problem (it lets you pick the slack); the 1-2 h slack against a 2-4 h capture is. Consistent versions: (a) a closed-bottom mesocosm (bag or cod-end limnocorral; the EPA Sarasota pens were 2 m diameter, 3 m deep), or (b) capture during the flocculation window, pumping the cell through the drum while flocs are suspended, which is the one operational advantage magnetic capture has over settling and which the settle-then-raster sequence does not exploit. Add "sequence not yet consistent with tidal timing" to weakness 8.

## 6. Regulatory: removal is not treated differently from deposition; a student gets a mesocosm authorisation only through an institutional permittee

The CT DEEP coastal permit overview (CGS 22a-359 to 363h) lists as regulated "discharge of dredged material or ... the placement of rock, soil or other fill material in tidal waters" and maintenance dredging; tracks are individual permit, Certificate of Permission (45 d, 90 d with more information) and General Permits (~90 d); it contains no temporary, scientific, research or educational exemption. Clay is "soil or other fill material"; a sled or vacuum removing bed material is dredging; both are regulated and nothing distinguishes them (consistent with RET s8). A clay-PAC-magnetite slurry released to tidal water is a discharge under CGS 22a-430; the general permits found cover stormwater, dewatering and industrial categories, none research releases, so an open-cell release needs an individual discharge permit plus the coastal permit, CWA 401, public notice and a USACE authorisation under the CT General Permit [GP category for the discharge itself: unverified]. Florida's precedents needed an NPDES-type permit and a permitted area; NOAA's own clay demonstration runs under a NEPA Programmatic EA. Realistic student path: a closed 1-10 m3 mesocosm hung from an existing dock with no discharge (contents to the school drain per the Al policy) is a temporary structure: COP or GP with USACE GP self-verification, ~45-90 days, with the DEEP/UConn partner as applicant. The curtain cell, Rhodamine and bed sled are 6-18 months and agency-led.

## 7. Forecast-to-dose logic: sound as a demonstration trigger, UNSUPPORTED as mitigation

`alert` is P(station chl exceeds its own p75 within 7 d) >= 0.50 and `onset_row` restricts to days with chl <= p75. A p75 exceedance is by construction a top-quartile day, not a harmful bloom; the trigger fires on the routine seasonal rise. That is fine for a demonstration and honest as a pre-emption test only in an enclosed volume; in a flushed slip v4 concedes it cannot work (s4), and no deployment has dosed pre-emptively on a forecast (RET s9).

**Wasted doses per station-season [calc from the AER duty cycle and the CLAUDE.md table].** ~17 weekly summer rows. At 0.60: 7% flagged, recall 0.48, precision 0.50 (~7% summer base rate): 1.2 alerts, ~0.6 wasted. At 0.50: 15% flagged, recall 0.60, precision ~0.29: 2.6 alerts, ~1.8 wasted. Per station at 0.60: A4 flags 57% of visits at precision 0.625, ~10 alerts, ~4 wasted (the issue_date lockout halves doses, not the ratio); B3 48% at 0.556, ~8 alerts, ~3.6 wasted; C1 10% at 1.0 on 7 positives, ~1.7 alerts, few wasted but n is tiny. At IEC (0.24 vs 0.13) three of four doses are wasted. One wasted field dose is 120-360 kg product, ~100 kWh, a working day and 3-9 kg Al plus 32-96 kg Fe in the water; at the bench, 4 g. So the gate is defensible for January and for a mesocosm; as an open-water trigger the false-alarm Al load roughly equals the true-alarm load, and true alarms are not shown to prevent anything. Keep VERIFY (24 h count < 0.3 C0, hard stop after one retry); do not describe the gate as "predicting a bloom".

**Three biggest risks to the January result.**
1. Seawater-aged stock under-flocculates at 0.2 g/L (Yu 2016; EPA "baseball aggregates"); H11a lands at 40-60% and the 0.6 g/L rerun costs a batch week. Mitigation is one week-1 afternoon and a recipe change before Nov 6.
2. Recovery is measured after a raster that resuspends its own floor, and pad sheds on every release: total-solids recovery can miss 70% while magnetite-equivalent passes, and the board shows two numbers that disagree for no stated reason. Attribute every miss and report resuspension.
3. Measurement floor: 0.2-3 gf pull tests on a ~2 mg-drift balance and counts capped at 200 cells give CIs wide enough that "lower bound > 50%" at n = 3 fails on noise if true RE is ~70%. Pre-register that mean >= 70% with a lower bound of 40% reads "consistent, underpowered", not failed.

**Section 9 board sentences if H11a-b pass.** Sentence 1: mostly, with one fix: the jars were not dosed "when a test forecast row alerted", only the tank runs were; split removal (jars) from trigger (tank). Sentence 2: supportable on recovery; qualify "no published study had measured the recovered fraction in seawater" to "no HAB clay study", since Cerff 2012 and Hu 2013 measured magnetic separation of marine algae in culture and CoMag reports magnetite recovery in freshwater. Sentence 3: supportable as written. Add to the reject list: "predicts blooms" (it predicts a p75 exceedance) and "removes aluminium" (it adds 5 mg/L).

## Verdict

The process is chemically and physically sound at bench scale: PAC-kaolin flocculation of a chain diatom at 1e4 cells/mL in seawater has direct analogues above 80% removal, and magnetite enmeshed in a hydroxide floc and recovered by a magnet is an industrial process with >99% ballast recovery in freshwater and beaker-scale marine analogues. What has never been measured is the combination in seawater with a raster, which is exactly what the MVP measures, so the January headline is a genuine result if the gates hold. Two things should change before freezing: the stock should not be aged and stored in seawater without a week-1 comparison against DI-aged and PAC-first preparation, because the two best sources on seawater PAC-clay say that preparation costs floc size and dose; and the tank protocol should log floor DO/turbidity and attribute recovery misses so raster resuspension is a measured quantity, not a confound. Everything past the tank is not ready, and v4 says so: the field sequence is inconsistent with tidal timing, the benthic benefit has no supporting comparison, dissolved Al from PAC is likely above the marine guideline for hours regardless of retrieval, the forecast trigger wastes about as many doses as it justifies at the test precision, and a student cannot hold the permits for an open-water release. The board sentences are supportable with the two qualifications above.
