# Evidence review: live seaweed panels (sugar kelp first, *Ulva* second)

Written 2026-09-25. Companion to `notes/mitigation/02_SEAWEED.md` (the method file) and
`00_CONTROL_LOOP.md` (the shared loop). Every source below was retrieved by DOI (OpenAlex,
Europe PMC, Crossref, Springer or the publisher/repository PDF).

**How each source was read** (the "Read" column in the master table):
- **Full text:** read in full.
- **Full text (02_SEAWEED.md):** the three Sylvers & Gobler papers. They were read in full earlier today; the numbers come from that note.
- **Abstract:** only the abstract was available.
- **Table in #15:** the numbers come from Table 1 of Ben Gharbia et al. 2017 (#15), which summarises the earlier *Ulva* studies. The original paper is paywalled here and was not read.
- **TLDR:** only the Semantic Scholar one-line summary was available.

Numbers taken from an abstract are marked "(abstract)". FW = fresh (wet) weight; DW = dry weight.

## 1. Summary and verdict

This review covers 20 sources:
- 17 experimental studies of seaweed against bloom-forming microalgae (#1-#17). Most are lab or bottle studies; 3 reach mesocosm or field scale (#1, #2, #13).
- 2 field or lab studies of kelp's environmental effects: pH (#18) and nitrogen removal (#19).
- 1 methods review (#20).

The evidence is strong that live *Ulva* and sugar kelp reduce many harmful algae in closed vessels:
- **Size of effect:** typically 40-95% fewer cells than controls.
- **Density:** 0.5-3 g/L FW.
- **Time:** effects usually become large only after **48-72 h**. At low density (0.2 g/L) or with tolerant species, they take 5-20 days.

Allelopathy is the main explanation in almost every paper:
- The effect persists when nutrients are replete and pH is held at 8.
- Culture filtrates are usually weak, which points to short-lived compounds that the living tissue must keep releasing.

The evidence is **weak or missing** for:
- open-water effects beyond a few metres of a farm (only one field survey, for *Gracilaria* in China);
- our exact stand-in organisms: there are no *Phaeodactylum* or *Thalassiosira* data. The closest diatoms are *Pseudo-nitzschia*, *Nitzschia closterium* and *Skeletonema*;
- what happens after the seaweed is removed (rebound).

**Verdict:** moderate-to-strong lab evidence, weak field evidence. The method is well suited to a
bench test of "does a retrievable panel suppress a culture within one X = 72 h cycle". The
evidence does **not** support claims of bloom prevention or field readiness.

## 3. Per-source detail

### 1. Sylvers & Gobler 2021: kelp, *Ulva*, Irish moss vs *Alexandrium catenella*
- **Hypothesis:** cultivable seaweeds at aquaculture densities reduce *A. catenella* and toxin uptake by mussels.
- **Method:** 250 mL co-cultures in f/2 at 15-21 °C, 100 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹, 16:8; start 2-10 × 10² cells/mL; seaweed 0.7-3.8 g/L FW. Field bloom bottles at 3-6 g/L. 300 L mesocosms at 1 g/L. Mussel STX test.
- **Results:** −17-74% at 2-3 d and −42-96% at about 1 week. Bottles: up to −95%. Mesocosms: kelp −73% in 48 h, *Ulva* −54% in 96 h. Mussel STX 71.8 vs 93.5 µg/100 g (closure limit 80). Ranking: kelp > *Ulva* > *Chondrus*.
- **Explanation:** mainly allelopathy (cell lysis). Nutrient competition, higher pH and seaweed-attached bacteria contributed in some runs.
- **Environment:** in the mesocosms, **total chlorophyll did not fall**, because other algae replaced *A. catenella*. *Ulva* pushed closed vessels to pH 9.0-9.5 (sometimes above 10); kelp stayed at 8.1-8.9 (from 02_SEAWEED.md).
- **What we take:** densities of 1 and 2 g/L, the X = 72 h window, and kelp over *Ulva* for pH. The OFF rule needs **cell counts**, not only chlorophyll.

### 2. Sylvers & Gobler 2023: seaweeds vs *Margalefidinium* and larval fish
- **Hypothesis:** seaweeds reduce the toxicity of *M. polykrikoides* to fish.
- **Method:** fish survival in well plates with cultured and wild bloom water. Partition chambers. DMSP added alone. 300 L mesocosm with 0.17 g/L *Ulva* on ropes for 7 d.
- **Results:** survival extended in all cases, sometimes to control levels. DMSP alone reduced toxicity (p < 0.005). Protection needed close contact. The mesocosm showed no cell reduction until day 5, then −68%, −90% and −92% on days 5-7. Live seaweed beat frozen.
- **Explanation:** an antioxidant microlayer at the thallus surface neutralises the short-lived reactive oxygen species (ROS) that *Margalefidinium* releases. This is separate from killing cells.
- **What we take:** at low density, **MAX_ON has to be ≥ 7 d**; use live seaweed, not frozen. The protective effect is local, so the panel needs good water contact (mixing).

### 3. Sylvers & Gobler 2025: kelp and *Ulva* vs the diatom *Pseudo-nitzschia*
- **Hypothesis:** cultivable seaweeds inhibit toxic *Pseudo-nitzschia* and domoic acid uptake by shellfish.
- **Method:** 250 mL flasks, f/4, 15 °C, ~100 µmol photons, 16:8; start 1-4 × 10³ cells/mL; seaweed 0.5-3 g/L FW, salad-spun before weighing; counts every 24-48 h. Field bloom bottles. Mussel and razor-clam toxin test.
- **Results:** −13-47% at 24-48 h; −74-94% at ≥72 h. The controls entered exponential growth at about 72 h, and the seaweed flasks never did. Kelp at 0.5 g/L worked on *P. multiseries* by day 7 (−83%); *P. australis* needed 2 g/L. Bloom water: 2 g/L kelp gave −69 to −81%, and 1 g/L was not significant. *Gracilaria* had no effect.
- **Explanation:** allelopathy. The diatoms lost photosynthetic efficiency and turned pale. The effect persisted in nutrient-rich water.
- **What we take:** the **closest analogue to our diatom bench**: kelp at 2 g/L, X = 72 h, and 0.5 / 1 / 2 g/L dose arms.

### 4. Tang & Gobler 2011: *Ulva lactuca* vs seven HAB species
- **Hypothesis:** *U. lactuca* from Long Island inhibits common HAB species through allelopathy.
- **Method:** fresh thalli; dried-powder aqueous extracts, heated and unheated; Long Island brown-tide field bottles. Per Semmouri (#20), the authors measured PAR (to rule out shading) and used DAPI checks for bacteria.
- **Results (table in #15):** fresh thalli at up to 725 mg/L DW (the table's units) gave −24% (*Prorocentrum minimum*), −38% (*Karlodinium veneficum*) and −50% (*Karenia brevis*) at 72 h, and −29% for *Cochlodinium* at 120 h (400 mg/L DW). Extracts lysed *Karlodinium* and *K. brevis* after 5 d.
- **Explanation:** heat-stable allelochemicals (TLDR).
- **What we take:** *Ulva* works on several dinoflagellates, but the effect at 72 h is moderate (24-50%). This caveat matters for the dinoflagellate series. Doses may have been reported in DW; check the original before converting.

### 5. Jin & Dong 2003: two *Ulva pertusa* strains vs *Heterosigma* and *Alexandrium*
- **Hypothesis:** fresh tissue and dry powder of *U. pertusa* inhibit two HAB species, and strains differ.
- **Method:** long-term coexistence cultures, 0.625-10 g/L FW for 10 d; dry powder 0.15-2.4 g/L DW; filtrate made from 100 g/L FW.
- **Results (table in #15):** EC50 against *A. tamarense* 2-2.5 g/L FW; powder EC50 0.6-0.8 g/L DW; filtrate had a slight effect that was not significant.
- **Explanation:** continuous release of small amounts of **rapidly degradable** allelochemicals from fresh tissue (TLDR).
- **What we take:** a dinoflagellate EC50 of about **2 g/L FW over 10 d**. Filtrate alone is a weak treatment, so the panel must stay in the water.

### 6. Nan, Zhang & Zhao 2004: *U. pertusa* vs eight microalgae
- **Hypothesis:** *U. pertusa* releases allelochemicals that affect a range of microalgae.
- **Method:** coexistence and filtrate cultures with *Heterosigma*, *Skeletonema costatum*, *Tetraselmis*, *Nitzschia closterium*, *Chaetoceros gracile*, *Chroomonas*, *Isochrysis* and *A. tamarense*.
- **Results:** 12.5 g/L FW cut *A. tamarense* about 70% in ~11 d (table in #15). Filtrate (80 g/L) inhibited only *Chroomonas* (TLDR). The per-species diatom numbers were not retrieved.
- **What we take:** diatoms were in the test set, but we could not extract their numbers; the full text is needed. Filtrate is again weak.

### 7. Jin, Dong & Wang 2005: *U. pertusa* and *U. linza* vs *Prorocentrum micans*
- **Hypothesis:** *Ulva* inhibits *P. micans*, which is our candidate non-toxic dinoflagellate.
- **Method:** fresh tissue 0.625-10 g/L FW and dry powder 0.15-2.4 g/L DW for 10 d. Aqueous extract and four organic-solvent extracts. Filtrate added once or semi-continuously.
- **Results:** fresh tissue and powder strongly inhibited growth: EC50 1.8 (*U. pertusa*) and 2.3 (*U. linza*) g/L FW; powder 0.7-0.8 g/L DW (table in #15). Aqueous and methanol extracts were active; acetone, ether and chloroform extracts were not, so the compounds are **polar**. A single filtrate dose had no effect; semi-continuous addition did. *P. micans* did not affect the seaweed (abstract).
- **What we take:** the **direct number for our dinoflagellate: EC50 ~2 g/L FW over 10 d**. At 1 g/L over 72 h, expect well under 50%. This supports a longer X, or 2 g/L, for the dinoflagellate series.

### 8. Wang et al. 2007: *U. pertusa* and *G. lemaneiformis* vs four dinoflagellates
- **Method:** fresh thalli and filtrate against *P. donghaiense*, *A. tamarense*, *Amphidinium carterae* and *Scrippsiella*.
- **Results (table in #15):** at 3.4 g/L FW, *U. pertusa* gave LT50s of 38 h, 60 h and 64 h (*P. donghaiense*, *A. tamarense*, *Scrippsiella*); *Amphidinium* fell only 34%. Filtrate inhibited 5-27%, and *stimulated* *Amphidinium* by 78%.
- **Explanation (TLDR):** allelopathy for *Ulva*; allelopathy plus nutrient competition for *Gracilaria*.
- **What we take:** at about 3 g/L, the effect appears within **1.5-3 days** (supports X = 72 h). Some species resist or are even stimulated, so a second target species matters.

### 9. Nan et al. 2008: *U. lactuca* vs HAB microalgae
- **Results (table in #15):** 0.8 g/L FW gave −48% *A. tamarense* over 12 d. Filtrate inhibited only for the first 2 d, then growth recovered. Powder EC50 0.19 g/L DW (3 d).
- **Explanation (TLDR):** long-term inhibition "might need the continuous addition" of seaweed compounds.
- **What we take:** direct support for **H6 (the effect is reversible)**: once the source is gone, cells recover.

### 10. Alamsjah et al. 2008: algicidal fatty acids from *Ulva*
- **Hypothesis:** specific polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) are the algicidal agents in *Ulva*.
- **Method:** isolated HDTA (C16:4 n-3), ALA (C18:3 n-3) and ODTA (C18:4 n-3). 4 h lethality assays in 24-well plates at 20 °C. Measured release from dry powder (1.5 g/L) and fresh tissue (50 g/L).
- **Results:**
  - Dry powder 4 h LC50 against *Heterosigma*: 0.40 g/L DW (*U. fasciata*), 0.70 (*U. pertusa*).
  - The PUFAs were very active on raphidophytes and *Karenia mikimotoi*, moderately active on *P. minimum* and *Scrippsiella*, and weak on *Alexandrium* and *Cochlodinium*.
  - Powder released 47% of its PUFAs by 6 h, and only 2% of the peak remained at 48 h.
  - Fresh tissue at 50 g/L released only 0.22-0.48 mg/L of PUFAs and killed just 1.6-6.6% of cells in 4 h.
- **Explanation:** PUFAs disrupt cell membranes.
- **What we take:**
  - **Fresh tissue acts slowly**, and the compounds break down within 1-2 days. That is why the panel must stay in and why X is days, not hours.
  - Powder is fast but cannot be retrieved: an "extract" arm is possible, but it is not our method.

### 11. Wang, Zhou & Tang 2009: *U. pertusa* and *G. lemaneiformis* vs *Heterosigma*
- **Results (abstract):** fresh thalli killed *H. akashiwo* completely, with a clear dose-response. In the *Gracilaria* co-culture, nitrate and phosphate were almost exhausted; in the *Ulva* co-culture they stayed well above limiting, yet the cells still died. A daily f/2 top-up relieved the *Gracilaria* inhibition. Filtrate was also inhibitory for this species.
- **What we take:** the **nutrient top-up test** tells nutrient competition apart from allelopathy. Use it as a diagnostic arm.

### 12. Tang, Kang, Berry & Gobler 2015: *Porphyra purpurea* (nori) vs seven HAB species
- **Results (abstract):** fresh thalli at "environmentally realistic levels (mg L⁻¹)" inhibited all seven species, depending on both the seaweed and the microalga dose. Extracts of fresh and dried tissue were as strong or stronger. Brown-tide field bottles showed reductions at high and low nutrients. **High seaweed levels did not harm 1-week-old larval fish.**
- **What we take:** evidence that the seaweed does not harm a non-target animal. It supports H8 with *Artemia* as our stand-in.

### 13. Yang, Liu, Chai & Tang 2015: farmed *G. lemaneiformis* vs bloom phytoplankton (China)
- **Results (abstract):**
  - Fresh thalli inhibited 4 of 6 species with a dose-response, including the **diatom *Skeletonema costatum***, *P. micans* and *Scrippsiella*, plus the natural assemblage.
  - In-situ bottles (1 L) and mesocosms (50 L and 1000 L) inhibited *Phaeocystis globosa* blooms.
  - A large field survey found lower phytoplankton abundance in *Gracilaria*-farming areas.
- **Caveat:** the field result is a correlation (farms sit in chosen places). The authors' claim that farming "prevents HABs" is theirs, not ours.
- **What we take:** the only farm-scale evidence, and it is for a red seaweed, not kelp.

### 14. Accoroni et al. 2015: macroalgae vs *Ostreopsis* cf. *ovata*
- **Method:** 21 °C, 90-100 µmol photons, 12:12, f/4 at salinity 35. Epiphytes and bacteria removed (surfactant wash, chloramphenicol). 1 g FW in 500 mL (2 g/L) for 20 d. Filtrate from 24 g/L FW (1 week). Dry powder 0.4-1.6 g/L DW for 18 d. **pH adjusted to 8 weekly.**
- **Results:** *Ulva* co-culture ended 94% below the inoculum (31.6 vs 735 cells/mL in the control; LT50 12 d). Powder: −17-37% vs inoculum. **Filtrate stimulated growth** (0.38 vs 0.25 d⁻¹ in the control). *Ulva* drew down DIN, but PO₄ did not change. Cells formed cysts.
- **Explanation:** allelopathy, since pH was held at 8 and the filtrate was inactive. The compounds are either released only in the target's presence or degrade quickly.
- **What we take:** evidence that pH can be ruled out. With a benthic, tolerant target the effect takes **weeks**. Cyst formation means a lower cell count may not mean dead cells.

### 15. Ben Gharbia et al. 2017: *Ulva rigida* and seagrasses vs four dinoflagellates
- **Method:** 0.08-1.0 g FW *Ulva* in 200 mL (0.4-5 g/L FW) for 10 d, 25 °C, salinity 36. Blotted dry before weighing. pH, DO and nutrients measured.
- **Results:** *Ulva* was the strongest macrophyte. Growth rates fell 42-62% (*Ostreopsis*), 38-51% (*P. lima*) and 35-47% (*Coolia*). The planktonic *A. pacificum* was killed at ≥0.8 g/L by day 6 (EC50 < 0.4 g/L FW). Fv/Fm fell 13-15%. pH, DO and nutrients were not different from controls.
- **Conversion:** 1 g/L FW *Ulva* = 0.153 g/L DW.
- **What we take:** the FW:DW ratio, and that planktonic species are more sensitive than benthic ones. Table 1 of this paper is also our source for the numbers in #4-#9.

### 16. Benitt, Young, Sylvers & Gobler 2022: red seaweeds vs brown tide
- **Method:** 250 mL, 21 °C, 100 µmol photons, 14:10; 0.25-3 g/L; nutrient ± (50 µM NO₃, 3 µM PO₄); antibiotics ±. A **pH-only control** buffered with 1 mM sodium carbonate to pH 8.00-9.00. Field bottles from Great South Bay.
- **Results:**
  - Dose-dependent reductions in nearly all lab runs.
  - Under low nutrients or low cell density, **≥0.75-1 g/L and ≥72 h** were needed.
  - **pH 9.0 alone reduced *Aureococcus***; pH 8.00-8.50 did not.
  - Field bottles over 24 h: *Dasysiphonia* 3 g/L −30%; *Gracilaria* not significant.
- **Explanation:** allelopathy is primary. Bacteria, nutrients and pH are secondary.
- **What we take:** the **pH-matched control method**, and **pH 9.0 as a confound limit**. Blooms are more sensitive at high cell density and high nutrients.

### 17. Huang et al. 2022: *Gracilaria bailiniae* vs the diatom *Nitzschia closterium*
- **Results (abstract):** fresh thalli IC50 (4 d) = 0.667 × 10⁻³ g/mL (**0.67 g/L**); aqueous extract 3.89 g/L. Candidate compounds: citrate and isethionic acid (IC50 1.04 and 1.15 mM); taurine was weak. Cells shrank, plasmolysed, and photosystem II was damaged.
- **What we take:** a **pennate diatom** (the closest to *Phaeodactylum*) is inhibited by a live seaweed at under 1 g/L within 4 d. It also gives a mechanism (PSII damage), which fits measuring Fv/Fm or chlorophyll per cell.

### 18. Young et al. 2022: kelp raises pH (side effect)
- **Results:**
  - In an acidified experiment, pH was 7.44 / 7.74 / 8.01 / 8.42 at 0 / 0.3 / 0.7 / 1.0 g/L kelp.
  - In a closed CO₂-bubbled vessel, kelp raised pH ~0.3-0.4 per day, reaching ~8.8 by day 7.
  - On an oyster farm, pH was +0.15 inside the kelp lines.
- **What we take:** kelp raises pH as a function of dose. In a closed tank starting at 8.0, expect it to approach **8.6-9.0 within days**, so pH must be logged and capped.

### 19. Kim, Kraemer & Yarish 2015: kelp farms remove nitrogen in Long Island Sound
- **Results (abstract):** up to 180 / 67 / 38 kg N/ha per season at the Bronx River Estuary, western LIS and central LIS.
- **What we take:** a prevention co-benefit. It is not part of the bench test.

### 20. Semmouri, Janssen & Asselman 2024: review of allelopathy methods
- **Content:** 138 putative allelopathic seaweeds. The confounders are nutrient depletion, shading, contact effects, pH rise (*Ulva intestinalis* pushes rock pools above pH 10) and algicidal epiphytic bacteria. For contact effects it recommends **"a plastic model or another algal mimic"** as a control. Few studies held pH constant.
- **What we take:** the justification for the fake-seaweed control, the pH log, the nutrient log and a rinse step.

### 4b. Ranges and central values
- **Effective density:** **0.5-3 g/L FW** in nearly every study. Around 2 g/L is where diatoms (#3) and dinoflagellate EC50s (#5, #7) converge. Below 0.5 g/L, effects need ≥5 days (#2).
- **Time:**
  - 24 h: small (13-47%).
  - 48-72 h: the effect becomes large (LT50 38-64 h at 3.4 g/L, #8; 74-94% at ≥72 h, #3).
  - 5-20 days: needed for low density or tolerant or benthic species.
- **EC50 for planktonic dinoflagellates over 10 d:** about **2 g/L FW** (1.8-2.5; #5, #7). *P. micans* specifically: 1.8-2.3 g/L FW.
- **FW → DW:** *Ulva* ≈ 0.15 (#15). Powder is 3-10× more potent per gram of dry matter (powder EC50 0.19-0.8 g/L DW vs fresh ~2 g/L FW ≈ 0.3 g/L DW), but it cannot be retrieved.
- **Filtrate (medium conditioned by seaweed, then seaweed removed):** usually weak or none (#5, #6, #7 single dose, #14), transient (#9), or stimulating (#8 *Amphidinium*, #14). It worked for *Heterosigma* (#11) and with semi-continuous addition (#7).

## 5. Hypotheses for our experiment

Each hypothesis is falsifiable and fixed before the run. "Control" = same culture, same medium,
no seaweed.

| # | Hypothesis | Measurement | Expected direction / falsified if | Grounded in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Live sugar kelp at 2 g/L FW reduces diatom cell density vs control at 72 h | Sedgewick-Rafter counts at 0, 24, 48, 72 h (n = 3-4 flasks) | ≥50% lower at 72 h. Falsified if <20% lower or the 95% CI overlaps 0 | #3 (74-94% at ≥72 h), #17 |
| H2 | The effect is dose-dependent (0 < 0.5 < 1 < 2 g/L) | 72 h counts; % reduction vs dose | monotonic increase. Falsified if 0.5 ≥ 2 g/L | #3, #5, #7, #16 |
| H3 | Most of the effect appears after 48 h, not in the first 24 h | the time course | reduction at 24 h < half the reduction at 72 h | #3 (13-47% vs 74-94%), #8 |
| H4 | A plastic "fake seaweed" of matched area and shape does not reduce cells (shading and contact are not the cause) | the fake-panel arm vs the control at 72 h | within ±15% of control. Falsified if ≥30% lower | #20 |
| H5 | The effect is not explained by nutrient depletion | NO₃/PO₄ strips at 0 and 72 h; arm with a daily f/4 top-up | nutrients stay above limiting, and the top-up arm still shows ≥50% of the effect | #11, #14, #15 |
| H6 | The effect is not explained by pH | pH log; a "pH-matched control" arm raised with Na₂CO₃ to the kelp arm's pH (≤9.0) | the pH-matched control shows <25% reduction while kelp shows ≥50% | #16 (method; pH 9.0 alone inhibits), #14, #18 |
| H7 | A single dose of kelp-conditioned filtrate is weaker than the live panel | filtrate from 2 g/L kelp for 24 h, 0.2 µm or coffee-filter plus GF filtration | filtrate reduction < half the live-panel reduction | #5, #7, #9, #10, #14 |
| H8 | Suppression is reversible: after the panel is lifted (OFF), the culture regrows within the 5-day cool-down | counts and chlorophyll for 5 d after OFF | growth rate returns to ≥50% of the control's. Exploratory, no prior number | #9 (filtrate effect gone after 2 d), #10 (PUFAs decay) |
| H9 | The dinoflagellate (*P. micans*) responds more slowly than the diatom at the same dose | the same design with *P. micans* over 7-10 d | <50% at 72 h at 1-2 g/L; ~50% by day 10 at ~2 g/L | #7 (EC50 1.8-2.3 g/L over 10 d) |
| H10 | Kelp water does not harm a non-target animal | *Artemia* 24 h survival in 72 h panel water vs control | survival within 20 percentage points of control | #12 (larval fish unharmed), 00_CONTROL_LOOP safety rule |
| H11 | Kelp keeps pH ≤ 9.0 at 2 g/L in the bench vessel, while *Ulva* may exceed it | continuous pH | kelp max < 9.0; *Ulva* arm may exceed it | #1 (kelp 8.1-8.9, *Ulva* 9-10), #18 |

## 6. Mechanisms

| Mechanism | Evidence for | Evidence against / limits | How we test it on the bench |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Allelopathy** (released compounds) | Named in nearly every paper as primary (#1, #3, #14, #15, #16). Candidates: PUFAs HDTA, ALA, ODTA in *Ulva* (#10); polar compounds (#7); phenolic acids and alkaloids in *U. pertusa* (Sun et al. 2016, found in the search but not reviewed here); isethionic acid and citrate in *Gracilaria* (#17). Phlorotannins in brown algae (cited in #14) are the likely kelp candidates, but they are **not identified in kelp** in these sources | compounds degrade fast (#10); filtrate is often inactive (#5, #14) | the filtrate arm (H7); ruling out the other causes (H4-H6) |
| **Nutrient competition** | *Ulva* drew down DIN (#14); *Gracilaria* exhausted N and P, and a top-up removed its effect (#11) | cells still died when nutrients were replete (#11 *Ulva*, #15, #3) | NO₃/PO₄ strips; top-up arm (H5) |
| **pH rise** | pH 9.0 alone inhibits *Aureococcus* (#16); *Ulva* pushes pH to 9-10 (#1, #20) | effect persists with pH held at 8 (#14); kelp works at pH < 8.9 (#1) | pH log plus the pH-matched control (H6) |
| **Surface antioxidants** (DMSP) | protects fish from ROS near the thallus (#2) | local only; protects fish rather than killing algae | not tested (no fish) |
| **Epiphytic algicidal bacteria** | contributory in #1 and #16; 10⁵-10⁶ per g on *Ulva* (Imai et al. 2002, in `notes/snowball/marine.md` #27) | antibiotics did not remove the effect (#16) | rinse and wipe the seaweed; no antibiotics at school (see gaps) |
| **Shading / contact** | possible with floating panels (#20) | seaweed sat below the light in most studies | fake-seaweed control (H4) |

Physiological signs of allelopathy: cells shrink, turn pale and lyse; photosynthetic efficiency
(Fv/Fm, PSII) falls (#3, #15, #16, #17); benthic dinoflagellates encyst (#14).

## 8. Gaps and open questions

1. **No data for our stand-in species.** No paper tests *Phaeodactylum* or *Thalassiosira*; the closest are *Pseudo-nitzschia* (#3), *Nitzschia closterium* (#17) and *Skeletonema* (#6, #13). *Phaeodactylum* is famously robust, so a pilot must confirm it responds at all before the loop run.
2. **Kelp allelochemicals are unidentified.** Candidates are phlorotannins (brown-algal phenolics), but no source here isolates them from *Saccharina* in a HAB test.
3. **Reversibility and rebound after OFF (H8)** have not been measured directly for live panels. This is the key question for a controller that lifts the panel out.
4. **Open-water reach.** All effects are in closed vessels or 300-1000 L mesocosms. How far a panel works in flowing water is unknown (#2 shows the fish protection needs contact).
5. **Chlorophyll as the sensor signal.** Total chlorophyll did not fall in mixed mesocosms (#1). In a single-species tank it should track cells, but paling cells (lower chlorophyll per cell, #3) could exaggerate the apparent effect. Report both measures.
6. **Bacteria.** We cannot use antibiotics safely or easily at school, so the bacterial contribution stays confounded. Rinsing and wiping are the only controls.
7. **Five papers were read only through Ben Gharbia's Table 1 or a TLDR** (#4, #5, #6, #8, #9). Their doses and time points should be checked against the originals via interlibrary loan, especially whether Tang & Gobler 2011 reported doses in FW or DW.
8. **Seasonal sourcing.** Kelp is a winter-spring crop, so bench timing (Nov-Dec) relies on young farm or hatchery kelp, and its potency may differ from mature blades.
9. **Species can resist or be stimulated** (*Amphidinium* +78% in filtrate, #8; *Ostreopsis* in filtrate, #14; *G. vermiculophylla* raises *Alexandrium* toxin, `marine.md` #26). One non-target phytoplankton species in a mixed-culture arm would test selectivity.
