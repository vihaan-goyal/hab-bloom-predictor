Subject: Question about LIS chlorophyll decline after 2014 — student research project

Dear Dr. [Name],

I'm a senior at Westhill High School in Stamford working on a science research
project using the CT DEEP Long Island Sound water quality dataset (1993–2025,
via the UConn ERDDAP server). I'd be grateful for a few minutes of your
expertise on one result I don't want to misinterpret.

I've been building a chlorophyll-bloom early-warning model for the Sound, and
in trying to understand why its alerts are so often wrong I found something in
the raw data that I think is the real story: the fraction of station-days with
chlorophyll-a above 10 µg/L dropped from 42–59% in 2009–2013 to 9% in 2014 and
has stayed at 3–11% every year since. Over the same period, Narragansett Bay
(RIDEM's sonde network, which I used as a comparison) held steady at roughly
30–40%.

My working interpretation is that this is the nitrogen TMDL taking effect
around 2014, and that bloom rarity — not model quality or sampling frequency —
is what limits forecast precision in the Sound today. I tested the
sampling-frequency explanation three ways and it didn't hold up.

Two questions, if you have a moment:
1. Does a step change in chlorophyll exceedance around 2014 match your
   understanding of the Sound's response to nitrogen reductions, or is there
   a monitoring change (method, stations, lab) around then that I should be
   aware of?
2. Is there anyone at DEEP or UConn who has written this up whom I should cite?

I'm happy to share the analysis, code, or the year-by-year table. Thank you
for your time.
