# Marine and estuarine HAB mitigation: citation snowball (compiled 2026-09-24)

**Summary**
1. **Most promising low-impact marine methods:** live seaweeds (*Ulva*, sugar kelp *Saccharina*, *Gracilaria*) grown beside shellfish or fish (Gobler lab, Stony Brook; #21-#26), and bubbling with large bubbles in enclosed water (#33). Both add nothing foreign to the water and have been tested on Long Island species (*Margalefidinium*, *Alexandrium*, *Pseudo-nitzschia*, brown tide). After those come curcumin (#9-#11) and hydrogen peroxide (#16-#19), which break down in hours to days but still kill non-target plankton.
2. **Best teen experiment for Long Island Sound:** a co-culture of live or dried sea lettuce (*Ulva*) collected from a Connecticut beach, at 0-3 g/L, with a NON-TOXIC marine dinoflagellate in 250 mL flasks. Add a harmless control alga such as *Rhodomonas* to test selectivity. Measure cell counts or chlorophyll fluorescence for 72-96 h. It copies the design of #21-#24, and a curcumin arm (#9) can be added. **Do not use *Scrippsiella trochoidea* as the "safe" alga**: a Gobler paper title reports its NW Atlantic isolates are lethal to oyster and quahog larvae (listed at the end; not read).
3. **CLEAR (Dr. Dana Wetzel, Mote):** there is **no peer-reviewed paper and no published dataset**. Mote's official 2025 Initiative Report (agency-style report to the Florida legislature, #6) says 40 plant extracts and pure compounds were screened and two candidates chosen. Two formulations were made: slow- and fast-release particles, and a liquid for injection below the surface. All permits were obtained, and a pilot in a canal during an active bloom took place in February 2025.
4. The only CLEAR numbers are in press releases and news coverage. CLEAR and Xtreme were applied on 14 Feb 2025 in two Venice, FL canals. Mote's progress page reports a "70-80% reduction in red tide cells within an hour"; WUSF quoted a "70% kill rate". Wetzel was quoted: "total mortality in less than 15 minutes" in lab mesocosms; "97% of CLEAR is gone" in 8 h. None of this has methods, controls, or non-target data. The formula is proprietary.
5. **Big picture (#1):** Anderson et al. 2025 (*Harmful Algae*) conclude that marine HAB control is "one of the least developed areas of HAB science." Only clay and bacterial-algicide hydrogels (DinoSHIELD) had reached mesocosm or field stage in the US case studies. The US HAB Control Technologies Incubator (#3) is now funding marine seaweed, curcumin and bacterial-screening projects, including the Gobler seaweed project on *Alexandrium* and *Pseudo-nitzschia*.

Format notes: "Read" says what I actually saw. Numbers are only those in the text I read. "Agency report" covers NOAA or legislature-mandated program reports; "press" covers news or institutional releases.

## A. Reviews, agency programs and road maps

### 1. Controlling HABs in marine waters: status and prospects (Anderson, Wells, Trainer, Suddleson et al. incl. Coyne, Gobler, 2025) [peer-reviewed review]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.hal.2025.102989 · NOAA summary: https://coastalscience.noaa.gov/news/review-highlights-unmet-need-for-marine-hab-control-solutions/
- **What they did:** international review of marine and estuarine HAB control methods, focused on the few tested in mesocosms or the field. Its case studies are DinoSHIELD (bacterial algicide in a hydrogel) and modified clay flocculation against *Karenia brevis*.
- **Environment:** cites public, stakeholder and scientific uncertainty about "potentially undesirable environmental consequences" as a main barrier. Proposes a phased, precautionary framework (lab, then outdoor tests, then full scale) that weighs these against "the risk of inaction".
- **Results:** marine HAB control "remains one of the least developed areas of HAB science" and lags behind freshwater work. The barriers are regulation of in-situ testing, scaling up to 3-D open-water blooms, and an immature market. It recommends supporting the US HAB-CTI (#3).
- **Effective?** N/A (review). It is the best current map of what has been tried at sea.
- **Teen-reproducible?** N/A. Use it to frame the project's "gap" statement.
- **Read:** abstract and NOAA NCCOS news summary (the full text PDF was blocked).

### 2. Marine HABs in the United States: history, status, trends (Anderson, Fensin, Gobler, Hoeglund et al., 2021) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.hal.2021.101975
- **What they did:** 30 years (1990-2019) of HAEDAT records of US marine HAB events.
- **Environment:** background on problems, not mitigation.
- **Results:** PST events show no significant trend but have spread to new regions. Amnesic (domoic-acid) events have significantly increased, with "emerging, recurring outbreaks on the East Coast". Brown tides spread from New York (first in 1985) to Delaware-Virginia.
- **Effective?** N/A. It justifies why a Long Island Sound student would target *Alexandrium*, *Pseudo-nitzschia*, *Dinophysis* or *Margalefidinium*.
- **Teen-reproducible?** N/A.
- **Read:** abstract.

### 3. US HAB Control Technologies Incubator (HAB-CTI) (NOAA NCCOS, UMCES-IMET, Mote; 2022-2027) [agency program]
- **Link:** https://coastalscience.noaa.gov/project/united-states-harmful-algal-bloom-control-technologies-incubator/ · funded list: https://imet.usmd.edu/funded-projects
- **What they did:** annual competitions funding control technologies (Sept 2022 to Aug 2027). The NCCOS page says the goal is to test whether methods can "disrupt the life cycle of a HAB species" while staying environmentally sound and scalable. It also builds a clearinghouse of permitting requirements.
- **Environment:** environmental compliance is a stated selection criterion.
- **Results (funded marine projects):**
  - 2024-25: Stony Brook (Gobler), native seaweeds vs *Alexandrium* and *Pseudo-nitzschia*, $200,000. Mote, curcumin vs *Alexandrium* and *Pseudo-nitzschia*, $157,616. Mote, bacterial screening vs *Alexandrium*. WHOI, clay formulations. FIT, PAC-biochar.
  - 2025-26: UCSC (Kudela), FIFRA-exempt organic algaecide C71X. Mote, laser plasma on *Alexandrium* cyst beds. Plus others.
- **Effective?** N/A (funding program). It shows where US marine control research is heading: natural products and seaweeds.
- **Teen-reproducible?** N/A. It is a good citation that seaweed and curcumin are funded priorities.
- **Read:** NCCOS project page and IMET funded-projects page.

### 4. Assessing risks and mitigating impacts of HABs on mariculture (Brown, Lilley, Shutler, Lowe et al., 2020) [peer-reviewed review]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1111/raq.12403
- **What they did:** critical review of HAB impacts on fish and shellfish farming and wild fisheries.
- **Environment:** favours "pre-emptive, ecosystem-based approaches" over "reactive physical, chemical or microbiological control measures". Examples are siting farms well and growing extractive shellfish and macroalgae next to finfish farms.
- **Results:** estimates HAB impacts at about US$8 billion per year worldwide.
- **Effective?** Partly. It argues that prevention and siting beat killing blooms.
- **Teen-reproducible?** N/A.
- **Read:** abstract (open access).

### 5. Prevention, control and mitigation of HAB impacts on fish, shellfish and consumers (Sellner & Rensel, 2018) [book chapter]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1002/9781118994672.ch12
- **What they did:** a chapter in *Harmful Algal Blooms: A Compendium Desk Reference*, covering prevention, control and mitigation from freshwater ponds to open-ocean fish cages.
- **Environment:** notes multi-trophic designs (fish, shellfish, macroalgae, abalone).
- **Results:** the abstract gives no quantitative results.
- **Effective?** N/A.
- **Teen-reproducible?** N/A.
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 6. Florida Red Tide Mitigation & Technology Development Initiative, 6th report (Mote Marine Laboratory, Jan 2026) [agency report, legislature-mandated under F.S. 379.2273]
- **Link:** https://mote.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/RedTideInitiative_AccomplishmentsPrioritesReport2025.pdf · progress page: https://mote.org/research/centers-of-excellence/red-tide-initiative/progress/
- **What they did:** the status of more than 40 *K. brevis* projects, run through Tier 1 (lab), Tier 2 (1,400 L mesocosms) and Tier 3 (field). Five products had cleared permitting for field tests: Clear, Xtreme, Lake Guard Oxy, Ozonix and clay.
- **Environment:** projects must pass EPA (FIFRA) permitting, along with the Army Corps, FDEP, FDACS and FWC, and the report says "navigating these regulatory steps can be lengthy". Mesocosm non-target tests use blue crab, sea urchin and hard clam.
- **Results:** three field deployments since early 2025:
  - Clear and Xtreme in Venice canals on 14 Feb 2025, during a bloom.
  - Lake Guard Oxy at Sapphire Shores, 12 Nov 2025, with no bloom present.
  - Ozonix, a logistics trial near Fort Myers, Dec 2025.
  - The report gives no efficacy tables.
- **Effective?** Partly. It is the main public record, but it is not peer-reviewed.
- **Teen-reproducible?** N/A. It is the source for entries #7, #8, #12, #13 and #15.
- **Read:** full report text (PDF), plus the 2024 report and the progress page.

## B. Mote Red Tide Initiative technologies (Karenia brevis)

### 7. CLEAR, a plant-derived algicide ("Natural Compound Control and Mitigation for Red Tide") (Wetzel & Medvecky, Mote; 2020-2026) [agency report + press; NO peer-reviewed data]
- **Link:** 2025 report (#6), project 40 · Mote release https://mote.org/news/mote-and-partners-field-testing-promising-red-tide-initiative-technologies/ · WUSF https://www.wusf.org/environment/2025-01-30/new-weapons-in-fight-against-red-tide-unveiled-at-sarasotas-mote-marine-laboratory · field result https://www.wusf.org/environment/2025-02-20/new-tools-against-red-tide-show-success-rate-venice-canal-testing-mote · Your Observer https://www.yourobserver.com/news/2025/feb/03/mote-technology-combat-red-tide/
- **What they did:** dose-response cell counts of *K. brevis* against "40 unique plant extracts and pure compound candidates" (the report; Your Observer quotes "nearly 50"). Two candidates became two products: engineered particles for rapid and slow release, and a liquid injected below the surface. The liquid was piloted in a Venice canal during an active bloom in Feb 2025.
- **Environment (developer claims):**
  - Active ingredients are FDA "GRAS" (generally recognized as safe).
  - "Degrades fully within half a day"; "97% of CLEAR is gone" in 8 h (WUSF).
  - "No measurable accumulation."
  - Can be used "in areas where there's fish and in shellfish harvest areas."
  - No non-target data are published.
- **Results:**
  - Lab mesocosms: "total mortality in less than 15 minutes" (Wetzel, quoted in Your Observer).
  - Field, CLEAR and Xtreme together in two canals: "70-80% reduction in red tide cells within an hour" (Mote progress page), or a "70% kill rate" (WUSF, quoting K. Claridge).
  - Neither source gives methods, controls or toxin data.
- **Effective?** Unknown or Partly. The claims are promising, but no data can be checked.
- **Teen-reproducible?** No. The formula is proprietary. The screening design (a dose-response series of plant extracts against a dinoflagellate) *is* reproducible with kitchen extracts.
- **Read:** report text, Mote release, WUSF (x2), Your Observer.

### 8. Xtreme, a "natural non-toxic" product (Rowley, Heartland Energy Group; Pierce, Mote; 2022-2026) [agency report + press]
- **Link:** 2025 report (#6), project 36; Mote release (see #7)
- **What they did:** lab tests, then a mesocosm dose-range trial with clam toxicity tests. In Feb 2025 a barge-mounted sprayer treated "a one-acre canal in Venice, Florida" during a bloom. A small trial followed in St Andrews Bay (Jan 2026).
- **Environment:** judged exempt under EPA and FDACS pesticide rules. The developer claims "no harm to marine life, including commercially important clams." Ingredients are undisclosed.
- **Results:** "a reduction in red tide cells". Tides and wind need more study. See #7 for the combined 70-80% figure.
- **Effective?** Partly (field reduction reported, with no numbers of its own).
- **Teen-reproducible?** No (commercial product with undisclosed ingredients).
- **Read:** report text and Mote release.

### 9. Curcumin against *K. brevis* cells and brevetoxins (Hall, Heil, Frankle, Klass, Devillier, Lovko, Toyoda & Pierce, 2024) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.3390/w16101458
- **What they did:** benchtop tests at 0.1-40 mg/L curcumin on *K. brevis* at about 1.0 × 10^6 cells/L.
- **Environment:** not tested in this paper (see #10).
- **Results:**
  - 5 mg/L: 89% fewer cells and 60% less brevetoxin in 24 h.
  - 0.1-3 mg/L: 2-45% fewer cells.
  - 30-40 mg/L: 100% of cells gone by 6 h, and at least 64% less toxin by 48 h.
- **Effective?** Yes (lab).
- **Teen-reproducible?** Yes. Curcumin powder is cheap. Use a non-toxic dinoflagellate, and include a solvent control because curcumin dissolves poorly in water.
- **Read:** abstract (the MDPI page was blocked for full text).

### 10. Lessons from curcumin mesocosm experiments (Devillier, Hall, Heil, Frankle et al., 2025) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1002/lom3.10731
- **What they did:** 5 mg/L curcumin added to about 1.0 × 10^6 *K. brevis* cells/L in 1,400 L mesocosms (2022 and 2024). The 2024 run included hard clams, sea urchins and blue crabs.
- **Environment:** turbidity rose significantly above background. There was no significant effect on total dissolved N or P, and no significant effect on animal mortality or behaviour after 72 h.
- **Results:** cells and total toxins fell significantly within 24 h in both years.
- **Effective?** Yes at mesocosm scale. Mote's 2025 report says curcumin was "the most effective" of the compounds tested and is now in permitting for field trials.
- **Teen-reproducible?** Partly (the tank scale is not; the jar version is).
- **Read:** abstract.

### 11. Curcumin inhibits *K. brevis* thioredoxin reductase (Taher, Liu, Hondal & Rein, 2025) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plaphy.2025.110905
- **What they did:** enzyme assays and LC-MS/MS on the *K. brevis* antioxidant enzyme TrxR.
- **Environment:** N/A.
- **Results:** curcumin inhibited the enzyme at 3.0 μM, "approximately 20-fold more potent than PbTx-2". It binds covalently to Cys53 and turns the enzyme into a prooxidant.
- **Effective?** A mechanism paper. It explains why curcumin is an oxidative-stress killer, like peroxide.
- **Teen-reproducible?** No (biochemistry). It is useful for the hypothesis section.
- **Read:** abstract fragment (OpenAlex).

### 12. Minimum-risk (FIFRA-exempt) ingredients: cinnamon oil and thyme oil (Heil & Pierce, Mote; 2024-2025) [agency report]
- **Link:** 2025 report (#6), project 32
- **What they did:** reviewed EPA "Minimum Risk Pesticide" ingredients (exempt from registration) and ran Tier 1 lab range-finding tests of five commercially available ones on *K. brevis* cells and brevetoxins.
- **Environment:** chosen because the regulatory path is easier. No non-target data were reported yet.
- **Results:** "cinnamon oil and thyme oil emerged as the most effective compounds, consistently reducing K. brevis cells and brevetoxins within 24 hours". Essential oils beat powders.
- **Effective?** Partly (lab, with no numbers published).
- **Teen-reproducible?** **Yes.** Food-grade essential oils in jars with a non-toxic alga. A good second arm next to curcumin or *Ulva*.
- **Read:** report text.

### 13. Lake Guard Oxy (floating time-release peroxide) and Ozonix (Mote Initiative, 2023-2026) [agency report]
- **Link:** 2025 report (#6), projects 34 and 37
- **What they did:** Lake Guard Oxy floats and releases hydrogen peroxide. After Tier 1 and 2 tests, it was sprayed in a Sarasota cove in Nov 2025 with no bloom present. Ozonix is a 53 ft trailer that combines cavitation, ozone, ultrasound and electrochemical oxidation; it was deployed on a barge in Dec 2025.
- **Environment:** Lake Guard Oxy is "approved by the EPA for marine use". Ozonix claims "without harming live organisms". The report gives no bromate or oxidant by-product data for ozone in seawater.
- **Results:** the field runs tested logistics only, with no efficacy numbers.
- **Effective?** Unknown in the field.
- **Teen-reproducible?** No.
- **Read:** report text.

### 14. Calcium peroxide inside flocs for targeted *K. brevis* kill (Hossain, Mays, Hanhart, Hubble et al., 2024) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.hal.2024.102707
- **What they did:** built CaO₂ (which slowly releases H₂O₂) into polyaluminum chloride (PAC) flocs, so the cells are killed inside the floc and cannot escape. Measured by PAM fluorometry and viability assays.
- **Environment:** the peroxide breaks down. PAC adds aluminium, and the floc settles (the same objection as clay).
- **Results:** doses "as low as 30 mg/L" within flocs suppress photosynthesis and kill cells in 3-6 h.
- **Effective?** Yes (lab).
- **Teen-reproducible?** Partly. Pond-grade CaO₂ is available, but it is an oxidizer, and flocculation raises the clay objection.
- **Read:** abstract.

### 15. Sprayable foams carrying algaecides and flocculants (AziziHariri, Hossain, Burni, Raghavan et al., 2025) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1021/acsestwater.5c00111
- **What they did:** a surfactant foam laid on the bloom surface carries H₂O₂ generators (gallic acid or CaO₂) and/or PAC against *K. brevis*.
- **Environment:** high oxidant at the surface, diluted deeper down, "to potentially minimize impact to off-target organisms". The foam dissipates, "leaving no residue" (authors' claim). The surfactant itself is not addressed in the abstract.
- **Results:** the abstract says flocculation and delivery were effective, with no numbers.
- **Effective?** Partly (proof of concept).
- **Teen-reproducible?** Partly. A foam delivery demo in a tank is feasible; the surfactant choice needs care.
- **Read:** abstract.

## C. Oxidizers tested on marine HAB species

### 16. Termination of a toxic *Alexandrium* bloom with hydrogen peroxide (Burson, Matthijs, de Bruijne, Talens et al., 2014) [peer-reviewed; FIELD]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.hal.2013.10.017 · https://research.wur.nl/en/publications/termination-of-a-toxic-alexandrium-bloom-with-hydrogen-peroxide
- **What they did:** treated an *Alexandrium ostenfeldii* bloom of more than 1 million cells/L in the Ouwerkerkse Kreek, a brackish creek in the Netherlands (Aug 2012), with 50 mg/L H₂O₂ across the entire creek system.
- **Environment:** significant effects on zooplankton; minimal effects on macroinvertebrates and fish. H₂O₂ breaks down to water and oxygen within days.
- **Results:** vegetative cells and pellicle cysts fell 99.8% within 48 h, and PSP toxins in the water dropped below the 15 µg/L local limit. The authors call it the first successful field use of H₂O₂ on a marine HAB. *Alexandrium* came back at lower levels the next year.
- **Effective?** Yes, but temporary, with zooplankton collateral damage.
- **Teen-reproducible?** Yes, as jars of drugstore 3% H₂O₂ on a non-toxic dinoflagellate, with a zooplankton arm (brine shrimp) to measure non-target harm.
- **Read:** abstract (WUR repository page).

### 17. USEPA-registered algaecides on *K. brevis* (Hu, Berthold, Wang, Xiao et al., 2022) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.hal.2022.102347
- **What they did:** six registered algaecides (3 copper-based, 3 peroxide-based) on *K. brevis* at natural bloom density (1.79 × 10^7 cells/L).
- **Environment:** copper persists (not low-impact). Peroxide stability depended on salinity, microbes and organic matter. Oximycin P5 decayed at 0.467 mg/d in natural seawater. The authors say "pilot studies on marine non-targets are still needed."
- **Results:** 0.31-0.34 mg Cu/L caused a significant cell drop within 24 h. The peroxide products needed 4.89-7.08 mg H₂O₂/L, below the maximum label rate. Toxin reduction was strongest at higher peroxide doses (hydroxyl-radical oxidation).
- **Effective?** Yes (lab). Peroxide also destroys toxin; copper does not.
- **Teen-reproducible?** Partly. Do the peroxide arm only; skip copper.
- **Read:** abstract.

### 18. Five oxidants against ichthyotoxic *Prymnesium* and *Heterosigma* (Moreno-Andrés, Romero-Martínez, Seoane, Acevedo-Merino et al., 2023) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhazmat.2023.131279
- **What they did:** 14-day concentration-response curves for H₂O₂, peracetic acid, peroxymonosulfate, peroxydisulfate and chlorine (ballast-water context).
- **Environment:** framed as alternatives to chlorine, which forms by-products.
- **Results:**
  - EC50: peroxymonosulfate 0.40-1.99 mg/L; peracetic acid 0.32-2.70 mg/L (strong but short-lived); H₂O₂ 6.67-7.08 mg/L (longer-lasting).
  - Peroxydisulfate had negligible effect.
  - *Heterosigma* was more resistant than *Prymnesium*, except to H₂O₂.
- **Effective?** Yes (lab).
- **Teen-reproducible?** Partly. The H₂O₂ dose curve is feasible; the other oxidants need a school chemistry lab.
- **Read:** abstract.

### 19. H₂O₂ vs copper sulfate on fish-killing flagellates, including swimming behaviour (Allaf, Yi, Zhang, Zhang et al., 2026) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.3390/microorganisms14051086
- **What they did:** *Prymnesium parvum*, *Heterosigma akashiwo* and *Fibrocapsa japonica* exposed to H₂O₂ and CuSO₄. Measured mortality, viability and motility tracking (mean squared displacement).
- **Environment:** argues that stopping motility at sublethal doses could "minimize chemical loading" near fish farms.
- **Results:** *P. parvum* was most sensitive to H₂O₂; *H. akashiwo* and *F. japonica* were more sensitive to copper. Motility fell quickly at sublethal doses.
- **Effective?** Partly (lab; species-specific).
- **Teen-reproducible?** **Yes (the motility part).** Phone-microscope video of swimming cells under low peroxide doses is a cheap, quantitative endpoint.
- **Read:** abstract.

## D. Seaweeds and allelopathy (Northeast US, Gobler lab)

### 20. Allelopathy of *Cochlodinium* (*Margalefidinium*) from Long Island estuaries (Tang & Gobler, 2010) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.3354/meps08537 (open access PDF at int-res.com)
- **What they did:** exposed cultured and natural phytoplankton to *C. polykrikoides* strains and blooms from Long Island estuaries.
- **Environment:** background. The killing agent is extracellular, short-lived and ROS-like: adding the enzymes peroxidase and catalase removed or reduced it.
- **Results:** target algae lost motility and had 60-100% mortality within minutes to 24 h. Freezing, heating, sonication or filtration abolished the effect.
- **Effective?** N/A. It explains why antioxidants (seaweed DMSP, #23) protect fish.
- **Teen-reproducible?** No (needs the HAB species).
- **Read:** abstract.

### 21. *Ulva lactuca* inhibits seven HAB species (Tang & Gobler, 2011) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.hal.2011.03.003
- **What they did:** fresh *Ulva lactuca* thalli, and dried-powder extracts with and without heat treatment. Tested against *Aureococcus*, *Chattonella*, *Cochlodinium*, *Karlodinium*, *K. brevis*, *Prorocentrum minimum* and *Pseudo-nitzschia multiseries*. Field experiments used Long Island brown-tide blooms.
- **Environment:** a native seaweed. It contains "heat-stable allelochemicals".
- **Results:** at environmentally realistic levels it lysed or strongly inhibited all seven species, dose-dependently. Live thalli gave "significant and often large (>50%)" reductions of *Aureococcus* in about 48 h in field bottles.
- **Effective?** Yes (lab and field bottles).
- **Teen-reproducible?** **Yes, the best fit.** Sea lettuce is abundant on Connecticut shores, and it can be used fresh or as dried powder.
- **Read:** abstract as quoted in search results (ScienceDirect blocked); Semantic Scholar summary.

### 22. Cultivable seaweeds vs *Alexandrium catenella* and saxitoxin in mussels (Sylvers & Gobler, 2021) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.hal.2021.102056
- **What they did:** sugar kelp (*Saccharina latissima*), Irish moss (*Chondrus crispus*) and *Ulva*. Tested in co-cultures, bottle incubations of field blooms, mesocosms, and a toxin-uptake test with blue mussels.
- **Environment:** called "non-invasive, environmentally friendly and potentially profit-generating". Nutrient competition, higher pH and seaweed-attached bacteria contributed; the main mechanism is allelopathy.
- **Results:**
  - Co-cultures: 17-74% reduction in 2-3 days and 42-96% in about a week.
  - Field-bloom bottles: up to 95% reduction.
  - Mesocosms: kelp cut *A. catenella* 73% in 48 h; *Ulva* cut it 54% in 96 h.
  - Mussels: STX with kelp was 71.8 vs 93.5 µg/100 g in controls, i.e. below the 80 µg closure limit.
- **Effective?** Yes (up to mesocosm scale). Directly relevant to Long Island Sound shellfish and kelp farms.
- **Teen-reproducible?** Yes, with a non-toxic dinoflagellate in place of *Alexandrium*.
- **Read:** abstract.

### 23. Seaweeds eliminate *Margalefidinium* toxicity to larval fish (Sylvers & Gobler, 2023) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aquaculture.2023.739676
- **What they did:** *Ulva*, *Gracilaria*, *Chondrus* and *Grateloupia* with cultured and wild *M. polykrikoides*. Test fish were early-life silversides (*Menidia menidia*, *M. beryllina*) and sheepshead minnow.
- **Environment:** a protective effect. DMSP (a seaweed antioxidant) alone significantly reduced toxicity. Protection needed close physical proximity to *Ulva*.
- **Results:** seaweeds extended fish survival in all cases, sometimes to control levels. 0.2 g/L *Ulva* cut wild *M. polykrikoides* 68-92% over longer times.
- **Effective?** Yes (lab). It suggests *Ulva* beds are refuges for fish.
- **Teen-reproducible?** Partly. Seaweed vs algae is easy; fish assays need an animal-care protocol (use brine shrimp instead).
- **Read:** abstract.

### 24. Seaweeds inhibit toxic *Pseudo-nitzschia* and domoic acid in shellfish (Sylvers & Gobler, 2025) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1002/lno.70127
- **What they did:** sugar kelp, *Ulva* and *Gracilaria* vs *P. multiseries* and *P. australis* in co-cultures and field bottles. Toxin accumulation in blue mussels and razor clams.
- **Environment:** a non-invasive measure that can be integrated with shellfish farms.
- **Results:**
  - Co-cultures: 13-47% reduction in 24-48 h, and 74-94% at 72 h and later (*Gracilaria* was not effective).
  - Field bloom bottles: kelp at 2 g/L gave 69-81% reduction.
  - Kelp significantly lowered DA accumulation in mussels and razor clams.
- **Effective?** Yes (lab and field bottles). The diatom target is relevant to Long Island Sound's diatom blooms.
- **Teen-reproducible?** Yes, with a non-toxic pennate diatom (e.g., *Phaeodactylum*) as a stand-in.
- **Read:** abstract (open access).

### 25. Red seaweeds *Gracilaria tikvahiae* and *Dasysiphonia japonica* vs brown tide (Benitt, Young, Sylvers, Gobler et al., 2022) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10811-021-02677-9
- **What they did:** co-cultured *Aureococcus anophagefferens* strains with 0.25-3.00 g/L seaweed, measuring cells and photosynthetic efficiency, with nutrient, bacteria and pH co-factors.
- **Environment:** native *Gracilaria* is a candidate crop. *Dasysiphonia* is invasive and was stronger, which is not a crop to promote.
- **Results:** significant dose-dependent reductions in nearly all experiments. *Dasysiphonia* was stronger than *Gracilaria* and *Ulva*. Allelopathy was the main mechanism.
- **Effective?** Yes (lab).
- **Teen-reproducible?** Yes. *Gracilaria tikvahiae* is native to Long Island Sound.
- **Read:** abstract (open access).

### 26. Caution: an invasive red alga raises *Alexandrium* toxin per cell (Barriuso, Díaz-Alonso, Teira, Rodríguez et al., 2026) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jap.2026.100009
- **What they did:** exudates of eelgrass *Zostera marina* and invasive *Gracilaria vermiculophylla* (also present in the NW Atlantic) on *Alexandrium minutum*.
- **Environment:** a non-target warning: seaweed exudates can make HAB cells *more* toxic.
- **Results:** *Zostera* exudate lowered growth rate. *G. vermiculophylla* did not inhibit growth, and toxin per cell rose significantly.
- **Effective?** Partly (eelgrass yes; *G. vermiculophylla* backfires).
- **Teen-reproducible?** Yes, as a seaweed-choice control. Always check the species.
- **Read:** abstract (open access).

### 27. Co-culture of fish with macroalgae as a red tide defence (Imai, Fujimaru & Nishigaki, 2002) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.2331/fishsci.68.sup1_493
- **What they did:** counted algicidal bacteria on *Ulva* and *Gelidium* surfaces and in seaweed-bed water (Osaka Bay).
- **Environment:** a "gentle" strategy for enclosed small bays.
- **Results:** up to about 10^5-10^6 algicidal bacteria per gram wet seaweed. The authors propose co-culturing fish and seaweed.
- **Effective?** Partly (a proposal from counts, not a trial).
- **Teen-reproducible?** Partly (plating seaweed rinse on agar is doable; bacterial ID is not).
- **Read:** abstract (open access).

## E. Biological control (bacteria, viruses, grazers) and physical methods (#33-#34)

### 28. Shewanella IRI-160 immobilized in alginate beads (Wang & Coyne, 2020) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.hal.2020.101798
- **What they did:** trapped the algicidal bacterium (from Delaware Inland Bays) in agarose, alginate, sponge and foam. Tested at 10^6-10^8 cells/mL on *Karlodinium veneficum* and *Prorocentrum minimum*, with harmless *Rhodomonas* as a non-target control.
- **Environment:** more than 99% of bacteria stayed in the alginate after 12 days. There was no effect on *Rhodomonas*.
- **Results:** the beads were as effective as free bacteria against both dinoflagellates.
- **Effective?** Yes (lab). The basis of DinoSHIELD.
- **Teen-reproducible?** Partly. Alginate beads are a classic school lab, but culturing the bacterium needs biosafety approval. The *Rhodomonas* selectivity design is worth copying.
- **Read:** abstract.

### 29. IRI-160AA algicide causes no cortisol stress in estuarine fish (Simons, Targett, Gaffney, Coyne et al., 2025) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10126-024-10383-z
- **What they did:** exposed mummichog (*Fundulus heteroclitus*) and Atlantic silverside (*Menidia menidia*), both Long Island Sound species, to IRI-160AA. Some tests added diel hypoxia and pH cycling, at 25 and 30 °C.
- **Environment:** there was no significant change in plasma cortisol under any combination.
- **Results:** see Environment. Supports use as "environmentally friendly".
- **Effective?** N/A (non-target safety study). Yes for safety.
- **Teen-reproducible?** No (fish blood sampling).
- **Read:** abstract (open access).

### 30. DinoSHIELD stakeholder engagement (Hounshell, Horgan, Cummings, Staugler et al., 2025) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envc.2025.101272
- **What they did:** workshops in SW Florida with pre- and post-surveys on DinoSHIELD (IRI-160 in hydrogel vs *K. brevis*).
- **Environment:** stakeholders flagged ecological safety and deployment as research gaps.
- **Results:** 97% of participants increased understanding and 85% increased comfort.
- **Effective?** N/A. It is a model for the social-acceptance part of a project.
- **Teen-reproducible?** **Yes.** A pre/post survey of Connecticut shellfishers or beachgoers on seaweed vs chemical control.
- **Read:** abstract.

### 31. Virus-laden sediment protects farmed oysters from *Heterocapsa circularisquama* (Nakayama, Hamaguchi, Yamaguchi, Masuda et al., 2020) [peer-reviewed; FIELD]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aquaculture.2020.735625
- **What they did:** dispersed local sediment containing the host-specific virus HcRNAV into 15,000 L floating mesocosms at an oyster farm on Sado Island, Japan, early in a bloom.
- **Environment:** uses local sediment and a species-specific virus. Wider effects were not reported in the abstract.
- **Results:** the target fell to about 1% of its initial density after 5 days, and virus counts rose significantly by day 2.
- **Effective?** Yes (field enclosure).
- **Teen-reproducible?** No (virology).
- **Read:** abstract (Semantic Scholar).

### 32. *Artemia* grazing on New York HABs (Marcoval, Pan, Tang, Gobler, 2013) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecss.2013.05.034
- **What they did:** grazing and survival tests with *Artemia salina* (non-native) and native *Acartia tonsa*. Prey were *Alexandrium fundyense*, *Aureococcus* and *C. polykrikoides*. Included 25 field experiments across six New York estuaries.
- **Environment:** *Artemia* is non-native, which raises an introduction risk.
- **Results:** *Artemia* grazed all three HABs, and *Acartia* did not graze *Alexandrium*. 48 h mortality on *A. fundyense* was 43% (*Artemia*) vs 72% (*Acartia*).
- **Effective?** Partly (grazing yes; field use unlikely).
- **Teen-reproducible?** **Yes.** Brine shrimp are sold as kits. Grazing clearance of a non-toxic dinoflagellate is a classic experiment, and *Artemia* also works as the non-target test animal for any treatment.
- **Read:** abstract.

### 33. Macrobubble aeration mitigates *Margalefidinium* growth and fish-killing (Sung & Gobler, 2026) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvman.2026.129015
- **What they did:** compared orbital shaking, nanobubbles (with and without ozone) and macrobubble injection on wild and cultured *M. polykrikoides*, with fish toxicity tests.
- **Environment:** "non-chemical". It shifted the community toward *Prorocentrum cordatum*, a genus-level change.
- **Results:** macrobubbles "significantly and consistently" suppressed growth and photosynthetic efficiency. They reduced, and sometimes eliminated, fish mortality where unbubbled controls had 100% mortality.
- **Effective?** Yes (lab and bloom water). Suited to aquaculture tanks and pens.
- **Teen-reproducible?** **Yes.** An aquarium air pump and air stone in flasks with a non-toxic dinoflagellate. Dinoflagellates are shear-sensitive; test bubble size.
- **Read:** abstract (Semantic Scholar).

### 34. Ultrasound frequency vs *Margalefidinium* and *Pyrodinium* (Sabah, Malaysia, 2022) [peer-reviewed, regional journal]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.21608/ejabf.2022.281481
- **What they did:** f/2 cultures exposed to 20 kHz-1,100 kHz ultrasound.
- **Environment:** no chemicals added. Non-target effects were not tested.
- **Results:** removal rose with frequency. At 20 kHz: 18±3% (*M. polykrikoides*) and 20±2% (*Pyrodinium*). At 1,100 kHz: 96±2% and 86±3%.
- **Effective?** Yes (lab).
- **Teen-reproducible?** Partly. A 40 kHz ultrasonic cleaner gives low removal; high-frequency transducers are not household items.
- **Read:** abstract.

### 35. Ciliate *Strombidinopsis jeokjo* controls *Cochlodinium* in mesocosms (Jeong, Kim, Yoo, Kim et al., 2008) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.hal.2007.12.004
- **What they did:** added the naked ciliate to mesocosm enclosures with *C. polykrikoides* (Korea).
- **Environment:** a native grazer, but mass culture and release change the plankton community.
- **Results:** the summary says grazing impact means "large-scale cultures of this ciliate could be used for controlling red tides ... in small areas" (no numbers seen).
- **Effective?** Partly.
- **Teen-reproducible?** No.
- **Read:** Semantic Scholar summary only.

### 36. Prodigiosin (bacterial pigment) vs *Prorocentrum donghaiense*, with non-target tests (Chen, Luo, Chen, Zhang et al., 2021) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecoenv.2021.112913
- **What they did:** mechanism (ROS, lipid peroxidation, cell-cycle arrest) plus acute toxicity to *Chlorella*, *Photobacterium*, *Artemia* and sea bass.
- **Environment:** all four non-targets were less sensitive than the HAB. Toxicity declined after 6 h of sunlight (photodegradation). Surviving cells recovered once the prodigiosin degraded.
- **Results:** the authors advise applying "at night, in small doses, with multiple applications".
- **Effective?** Yes (lab).
- **Teen-reproducible?** No (compound not accessible). The non-target panel design (algae, brine shrimp) is copyable.
- **Read:** abstract (open access).

## F. Non-target effects of clay-based treatments (context only; the counselor ruled out clay)

### 37. Modified clay and adult blue crabs (Devillier, Hall, Anderson, Lewis et al., 2023) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.hal.2023.102492
- **What they did:** 48 blue crabs exposed for 8 days to one of three treatments: PAC-modified clay (MC II, 0.5 g/L), *K. brevis* (1 × 10^6 cells/L), or both.
- **Environment:** no significant differences in mortality, righting or eyestalk reflexes.
- **Results:** clay removed 95% of cells from the water in 24 h.
- **Effective?** Yes (cell removal). Low harm to adult crabs.
- **Teen-reproducible?** No (clay ruled out).
- **Read:** abstract.

### 38. Mesocosm of PAC-modified clay: chemistry and benthic animals (Devillier, Hall, Lovko, Pierce et al., 2024) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.hal.2024.102609
- **What they did:** 1,400 L tanks with MC II at 0.2 g/L on 1 × 10^6 *K. brevis*/L. Animals were blue crab, sea urchin and hard clam, over 72 h.
- **Environment:**
  - Dissolved P was lower, and DIC and alkalinity were reduced.
  - pCO₂ rose after 2 h.
  - Turbidity returned to control levels by 48 h.
  - Some brevetoxin forms (BTx-3, BTx-B5) were higher in treated tanks.
  - There was no difference in animal responses.
- **Results:** 57% cell removal at 8 h and 95% at 48 h.
- **Effective?** Yes, but the chemistry of the water changes, which is what the counselor objected to.
- **Teen-reproducible?** No.
- **Read:** abstract.

### 39. Brevetoxin and aluminium from clay floc in clams (Ohnikian, Sibley, Freiberger, Buck et al., 2025) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.3390/toxins17110560
- **What they did:** MC II (0.2 g/L) in mesocosms, following the brevetoxins in the settled floc and in hard clams (*Mercenaria mercenaria*) over 72 h.
- **Environment:** brevetoxins accumulated in sediment. Clam tissue BTX was similar to controls, but aluminium was significantly elevated, with no mortality.
- **Results:** 91% fewer cells and 50% less waterborne BTX after 5 h.
- **Effective?** Yes (short-term), but the toxin moves to the seabed.
- **Teen-reproducible?** No.
- **Read:** abstract (open access).

### 40. Oxidized modified clay degrades paralytic shellfish toxins (Song, Song, Shen, Ding et al., 2023) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecoenv.2023.114667
- **What they did:** clay modified with potassium peroxymonosulfate, tested on *Alexandrium pacificum*.
- **Environment:** the oxidant destroys dissolved toxin. Viable cells survive in the settled floc.
- **Results:** at 10 mg/L, extracellular PSTs fell below detection within 15 min, and more toxic congeners (GTX1&4) were converted to less toxic ones.
- **Effective?** Yes (lab).
- **Teen-reproducible?** No.
- **Read:** abstract (open access).

### 41. Economic impact, management and mitigation of red tides in Korea (Park, Lim, Park, Lee & Jeong, 2013) [peer-reviewed review]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.hal.2013.10.012
- **What they did:** reviewed Korea's three decades of *Cochlodinium* fish-farm losses and yellow-clay dispersal.
- **Environment:** the review describes clay as having no "notable effects on aquatic organisms and water quality". That claim contrasts with the chemistry changes in #38 and #39.
- **Results:** about US$121 million in aquaculture losses over 30 years. Yellow clay is described as "the best practical method".
- **Effective?** Yes, operationally in Korea, but only around farms.
- **Teen-reproducible?** No.
- **Read:** abstract content via search-result excerpts only (ScienceDirect blocked). Verify before citing numbers.

## G. Long Island specific risk

### 42. Dying algae fuel *Vibrio* in Long Island bays (Gobler lab, State of the Bays 2026) [press / university news]
- **Link:** https://news.stonybrook.edu/university/gobler-lab-monitors-algae-hungry-bacteria-in-long-island-waters/
- **What they did:** monitored *Vibrio vulnificus* and saxitoxin-producing algae in Southold Bay, Shinnecock Bay and nearby creeks. Added lysed algal cells to water in experiments.
- **Environment:** **a key non-target risk for any algicide.** Killing a bloom releases organic matter that feeds pathogenic bacteria.
- **Results:** lysed algae gave "a 200-fold increase in Vibrio concentration in a single day." The 2025 Southold bloom set New York PSP-intensity records.
- **Effective?** N/A (risk evidence).
- **Teen-reproducible?** Partly. The concept can be tested with total heterotrophic bacteria (plate counts) after lysing a harmless alga, never with *Vibrio*.
- **Read:** university news page (no peer-reviewed paper cited).

### 43. Mote shellfish biosensor and land-based depuration (Wetzel, Sherwood & Miller; project dates 2020-2023) [agency report]
- **Link:** 2024 Initiative report https://mote.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/RedTideInitiative_AccomplishmentsPrioritesReport2024.pdf (project 13)
- **What they did:** developed an aptamer-based field test kit for brevetoxin in shellfish, and depuration of oysters, clams and sunray venus clams in land-based recirculating tanks.
- **Environment:** a mitigation method that treats the harvest, not the sea. No chemicals are added to open water.
- **Results:** "at red tide toxin accumulation levels of over 4.0 ppm, depuration to below regulatory limits was achieved in less than 24 hours."
- **Effective?** Yes (for protecting consumers).
- **Teen-reproducible?** No (toxin analysis). It is useful context for a "protect the shellfish, not the sea" angle.
- **Read:** report text.
