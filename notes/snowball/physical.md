# Physical HAB mitigation (no added chemicals): snowball review

**Summary (2026-09-24).**
1. **Best teen experiment, bubbling a marine HAB alga:** an aquarium air pump driving coarse "macrobubbles" versus a fine airstone versus still water, in tall clear columns of a non-toxic dinoflagellate culture. Sung & Gobler (2026, #22) found that macrobubbles cut *Margalefidinium polykrikoides*, a Long Island Sound fish-killer, by more than 60%, and that nanobubbles managed less than 20%.
2. **Second, darkness and shading jars:** foil or shade-cloth jars (100/85/65% light, plus 5-20 days of full dark), followed by re-lighting to measure how fast the alga regrows (#28-#31). This is the cheapest test and the easiest to control.
3. **Third, an ultrasonic jewellery cleaner:** a dose-response test (seconds versus minutes), with regrowth tracked over 1-7 days and a *Daphnia* non-target arm (#1-#3, #8). The lab effect is real; whether it lasts, and whether it spares other organisms, is the open question.
4. **Consensus:** artificial mixing works only in deep lakes (mean depth >15 m), with enough air and buoyant cyanobacteria. It fails or backfires in shallow water and near single surface devices. Hypolimnetic oxygenation improves oxygen and phosphorus over years, but it is not a quick fix for a bloom. Ultrasound works in beakers but has failed four independent field tests (2023-2026) and two lab tests of commercial units in 2014. Oxygen-only nanobubbles showed no effect in one mesocosm test. Skimming and harvesting buy days, not seasons.
5. **Marine relevance is thin:** almost all field work is in freshwater reservoirs. The only marine and brackish evidence is lab work (#22, #25, #26, #27) and one estuary flushing study (#33).

Numbers come from abstracts or agency pages unless an entry is marked "full text". Where only an abstract was read, the entry says so.

## A. Ultrasound

### 1. "Beating the blues": commercial ultrasound transducers (Lürling & Tolman, 2014) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.watres.2014.08.043
- **What they did:** lab cultures of *Anabaena*, *Cylindrospermopsis*, *Microcystis* and the green alga *Scenedesmus*, grown with or without a commercial pond transducer (a mix of 20, 28 and 44 kHz). One arm included the grazer *Daphnia magna*.
- **Environment:** killed **all *Daphnia* within 15 min**, which released *Scenedesmus* from grazing.
- **Results:** filamentous cyanobacteria grew more slowly (their filaments broke) but still grew. There was no effect on *Microcystis* or *Scenedesmus* growth.
- **Effective?** No. The authors concluded there is "no music" in in-situ control with the units they tested.
- **Teen-reproducible?** Yes, the design copies easily: an ultrasonic cleaner, a culture and *Daphnia* from a biology supplier.
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 2. Ultrasound kills Daphnia, so the water turns greener (Lürling & Tolman, 2014) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.3390/w6113247 (open access, MDPI)
- **What they did:** 15 *Daphnia* in 800 mL exposed to 20, 28, 36 or 44 kHz from commercial transducers, then 25 days in six 85 L tanks holding algae, cyanobacteria and *Daphnia*.
- **Environment:** every *Daphnia* died, taking 10 min at 44 kHz and 135 min at 20 kHz. Raising the volume to 3.2 L did not help them survive.
- **Results:** in the ultrasound tanks *Daphnia* nearly vanished and phytoplankton biomass rose; the control tanks cleared.
- **Effective?** No, and it backfired, because losing the grazers made the water greener.
- **Teen-reproducible?** Yes, and it is the best non-target test to copy: jars, *Daphnia* and an ultrasonic cleaner.
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 3. Peroxide against ultrasound on toxic *Microcystis* (Lürling, Meng & Faassen, 2014) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.3390/toxins6123260 (open access)
- **What they did:** lab assays on microcystin-producing *M. aeruginosa*: H2O2 at 1-8 mg/L against commercial pond-ultrasound transducers.
- **Environment:** both treatments released microcystin into the water. At 4-8 mg/L, peroxide raised dissolved toxin 9-12 times.
- **Results:** peroxide at 4-8 mg/L cut biomass. Ultrasound gave "only minimal growth inhibition".
- **Effective?** No for ultrasound: "Commercial ultrasound transducers are therefore ineffective at controlling cyanobacteria."
- **Teen-reproducible?** Partly. The biomass half is doable; measuring toxin needs ELISA kits.
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 4. Review of sonication for cyanobacteria (Rajasekhar, Fan, Nguyen & Roddick, 2012) [peer-reviewed review]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.watres.2012.05.054
- **What they did:** reviewed lab sonication of *M. aeruginosa*: power, frequency and exposure time.
- **Environment:** not assessed in the abstract.
- **Results:** mechanisms are gas-vesicle collapse, damage to photosynthesis and cell rupture. (Part 1 #16 quotes ">60% removal after 5 min at 20 kHz" from this review; that number was not rechecked here.)
- **Effective?** Partly: in the lab, at high power density.
- **Teen-reproducible?** Yes, in the lab.
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 5. Challenges and field applications of ultrasound (Park, Church, Son, Kim & Lee, 2017) [peer-reviewed review]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ultsonch.2017.03.003
- **What they did:** reviewed lab and field ultrasound studies against HABs.
- **Environment:** called "low impact", but that is asserted, not measured.
- **Results:** "Only a few field and pilot tests in small reservoirs were reported and the applicability ... is still under question." Rain, flow, turbidity and scale-up are unresolved.
- **Effective?** Partly: proven in the lab, unproven in the field.
- **Teen-reproducible?** Not applicable (a review).
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 6. Higher frequency removes more *Microcystis*, but toxin rises (Zhang, Zhang, Wang & Liu, 2006) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ultsonch.2005.09.012
- **What they did:** lab sonication of *M. aeruginosa* at 20-1320 kHz and 32-80 W, for minutes.
- **Environment:** 80 W at 80 kHz for 5 min raised extracellular microcystin from 0.87 to 3.11 µg/L.
- **Results:** first-order removal. The rate constant was 0.114/min at 1320 kHz against 0.0224/min at 20 kHz, and 0.023/min at 80 W against 0.007/min at 32 W. Chlorophyll-a fell and then levelled off after 5 min.
- **Effective?** Yes in beakers, with a trade-off in toxin release.
- **Teen-reproducible?** Partly. A cleaner runs at a fixed ~40 kHz, so the student can vary time but not frequency.
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 7. Mechanisms change with frequency (Wu, Joyce & Mason, 2012) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.watres.2012.02.019
- **What they did:** *M. aeruginosa* at 20, 580 and 1146 kHz; viability measured by flow cytometry.
- **Environment:** not assessed.
- **Results:** 20 kHz at high intensity (0.0403 W/cm³) ruptures cells. 580 kHz also inactivates them, through free radicals. 1146 kHz only broke up clumps. At low power, cells clump less but are not killed.
- **Effective?** Yes in the lab at high intensity.
- **Teen-reproducible?** Partly. Use cell counts or optical density instead of flow cytometry.
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 8. Growth stops only while the sound is on (Ahn, Park, Joung, Kim, Jang & Oh, 2003) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1021/es034048z
- **What they did:** a lab culture of *M. aeruginosa* UTEX 2388 plus an enclosure in a lake.
- **Environment:** in the enclosure, sonication lowered pH and dissolved oxygen, raised temperature and conductivity, and **raised orthophosphate**.
- **Results:** growth "recovered immediately after the interruption of ultrasonication". In the enclosure, cells and chlorophyll-a "drastically decreased after 3 d", and cyanobacteria were selectively reduced. Sonicating late in the day worked best, when the cells were dividing.
- **Effective?** Partly: only while running continuously.
- **Teen-reproducible?** Yes. An on/off recovery experiment with a cleaner is a clean design.
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 9. Three seconds of 28 kHz sinks cyanobacteria (Lee, Nakano & Matsumura, 2001) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1080/09593332208618270
- **What they did:** a naturally grown blue-green bloom suspension; 3 s to 5 min at 28 or 100 kHz and 120-1200 W.
- **Environment:** microcystin did not rise even after 5 min at 1200 W.
- **Results:** a 3 s exposure (120 W, 28 kHz) settled the cells by collapsing their gas vesicles (confirmed by electron microscopy). Sonicated cells did not regrow under dim or dark "lake bottom" conditions.
- **Effective?** Yes in the lab. Sinking cells to dark water is the idea behind it.
- **Teen-reproducible?** **Yes.** Sonicate a bloom sample for seconds, then compare how fast it settles in graduated cylinders.
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 10. Sinking continues after the gas vesicles recover (Rodriguez-Molares, Dickson, Hobson, Howard, Zander & Burch, 2014) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ultsonch.2014.01.027 (open access)
- **What they did:** proposed a repeatable sedimentation index and tracked long-term settling of *M. aeruginosa* after sonication.
- **Environment:** not assessed.
- **Results:** sinking continued after the gas vesicles had fully regrown, though more slowly.
- **Effective?** Partly (mechanism only).
- **Teen-reproducible?** **Yes.** A settling-column index is a ready-made measurement method.
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 11. Ultrasound plus water jets in Lake Senba, Japan (Nakano, Lee & Matsumura, 2001) [peer-reviewed; field]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1021/es010711c
- **What they did:** 10 units, each combining ultrasound with a jet circulator, added to an existing flushing scheme and monitored for 2 years.
- **Environment:** COD and phosphorus improved.
- **Results:** blooms were controlled only "when coupled with sufficient inflow water supply". The system mainly cut suspended solids and improved transparency, and did less for chlorophyll-a.
- **Effective?** Partly. Flushing probably did most of the work.
- **Teen-reproducible?** No.
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 12. Ultrasound plus pumps in a 9,000 m³ Korean pond (Ahn, Joung, Choi, Kim, Jang & Oh, 2007) [peer-reviewed; field]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1080/09593332808618800
- **What they did:** an ultrasound device with pumps in one pond, compared against a neighbouring untreated pond for one season.
- **Environment:** the share of green algae rose.
- **Results:** chlorophyll-a was 61% and total algae 53% of the control pond's. **Cyanobacteria rebounded within days of switching off.** There was only one control pond, in a rainy year.
- **Effective?** Partly: one positive, unreplicated trial.
- **Teen-reproducible?** No.
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 13. Pilot and field tests: ultrasound fades within 0.5 m (Park, Son & Lee, 2019) [peer-reviewed; pilot/field]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ultsonch.2019.03.007 (open access)
- **What they did:** 36-175 kHz at <650 W in 100-750 L pilots and a 4 m³ circulating pond, for 10-60 min.
- **Environment:** 36 kHz raised microcystin through cell damage.
- **Results:** sound pressure fell about 80% over 0.5 m. Algae fell 50-90% in 60 min in the 4 m³ pond. Distance from the source mattered most.
- **Effective?** Partly: only close to the unit.
- **Teen-reproducible?** Partly. A student can measure the fall-off with distance in a tub using cell counts.
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 14. Two short low-power pulses remove 87.6% (Huang, Wu, Sheng, Wu, Li & Wang, 2020) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.3390/w12092431 (open access)
- **What they did:** lab tests at 20-90 kHz, 0.0005-0.1 W/mL and 0.5-10 min, then a two-cycle protocol.
- **Environment:** not assessed. At low doses, damaged cells repaired themselves.
- **Results:** two low-dose cycles removed 87.6% of cyanobacteria with the best energy efficiency.
- **Effective?** Yes in the lab.
- **Teen-reproducible?** **Yes.** One pulse against two pulses in a cleaner is a direct copy.
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 15. 200 kHz on dense bloom water from a eutrophic lake (Tekile, Kim & Lee, 2017) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.3390/w9120915 (open access)
- **What they did:** mixed lake algae at "caution" (17.5 µg/L) and "outbreak" (1450 µg/L) chlorophyll-a levels, sonicated for 10-20 min.
- **Environment:** dissolved phosphorus rose in the dense suspension.
- **Results:** chlorophyll-a removal was 0.90 after a day for the outbreak level and 0.64 for the caution level. The treated outbreak water was still at 147 µg/L, "still in the outbreak level range".
- **Effective?** Partly: not enough for dense blooms.
- **Teen-reproducible?** Partly (a 200 kHz source is not standard).
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 16. Faster collapse of sonicated blooms means more methane (Xu, Tang, Liang, Chen & Dai, 2023) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envres.2023.116422
- **What they did:** a 40-day microcosm; *M. aeruginosa* sonicated at 29.4 kHz for 15 min, then left to decay.
- **Environment:** leaked organic matter and microcystins, anaerobic conditions, and **1.43 times more CH4**.
- **Results:** 33.49% of cells were removed.
- **Effective?** Partly, with hidden costs.
- **Teen-reproducible?** Partly. The oxygen-drop part is doable with a DO probe.
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 17. Independent field test: 18 months in an Australian reservoir (Vaughan et al., 2023) [peer-reviewed; field]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.3390/toxins15030186 (PMC10051689)
- **What they did:** one solar sonicator (2 transducers, 100 W, 20-60 kHz) in a 350-400 ML drinking-water reservoir from Dec 2020 to May 2022, compared with 3 years of earlier data and 3 other reservoirs.
- **Environment:** eukaryotic algal cell counts rose significantly after installation.
- **Results:** cyanobacterial biovolumes and counts did not change significantly, except for lower bloom-season counts at the treatment-plant intake. There was "no strong evidence that sonication significantly reduced cyanobacteria". A two-week outage had no detectable effect.
- **Effective?** No.
- **Teen-reproducible?** No.
- **Read:** full text, via a tool summary of the PMC article.

### 18. Five reservoirs: sound field and water quality (Tischer, Murphy, Weaver, Crafton-Nelson & Weavers, 2025) [peer-reviewed; field]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvman.2025.125057 (open access)
- **What they did:** mapped commercial ultrasound fields and water quality at 5 reservoirs, against control reservoirs.
- **Environment:** no differences found.
- **Results:** the signal fades exponentially, about 10 times faster than in pure water. Chlorophyll-a and phycocyanin did not vary with distance from the units or between ultrasound and control reservoirs. Field pressures are too low to collapse gas vesicles, and the frequencies too low to make them resonate.
- **Effective?** No.
- **Teen-reproducible?** No.
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 19. Low-power ultrasound does not collapse gas vesicles (Bohrerova et al., 2023) [peer-reviewed; field]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1002/aws2.1346 (open access)
- **What they did:** *Serratia* and bloom cyanobacteria (*Microcystis*, *Aphanizomenon*) in sound-transparent containers in front of a field unit, for up to 14 days.
- **Environment:** not applicable.
- **Results:** no change in gas vesicles, viability or phycocyanin. The top pressure, 3.5 kPa at 1 m, was "over 100× lower" than the pressure needed to collapse gas vesicles.
- **Effective?** No: the claimed mechanism is physically unlikely at field power.
- **Teen-reproducible?** No (needs a hydrophone).
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 20. Three-month pilot in a Finnish lake (Härkönen et al., 2026) [peer-reviewed; field]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1080/20442041.2026.2732081
- **What they did:** a 17 W, 24-57 kHz device in deep, stratifying Lake Ahvenlampi for 3 months, with nearby Lake Hiidenlampi as the control.
- **Environment:** no change in phytoplankton or zooplankton communities, and no visible damage to cells.
- **Results:** "neither prevented the development of cyanobacterial blooms". Seasonal succession tracked the control lake.
- **Effective?** No.
- **Teen-reproducible?** No.
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 21. Non-target organisms: fish, zooplankton and amphipods (Getchell et al., 2022; Klemenčič & Krivograd Klemenčič, 2021; Techer & Banas, 2018) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1080/10402381.2022.2077865 · https://doi.org/10.15292/acta.hydro.2021.01 · https://doi.org/10.1007/s11356-017-0980-4 · review https://doi.org/10.14720/abs.64.1.15758
- **What they did:** Getchell: Oneida Lake (NY) field transects and hydroacoustics, plus 2-week lab exposure of 7 fish species and tadpoles. Klemenčič: *D. magna* for 48 h in a 200 L aquarium. Techer: *Gammarus roeseli* in 8 L tanks under a 15 W, 23/46 kHz device.
- **Environment:** no fish avoidance and no tissue damage. 87% of adult and 82% of juvenile *Daphnia* stayed mobile after 48 h. *Gammarus* reached 50% mortality in 2 h 40 min, but with 6.5 °C of heating; with cooling it took 29 h.
- **Results:** the review concludes that low-power devices look safe for fish, that zooplankton results conflict, and that marine use is a concern for marine mammals and shad or herring (Alosinae).
- **Effective?** Not applicable (safety).
- **Teen-reproducible?** **Yes.** Small tanks make ultrasound heat the water, a confound the student must control with a water bath.
- **Read:** abstract only (all four).

## B. Bubbling, aeration and mixing

### 22. Macrobubbles against *Margalefidinium polykrikoides* (Sung & Gobler, 2026) [peer-reviewed] (most relevant to CT)
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvman.2026.129015 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41764922/
- **What they did:** tested orbital shaking, nanobubbles (with and without ozone) and macrobubbles on wild and cultured *M. polykrikoides*, the fish-killing dinoflagellate of Long Island's bays. Lab and bloom-water incubations, including fish bioassays.
- **Environment:** in bloom water, macrobubbles favoured co-occurring *Prorocentrum cordatum*, a shift in community composition.
- **Results:** macrobubbles repressed cell density by >60%, against <20% for nanobubbles. They lowered photosynthetic efficiency and gave **100% fish survival where unbubbled controls had 100% mortality**.
- **Effective?** Yes at bench scale. Not field-tested.
- **Teen-reproducible?** **Yes, the top pick.** Aquarium pump, coarse air outlet against fine airstone against still, in tall tubes. Use a non-toxic dinoflagellate culture and skip the fish.
- **Read:** abstract only (via Europe PMC; bubble sizes and flow rates not seen).

### 23. Artificial mixing to control cyanobacterial blooms: review (Visser, Ibelings, Bormans & Huisman, 2016) [peer-reviewed review]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10452-015-9537-0 (open access)
- **What they did:** reviewed lake and reservoir mixing studies and models.
- **Environment:** mixing raises oxygen, warms deep water and cools the surface. Total chlorophyll per m² often **rises**, from nutrients stirred up from depth.
- **Results:** a shift from cyanobacteria to greens and diatoms happens only if mixing is strong enough to entrain the cyanobacteria, deep enough to limit light, and spread evenly across the lake.
- **Effective?** Partly: the conditions for success are strict.
- **Teen-reproducible?** Partly. A tall-column demo can test the "entrain against float" mechanism.
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 24. Lake Nieuwe Meer: 2 and 7 years of mixing (Visser et al., 1996; Jungo, Visser, Stroom & Mur, 2001; Huisman et al., 2004) [peer-reviewed; whole lake]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1365-2427.1996.00093.x · https://doi.org/10.2166/ws.2001.0003 · https://doi.org/10.1890/03-0763
- **What they did:** bubble plumes from pipes on the lake bed (85 kW compressor) in a 1.3 km², 30 m deep hypertrophic lake, every summer since 1993.
- **Environment:** oxygen stayed above 5 mg/L at every depth, and the surface ran about 2 °C cooler. Total phosphorus and nitrogen did not change, and neither did transparency.
- **Results:** *Microcystis* biomass was up to 20 times lower, with no scums. Running the mixing intermittently cut energy use by 75% and still prevented stratification. The 2004 model and experiment show that high turbulent diffusivity favours sinking diatoms and greens over buoyant *Microcystis*.
- **Effective?** Yes, in a deep lake, for as long as it runs.
- **Teen-reproducible?** Partly (a column demonstration only).
- **Read:** abstract only (all three).

### 25. Global review: 138 destratification systems in 114 reservoirs (Chaaya, Miller, Gordos, Tamburic & Felder, 2025) [peer-reviewed review]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scitotenv.2025.178738 (open access)
- **What they did:** compared bubble plumes with mechanical mixers.
- **Environment:** successful destratification raised oxygen and lowered manganese and iron.
- **Results:** bubble plumes beat mechanical mixers. Results for cyanobacteria were mixed; control correlated with successful destratification in deeper reservoirs. Destratification failed below an air flow of about **0.005 L/s per ML**.
- **Effective?** Partly.
- **Teen-reproducible?** Partly. The air-flow-per-volume rule gives a scaling number to test in columns.
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 26. Chaffey Reservoir: the bubble plume that failed (Sherman, Whittington & Oliver, 2000) [peer-reviewed report]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.26181/22275796
- **What they did:** a bubble-plume destratifier in an Australian reservoir with *Anabaena* blooms.
- **Environment:** internal phosphorus load from sediments fell by over 80%.
- **Results:** the plume did not deepen the surface mixed layer. Temperature differences "as small as 0.04 °C" kept buoyant *Anabaena* near the surface, and the external phosphorus load matched the internal one.
- **Effective?** No for blooms; yes for sediment phosphorus.
- **Teen-reproducible?** No.
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 27. Surface mixers help only within 20 m (Slavin et al., 2022) [peer-reviewed; field]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1029/2021wr030068 (open access)
- **What they did:** top-down surface mixers in a 6.6 m deep, eutrophic UK drinking-water reservoir.
- **Environment:** some local sediment was stirred up. More than 90% of the reservoir stayed subject to daily stratification and anoxia.
- **Results:** effective within a 20 m radius. Low-light *Planktothrix* was **highest near the mixers**.
- **Effective?** No at reservoir scale.
- **Teen-reproducible?** No.
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 28. SolarBee circulators in St. Albans Bay, Lake Champlain (Smeltzer, Telep, Shambaugh & Stangel, 2008) [agency report, Vermont ANR]
- **Link:** https://anrweb.vt.gov/PubDocs/DEC/WSMD/lakes/docs/lp_solarbeereport.pdf
- **What they did:** 3 SolarBee units, May-August 2007, with a presumed 35-acre treatment zone each and a spatial-control design.
- **Environment:** not applicable.
- **Results:** no significant reduction in chlorophyll-a, no gain in Secchi depth, no change in the vertical chlorophyll profile or in cyanobacterial dominance. The goal of a 100-acre clear zone "was not achieved".
- **Effective?** No. This is a New England agency test of a commercial circulator.
- **Teen-reproducible?** No.
- **Read:** abstract page of the report.

### 29. Shallow or undersized systems: aerators, fountains and urban ponds (Oberholster et al., 2006; Zębek, 2014; Hao et al., 2021) [peer-reviewed; field]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1440-1770.2006.00297.x · https://doi.org/10.2478/limre-2014-0006 · https://doi.org/10.3390/w13233360 (open access)
- **What they did:** Sheldon Lake (Colorado), mixed; a fountain aerator in Jeziorak Mały (Poland); surface aerators in two Japanese urban ponds.
- **Environment:** in one pond, oxygen rose from 1.8 to 8.1 mg/L and total phosphorus fell from 1.6 to 0.4 mg/L.
- **Results:** in Sheldon Lake the mixing rate was "insufficient", and toxic *Microcystis* stayed dominant. The fountain "does not inhibit the growth of cyanobacteria". In the Japanese pond, chlorophyll-a rose from 29 to 51 µg/L and transparency fell from 81 to 27 cm after aeration, although the second pond had no blooms.
- **Effective?** Mostly no for blooms; yes for oxygen.
- **Teen-reproducible?** No.
- **Read:** abstract only (all three).

### 30. Pulsed ("surged") aeration beats continuous aeration (Lindenschmidt, 1999; Steinberg & Zimmermann, 1988) [peer-reviewed; field]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1002/iroh.199900026 · https://doi.org/10.1080/09593338809384575
- **What they did:** Tegeler See, Berlin, compared 5 years of continuous, no and surge operation of 15 aerators. Separately, intermittent destratification in a small lake.
- **Environment:** needs an aerobic sediment-water interface.
- **Results:** surged aeration suppressed *Microcystis*. With prolonged aeration or prolonged stratification, *Microcystis* adapts, so "little effect". Intermittent destratification removed cyanobacteria "very fast".
- **Effective?** Partly (older, correlational studies).
- **Teen-reproducible?** **Yes.** Continuous against pulsed bubbling on a plug-in timer is a neat variant of #22.
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 31. Bubble plumes: removal rate follows turbulence (Pacheco & Lima Neto, 2017) [peer-reviewed; field]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1061/(ASCE)EE.1943-7870.0001289
- **What they did:** aeration tests in a hypereutrophic shallow Brazilian lake (40-80 µg/L chlorophyll-a), sampling inside and outside the plumes.
- **Environment:** pheophytin rose, so cells were dying.
- **Results:** first-order decay of chlorophyll-a and cyanobacteria inside the plumes, with a rate that scales with turbulent dissipation.
- **Effective?** Partly: local and short-term.
- **Teen-reproducible?** **Yes.** Fit first-order decay curves to column data at 2-3 air flow rates.
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 32. Aeration shifts *Microcystis* to diatoms, depending on biomass (Wang et al., 2022; 2024) [peer-reviewed; greenhouse]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-25017-4 · https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-53920-5 (open access)
- **What they did:** continuous aeration of greenhouse *Microcystis* blooms (chlorophyll-a from 32.5 to 14,250 µg/L), up to 30 days.
- **Environment:** the diatom *Nitzschia palea* grew attached to *Microcystis* colonies.
- **Results:** medium biomass shifted to greens, and high biomass shifted to *Nitzschia* dominance (up to 9.41 mg/L). The outcome depends on starting biomass.
- **Effective?** Partly: the community changes, but the biomass does not disappear.
- **Teen-reproducible?** **Yes.** Buckets, an air pump and a microscope.
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 33. Turbulence can help as well as hurt (Feng et al., 2026; Strohm et al., 2024) [peer-reviewed; lab]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.hal.2026.103125 · https://doi.org/10.1016/j.hal.2024.102666
- **What they did:** Feng: *Microcystis* colonies across a range of turbulence. Strohm (with Gobler): 3 marine *Dinophysis* species (4 strains) under low or high turbulence (ε = 10⁻² m²/s³).
- **Environment:** high turbulence **raised** intracellular toxin in *D. acuminata* and caused toxin leakage in *D. ovum*.
- **Results:** low turbulence **increased** *Microcystis* growth by 241.1%; only extreme turbulence inhibited it. High turbulence slowed *D. acuminata* and *D. ovum* but **stimulated** *D. caudata*.
- **Effective?** Partly. Weak mixing can make things worse, which is a key caveat for any bubbling project.
- **Teen-reproducible?** Partly. An orbital shaker or stir-plate speeds are a direct analogue.
- **Read:** abstract only.

## C. Hypolimnetic oxygenation and nanobubbles

### 34. Hypolimnetic oxygenation reviews (Beutel & Horne, 1999; Preece, Moore, Skinner, Child & Dent, 2019) [peer-reviewed reviews]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1080/07438149909354124 · https://doi.org/10.1080/10402381.2019.1580325
- **What they did:** reviewed deep oxygen injection and Speece cones (Swiss lakes, Amisk Lake, Newman Lake, Camanche) across at least 30 systems.
- **Environment:** keeps the thermal layering. Improves cold-water fish and zooplankton habitat, and lowers manganese, iron and mercury.
- **Results:** lower internal nutrient loading and "reduce cyanobacteria blooms", but usually combined with other measures, "difficult to discern the exact impacts".
- **Effective?** Partly: good for oxygen and phosphorus, indirect for blooms.
- **Teen-reproducible?** No.
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 35. Camanche Reservoir: 12 years of oxygenation alone (Horne & Beutel, 2019) [peer-reviewed; field]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1080/10402381.2019.1648613 (open access)
- **What they did:** a hypolimnetic oxygenation system in a 514 million m³ California reservoir, with no other measures. Data cover 1988-2005.
- **Environment:** zooplankton was unchanged, the diatom *Fragilaria* fell by more than 90%, and the clean-water indicator *Merismopedia* appeared.
- **Results:** growing-season chlorophyll-a fell 79% (14.6 to 3.1 µg/L) and Secchi depth rose from 0.47 to 4.9 m. *Aphanizomenon* and *Dolichospermum* fell more than 95%.
- **Effective?** Yes: the strongest long-term field result in this family. It works where internal loading from anoxic sediments drives the blooms.
- **Teen-reproducible?** No.
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 36. Newman Lake Speece cone (Moore, Cross, Beutel & Dent, 2012) [peer-reviewed; field]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1080/07438141.2012.738463
- **What they did:** downflow bubble-contact oxygenation, the first lake use of a Speece cone, from 1992.
- **Environment:** oxic sediment-water interface.
- **Results:** the anoxic factor fell from 30-60 d to <10 d. Running below full capacity gave a disproportionately poor result.
- **Effective?** Yes for anoxia. Effects on blooms were not reported in the abstract.
- **Teen-reproducible?** No.
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 37. Ozone nanobubbles against oxygen-only nanobubbles, Grand Lake St Marys, Ohio (Chaffin et al., 2024) [peer-reviewed; mesocosm and field]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvman.2024.123406 (open access)
- **What they did:** 2,000 L mesocosms and two 4-week trials in a ~47 million L embayment, against a *Planktothrix* bloom.
- **Environment:** ozone oxidised dissolved organic carbon at the outlet, but not 3 m away.
- **Results:** ozone cut chlorophyll-a and phycocyanin 98-99% in the mesocosms. **Oxygen-only nanobubbles had no effect.** The embayment trial also had no effect.
- **Effective?** No for the chemical-free (oxygen) version. The ozone version is a chemical oxidant.
- **Teen-reproducible?** No (nanobubble generators; ozone is hazardous).
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 38. Oxygen nanobubbles for lakes: where are we? (Waters et al., 2022; Ali, Yang & Pan, 2023) [peer-reviewed reviews]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.3390/w14131989 (open access) · https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvman.2023.117281
- **What they did:** reviewed oxygen nanobubbles, mostly loaded onto clay or "modified local soils" and sunk to sediments ("floc, lock and oxygenate").
- **Environment:** relies on added clay or soil carriers, the same objection the counselor raised about clay. Possible smothering of bottom-dwelling organisms.
- **Results:** "mostly short-term, small-scale core incubations" in the lab. Scale-up, longevity and cost are unresolved.
- **Effective?** Unproven.
- **Teen-reproducible?** No.
- **Read:** abstract only.

## D. Flushing, withdrawal, light limitation and harvesting

### 39. Flushing and withdrawal in the field (Hosper, 1985; Lehman, 2014; Elliott et al., 2009) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.2166/wst.1985.0177 · https://doi.org/10.1080/10402381.2013.872739 (open access) · https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2486.2009.01998.x
- **What they did:** Lake Veluwe (NL) flushed in winter with low-phosphorus water, alongside phosphorus reduction. Ford Lake (Michigan) with selective hypolimnetic withdrawal, 2008-2012. Esthwaite Water (UK) with the PROTECH model at half to double the flushing rate.
- **Environment:** needs spare water and control of flows.
- **Results:** Veluwe chlorophyll-a fell 50-60%, but *Oscillatoria* biovolume fell only about 25%. At Ford Lake, withdrawal gave no nuisance blooms in 2008-11; in the 2012 drought it could not run and *Aphanizomenon* bloomed. The model shows cyanobacteria rising as flushing slows and water warms, but nutrients limit the effect.
- **Effective?** Yes where water is available. It fails in droughts.
- **Teen-reproducible?** Partly. A chemostat-style "dilution rate" experiment with a peristaltic or aquarium pump is possible.
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 40. Flushing and salinity control *Alexandrium* in an Osaka Bay estuary (Yamamoto et al., 2013) [peer-reviewed; marine or brackish]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10872-013-0203-7
- **What they did:** field study of a river and estuary.
- **Environment:** not available.
- **Results:** no abstract available. The title claims flushing rate and salinity may control this toxic dinoflagellate.
- **Effective?** Unknown (not verified).
- **Teen-reproducible?** No.
- **Read:** title only. Do not cite without reading.

### 41. Darkness: *Microcystis* dies back but regrows, and marine algae survive (Furusato, Asaeda & Fukuwatari, 2001; Walter, Peters & van Beusekom, 2017) [peer-reviewed; lab]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.2965/jswe.24.27 (open access) · https://doi.org/10.1007/s10452-017-9638-z (open access)
- **What they did:** *Microcystis*, *Scenedesmus* and *Melosira* in 5-20 days of darkness, then re-lit. The marine diatom *Thalassiosira weissflogii* and *Rhodomonas* under dark and short-light cycles.
- **Environment:** not applicable.
- **Results:** after 20 days dark, *Microcystis* was down to 1% of its starting cells while the green alga and diatom kept their biomass. After re-lighting, **all three regrew exponentially to maximum**. The marine species survived without significant cell loss.
- **Effective?** Partly. Darkness shifts competition but does not eliminate the alga.
- **Teen-reproducible?** **Yes, one of the top 3.** Foil-wrapped jars for 0/5/10/20 days, then re-lighting.
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 42. Shading and floating solar panels (Wang et al., 2023; Exley et al., 2022; Sandrini et al., 2025) [peer-reviewed]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10452-023-10050-w · https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvman.2022.116410 · https://doi.org/10.3390/w17081178 (open access)
- **What they did:** Wang: field mesocosms at 100, 85 and 65% of light in spring. Exley: model of 0-100% floating-solar cover on a UK reservoir. Sandrini: a sun-tracking floating array covering 24% of a shallow Dutch drinking-water reservoir.
- **Environment:** in Sandrini, stoneworts (*Chara*) and macrophytes largely disappeared, **benthic cyanobacteria "developed massively"** under the panels, and *Dreissena* mussels fouled the floats.
- **Results:** Wang: less light brought an **earlier** cyanobacteria peak, and greens and diatoms declined. Exley: total biomass fell with cover, and placing panels over faster-flowing water gave more reduction for less cover.
- **Effective?** Mixed: shading can favour cyanobacteria.
- **Teen-reproducible?** **Yes.** Shade-cloth jars at 3 light levels, with a mixed culture.
- **Read:** abstract only (Wang via a search-result summary of the abstract).

### 43. Harvesting by net compared with peroxide (Fan, Shi, Zhang, Liu & Chen, 2019) [peer-reviewed; in-lake mesocosm]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scitotenv.2019.133721
- **What they did:** a 15-day mesocosm in a lake. *Microcystis* colonies filtered out with a 30 µm net at 30, 60 and 90% intensity, compared with 10 mg/L H2O2.
- **Environment:** diversity rose, but non-*Microcystis* (filamentous) cyanobacteria gained share.
- **Results:** 30 and 60% harvests **stimulated** regrowth and lost their effect by day 6. A 90% harvest held biomass low for 15 days. Colony size recovered quickly. Peroxide beat harvesting as a one-time fix.
- **Effective?** Partly. Harvesting has to be heavy and repeated.
- **Teen-reproducible?** **Yes.** A plankton net and buckets; a clean retrieval story that answers the counselor's objection.
- **Read:** abstract only.

### 44. Agency guidance on physical methods (US EPA; NEIWPCC 2015; ITRC HCB-1; USACE HABITATS) [agency reports]
- **Link:** https://www.epa.gov/habs/control-measures-cyanobacterial-habs-surface-water · https://www.neiwpcc.org/neiwpcc_docs/NEIWPCC_HABControlMethodsSynopses_June2015.pdf · https://hcb-1.itrcweb.org/skimming-and-harvesting/ · https://hcb-1.itrcweb.org/ultrasound/ · https://www.erdc.usace.army.mil/Portals/55/CERL/HABITATS/HABITATS%20Pilot%20Reaserch%20Study-%20ERDC%20TR-20-1.pdf
- **What they did:** EPA: a table of effectiveness and limits. NEIWPCC (the New England states): circulation pages. ITRC: fact sheets. USACE HABITATS: a floating weir skimmer with booms at Florida lakes.
- **Environment:** EPA says hydrologic changes have likely "unintended consequences for other aquatic organisms". NEIWPCC says an oversized mixer can re-release sediment nutrients, and an undersized one can "perversely" favour cyanobacteria.
- **Results:** EPA says one ultrasound device "can cover up to 8 acres", but "further research ... required". Skimming is useful only once scums have formed. NEIWPCC gives destratification as more successful at mean depth >15 m, and SolarBee costs of $10-20k per device per summer, or $150k for three. ITRC notes that one 2014 ultrasound buoy deployment also used copper.
- **Effective?** These describe practice rather than test it.
- **Teen-reproducible?** Not applicable.
- **Read:** EPA page (tool summary); NEIWPCC circulation pages (full); ITRC ultrasound (search summary only, page returned 403); HABITATS (search summary only, 403).

### 45. Critical overviews of end-of-pipe measures (Lürling, Waajen & de Senerpont Domis, 2016; Lürling & Mucci, 2020; Kibuye, Zamyadi & Wert, 2021; Anderson et al., 2025) [peer-reviewed reviews]
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10452-015-9563-y · https://doi.org/10.1007/s10750-020-04297-9 · https://doi.org/10.1016/j.hal.2021.102119 · https://doi.org/10.1016/j.hal.2025.102989 (all open access)
- **What they did:** Dutch peer-reviewed and grey-literature survey; drinking-water source review; review of marine HAB control.
- **Environment:** see individual entries.
- **Results:** Lürling & Mucci found "no or limited effects" for air-bubble screens, surface mixers and low-energy ultrasound. Lürling et al. said mixing non-stratifying waters and ultrasound should not be considered "Columbus's egg". Kibuye found that mixing and hypolimnetic oxygenation work when properly sized, are undone by poor sediment chemistry (low Fe/P) and outside nutrient inputs, and that sonication findings are "contradictory". Anderson et al. call marine HAB control "one of the least developed areas of HAB science".
- **Effective?** Consensus: physical measures relieve symptoms; they do not cure blooms.
- **Teen-reproducible?** Not applicable.
- **Read:** abstract only.
