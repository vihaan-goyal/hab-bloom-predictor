## (c) What you are signing — a one-page briefing

**Vihaan Goyal, junior, Westhill High School. Connecticut Science and
Engineering Fair (ISEF-affiliated) and the Stockholm Junior Water Prize.**

The computational half of the project is done: a model that forecasts
chlorophyll-a exceedances at Long Island Sound monitoring stations from thirty
years of public state data. The bench half, October to December, tests whether
a clay dose can clear algae cells from Sound water and whether a magnet can
take the clay back out afterwards. That is the part needing your signature.

### The four forms, and what each one commits you to

| Form | Who signs | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Form 1 — Checklist for Adult Sponsor** | Adult Sponsor | You confirm you have read the research plan, that we have discussed the risks together, and you name the Designated Supervisor. One page of tick boxes. |
| **Form 1A — Student Checklist and Research Plan** | me, with your approval | I write it and attach the plan. You approve the plan and the date I am allowed to start. |
| **Form 3 — Risk Assessment** | Designated Supervisor and Adult Sponsor | Lists the hazardous materials and the controls below, and commits the Designated Supervisor to being in the room for the hands-on sessions. |
| **Form 1B — Approval Form** | me, my parent, Adult Sponsor | Signed before the first experiment. It says the project follows the fair's rules. |

### Why Friday, September 25

Fair rules allow no hands-on work before all four are signed. The first build
day is Monday, September 28. A live diatom culture ships on a fixed date in
early October and everything after it — the October chemistry checks, three
jar-test batches in November, three tank runs in December — is dated off that
week. A slip of one week moves the whole calendar into a February deadline
that does not move.

### What the bench work physically is

In a classroom, after school, about **twelve supervised sessions between
October and December**, most of them 1.5 to 3 hours.

- A **10 gallon glass aquarium** holding 20 litres of Long Island Sound
  seawater, collected by bucket at Milford or Stratford and filtered.
- A dry **clay blend** — kaolin, iron-oxide pigment and PAC powder — stirred in
  at 0.2 grams per litre with a cordless drill, then a slow paddle.
- A **magnet rake**: twenty N52 magnet blocks sealed inside capped acrylic
  tubes on a wooden frame, pushed by hand along the tank floor to lift the
  settled clay out.
- **Three weekly jar tests** in 2 litre glass jars on a small 12 V motor rig,
  plus three full tank runs.
- The organism is **CCMP1332 *Skeletonema marinoi***, a non-toxic marine diatom
  originally isolated off Milford, bought from a standard culture collection.

### The real hazards and the controls (from the written protocol, section 11)

- **PAC powder is an acidic aluminium coagulant.** Gloves, goggles and a dust
  mask; powders weighed in the fume hood; aluminium floc kept as lab solid
  waste; effluent bleached, diluted and disposed of per school drain policy.
  One clay paste sits at pH 3–4 and is handled with gloves.
- **N52 magnets pinch and shatter.** Each block pulls about 25 kg to steel and
  neighbouring stacks repel at roughly 12 kgf. They are assembled one at a time
  in the locked frame, kept away from phones and memory cards, and stay sealed
  in their tubes during a run.
- **Electrical: everything touching water runs at 12 V behind a GFCI outlet.**
  The drill and the hot plate sit on a separate bench.
- **Acid for sample preservation is handled only by the partner lab or by you.**
  I do not open it.
- **The cultures are non-toxic** and are bleached before disposal.
- Any work after normal hours happens only under a separate written agreement
  signed by me, my parent, you and the principal, with you present.
- One optional chemistry step (iron chlorides with 2 M sodium hydroxide) is
  **not** in the plan and would happen only if you separately agreed to it.

### What is not involved

No human subjects. No vertebrate animals. No pathogens, and no human or animal
tissue. No controlled substances. No recombinant DNA. Nothing here needs
Scientific Review Committee pre-approval — Form 3 plus the signatures is the
whole requirement, and the forms are uploaded at fair registration.

### What it actually costs you

- **Once:** about twenty to thirty minutes to read the research plan and this
  page, and sign four forms.
- **October to December:** being in the room for roughly twelve sessions, most
  1.5 to 3 hours, after school. I set up and clean up.
- **A few of those sessions** have a final reading five hours after the start,
  in the evening. Those only happen under the separate after-hours agreement
  above, and only if you are willing.
- **Nothing else.** I do not need you to design the experiment, run any of it,
  or help with the analysis. I need someone accountable in the room and a name
  on the forms.
