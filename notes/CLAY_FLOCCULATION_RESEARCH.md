# Modified-clay flocculation for bloom mitigation: research brief for a forecast-plus-dose project

Written 2026-09-15 from a web research pass (~30 fetches; research agent, this session). Companion to notes/AERATION_RESEARCH.md, which concluded aeration cannot prevent blooms. Facts carry URLs (Sources); items marked *inferred* are ours. Paywalled abstracts (ScienceDirect, Springer) mostly returned 403; numbers come from open PMC copies, the WHOI-hosted Yu et al. 2017 review PDF, NOAA/WHOI project pages, and NCBI E-utilities.

## 0. The founding US paper

Sengco & Anderson 2004, J. Eukaryotic Microbiology, "Controlling harmful algal blooms through clay flocculation" (PubMed 15134251; Woods Hole). Japan and Korea already used kaolinite, montmorillonite and yellow loess in the field to protect fish farms from Cochlodinium. US lab tests: >80% cell removal against Karenia brevis, Heterosigma akashiwo, Pfiesteria piscicida and Aureococcus anophagefferens (the Long Island brown-tide organism). PAC raised removal. Removal depends on cell concentration, salinity and mixing; cells die in the settled layer. Mesocosm, enclosure and flume work on water quality, bottom erodibility and benthic fauna. Called "promising" in 2004; still not permitted in the US in 2026.

## 1. Mechanism and recipes

- Phytoplankton are negatively charged (zeta: Skeletonema costatum -7.6 mV, Chaetoceros simplex -2.5, Prorocentrum minimum -12.4, Karenia brevis -5.8, Alexandrium tamarense -4.5; Yu et al. 2017 Table 1). Natural kaolinite is also negative (-17.6 mV at pH 7). Adsorbing poly-aluminum chloride (PAC) flips the clay positive (+10.5 to +11.3 mV), adding charge neutralisation, bridging and enmeshment. Modified clay is "dozens to hundreds of times" more efficient: field dose falls from 110-400 t/km2 (Japan) or 384 t/km2 (Korean loess) to 4-10 t/km2.
- Recipes stated in the literature: IOCAS PAC-clay = kaolinite:PAC 5:1 by mass, stirred, aged ~24 h, filter-pressed, dried (PMC8583585); Fangchenggang used Beihai kaolin 5:1 as a 40 g/L stock in filtered seawater. 2025 Malaysian A. minutum study: 2.5 g kaolinite + 2.5 g PAC (1:1) in 75 mL filtered seawater, vortexed, pipetted onto the surface (PMC12390390). Other modifiers: chitosan (>90% Microcystis at 0.011 g/L total), cationic starch (86% in 30 min), PDMDAAC (clay:modifier 200:1-200:5 at 0.2 g/L), Bacillus-fermented kaolin.
- Particle size: Korea <50 um; China D90 <40 um. "MC I / MC II" are IOCAS product generations, no open recipe for the difference. US patents 10,822,258 (spray device) and 10,981,813 (slurry concentrations, a monitoring-keyed dose table) give no clay formulation.

## 2. Dose-response by species

| Species | Clay and dose | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Karenia brevis | MCII 0.2 g/L | 91% cells at 5 h; 50% less waterborne brevetoxin | PMC12656167 |
| Alexandrium minutum | kaolinite+PAC 1:1, 0.05-0.3 g/L | 100% within 2 h; natural kaolinite <85% | PMC12390390 |
| A. pacificum | modified clay 0.2-1 g/L | ~75% at 0.2 -> ~99% at 1 g/L | PMC12567844 |
| Prorocentrum donghaiense | PDMDAAC clay 0.2 g/L | >95% within hours | Frontiers 2022 |
| P. micans, S. costatum, A. tamarense | IOCAS MC 0.25-0.4 g/L | rapid decline, no regrowth 30-80 d | Yu 2017 Fig. 7 |
| Cochlodinium | Korean loess ~400 g/m2 | "up to 95%" (press) | Yu 2017; Anderson Lab |
| Heterosigma akashiwo | MCI 0.2 g/L, 24 h | 60% (hardest species: hydrophobic) | PMC11922882 |
| Aureococcus | MC 0.5 g/L vs natural 2 g/L | 71% vs 72% at 3 h; MC residuals 1.1% at 360 h vs regrowth to 80% for natural | PMC8583585 |
| Phaeocystis globosa | MC 0.1 g/L | 90% biomass at 3 h | IJERPH 2021 |

Modifiers of efficiency: low-motility chain diatoms (Skeletonema, Pseudo-nitzschia) "readily form flocs and can be easily removed"; single-celled flagellates (P. minimum, Phaeocystis) form limited flocs without an oxidant (Yu 2017 s4.3). Removal falls with initial cell density and with accumulated algal organic matter; residual cells at 20-30% of a dense bloom are "still a blooming level". Clay made up in seawater needs ~3x the dose of clay made up in fresh water (0.6 vs 0.2 g/L for 80%, PubMed 26763322). Most of the effect within 2-5 h; 24 h for slow species. For S. costatum the clay also removed TN 44-72%, TP 93%, Si 44-64%. *Inferred:* LIS summer diatom blooms are the most favourable case; Prorocentrum less so.

## 3. Field deployments

- South Korea: yellow loess since 1996 against Cochlodinium fish kills; up to 225,000 t/yr at peak; ~60,000 t at ~400 g/m2 in one region; seven dedicated spray vessels. Losses $60M (1995), $18.6M (2003), $10.4M (2007).
- China (IOCAS): Xuanwu Lake 2005 (Microcystis 2.7e7 -> 6e3 cells/mL over 10 d); Qingdao Olympic sailing 2008, 86 km2 bloom, ~360 t in 40 h, abundance down 1-2 orders next day; Qinhuangdao 2016, ~100 t "isolation belt"; Fangchenggang nuclear plant 2015-16, 210 t in 28 threshold-triggered applications after Phaeocystis tripped a reactor; >20 coastal regions treated; standard national red-tide method since 2014.
- Chile: delegation to IOCAS after the 2016 salmon crisis; >30 t applied 2018 (press only).
- USA: Sengco/Anderson phosphatic clay 1990s-2004, abandoned (radioactivity/metals, no blooms to test). Since 2021 NOAA NCCOS PCMHAB + Florida Red Tide Initiative fund Mote/WHOI MCII mesocosms and one permitted demonstration in SW Florida (to Aug 2026). Not adopted because of "lack of data on U.S. ecosystems and species" and the fear that the treatment "may be viewed as more damaging than the HAB itself" (NCCOS; Anderson Lab).
- Japan: 1980s aerial trials, 200 g/m2. Europe: none.

## 4. Environmental effects

- Oxygen/sediment: lab DO minima improved in treated tanks; Florida field work saw bottom DO rise right after treatment; long-term bottom oxygen is an explicit open question (Anderson Lab); 2025 Mote mesocosm no DO difference. Modified clay "sandwich" stopped phosphate release for 35 d.
- Toxins in floc (2025): sediment brevetoxin 191 -> ~47 ng/g over 72 h; clam tissue toxin unchanged; clams took up ~5x more aluminum (12.6-20 vs <=4.2 mg/kg), judged not a concern; authors warn about sediment toxin accumulation.
- 2021 eco-environmental assessment (Song, Zhang, Yu, Harmful Algae 105:102237): no harm to nontarget organisms or benthos in Chinese field applications; Fangchenggang benthic metals unchanged.
- Zooplankton/bivalves: rotifer and mussel unharmed at 0.1 g/L; but natural loess cut bay-scallop clearance at 0.01 g/L (Shumway 2003). Cyst formation inhibited above 0.4 g/L but cyst density higher at 0.2-0.4 g/L (A. pacificum): a real trade-off for LIS Alexandrium.
- Aluminum: no seawater criterion; a CT trial would need its own Al monitoring. Seagrass: not addressed anywhere.

## 5. Cost

Materials US$31.75/ha at 0.1 g/L (Malaysia) to US$94/ha at 0.5 g/L (Australia, 700 kg/ha); ~$178k for a 1,900 ha bloom. Field dose 4-10 t/km2 (modified) vs 384 t/km2 (loess). No $/t for IOCAS clay published; vessel and labour not itemised. Do-nothing baseline: Korean losses $10-60M per major event.

## 6. Regulatory (US, CT)

No EPA or NOAA general approval. Only permitted US work is the NCCOS PCMHAB Florida demonstration under a programmatic environmental assessment. Connecticut: DEEP LWRD regulates "dumping, filling or depositing" in tidal waters (CGS 22a-359 to 363f), plus tidal wetlands, coastal management, CWA 401, and the Army Corps programmatic general permit. *Inferred:* a clay slurry release is also a pollutant discharge under CGS 22a-430; a field trial needs a discharge permit plus DEEP/Corps authorisation. Student scope = bench or contained mesocosm.

## 7. Bench protocol

Cultures (NCMA, Bigelow): CCMP1332 Skeletonema marinoi, isolated at Milford CT (Long Island Sound), f/2 or L1, 14 C; CCMP452 Heterosigma akashiwo, isolated in LIS, 20 C; CCMP696 Prorocentrum cordatum (= P. minimum), East Massapequa NY, 20 C, toxic. Prices $27.50-$1,265 by product type. f/2 medium sold premixed. Grow at 20-50 umol photons/m2/s, 12:12; filtered LIS seawater preferred over artificial (ionic strength matters).
Clay: kaolinite + PAC at 5:1 (IOCAS) or 1:1, 24 h ageing, 25-40 g/L stock. Dose series 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1 g/L; untreated and natural-kaolinite controls; sample at 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 5, 24 h just below the surface. Metric RE (%) = (1 - Ct/C0) x 100, or control-normalised. Count Lugol-fixed samples in a hemocytometer; in-vivo fluorescence is what the IOCAS dose table keys on; chl-a absorbance 665/750 nm and turbidity as secondary proxies. Include a 7-30 d regrowth check and two initial densities (1e4 and 1e5 cells/mL) to measure the dose-density relationship. Safety: PAC is an acidic aluminum coagulant; gloves, eye protection, ventilation; Al floc as lab waste.

## 8. Pairing a forecast with clay

No paper proposes forecast-triggered clay dispersal. Closest precedents: Fangchenggang's threshold-triggered applications; Qinhuangdao's pre-emptive isolation belt; the US 10,981,813 monitoring-keyed dose table; A. pacificum paper: early-stage removal "more cost-effective due to lower densities, smaller area coverage, and lower treatment concentrations"; 2025 WA review: treatment "should be coupled with monitoring". *Inferred:* a forecast that flags a station days ahead lets a fixed clay budget hit lower densities and smaller areas; the dose needed for >=90% removal at 1e4 vs 1e5 cells/mL is the direct bench test of that claim.

## Bottom line

1. Established: PAC-kaolinite reverses clay charge and cuts field dose ~50-100x to 4-10 t/km2; Korea and China have used clay operationally for decades; low-motility diatoms like Skeletonema are the easiest targets, Heterosigma the hardest.
2. Established: 0.1-0.5 g/L gives 70-100% removal within 2-5 h for most species, with regrowth suppression natural clay lacks.
3. Contested: long-term benthic effects, cyst promotion at sub-optimal doses, aluminum and toxin fate in floc, bivalve filtration. Chinese assessments say no harm; US work is cautious and mid-project.
4. Unknown: cost per tonne in US conditions; no US or CT permit path beyond NOAA's Florida PEA; no seawater Al criterion.
5. Defensible student claim: a jar-test dose-response on LIS-native NCMA strains at two cell densities quantifies the clay saved by treating earlier, the parameter a forecast-triggered strategy needs, and one no published study reports for Long Island Sound species.
