Left wing: the question

1 · Question

12 × 5 in · 30 pt
Can a coastal station forecast its own harmful algal bloom a week ahead, and what sets the limit?

Harmful blooms close shellfish beds and beaches with days of warning at best. Every monitoring station already logs chlorophyll, oxygen, temperature and salinity. Is the run-up to a bloom visible in that record, and if so, why are forecasts in some bays sharp and in others blunt?

2 · Hypotheses

12 × 7.5 in · 24 pt
  • H1 A 7-day bloom-onset probability can be forecast from a station's own recent record with AUC above 0.7 and precision above the base rate.
  • H2 What limits precision is bloom rarity, not model class, features, data volume or sampling frequency. Pre-registered 2026-09-01.
  • H3 A model trained in Narragansett Bay transfers to other water bodies with no retraining, after rescaling chlorophyll to the site's own distribution.
  • H4 Skill holds on forecasts issued before the outcome is known. Protocol frozen 2026-09-06.

Keep the italics: judges look for pre-registration, and the dates prove it.

3 · Data

12 × 8 in text + 10 × 4 in map strip · 22 pt
Two bays, two sampling regimes, then the world
  • Long Island Sound (CT DEEP): 50 stations, 1993–2025, boat visits every 2–3 weeks, 11,447 station-days, lab chlorophyll.
  • Narragansett Bay (RIDEM sondes): 18 stations, 2005–2023, readings every 15 minutes, 4.5 million rows aggregated to 42,315 station-days, fluorescence chlorophyll.
  • Transfer set: six foreign networks (Chesapeake, NERRS, UK shelf, Australia, Lake Erie, SF Bay; 144,475 station-days) and 87 public ERDDAP sonde sites on three continents.
Map stripCrop the top of nar_fig11_coverage_map.png to the site dots only; caption "87 sites the exported model runs on today".

4 · Method

12 × 7 in · 20 pt
  • Label: daily chlorophyll above 10 µg/L (or the site's own 75th percentile) within the next 7 days; 21 days for boat data.
  • Features: the station's own history only. Chlorophyll lags and rolling means, trend, anomaly versus climatology, oxygen, temperature, salinity, month. Nothing from the future.
  • Models: logistic regression and gradient boosting, trained on early years, tested on years never touched during development.
  • Scoring: onset rows only (today below threshold), so persistence cannot inflate skill. Precision always beside its base rate. Lift = precision ÷ base rate. 95 % CIs by station-year clustered bootstrap.
  • Every test pre-registered: criterion written down before the run, negative results kept.

6 · Result 1: it works

11 × 8 in figure · 22 pt caption
Figurenar_fig4_precision_comparison.png (model vs always-alert, climatology, chlorophyll rules; onset rows, with CIs). Alternative if it prints cleaner: nar_fig5_skill_vs_horizon.png.
Narragansett: precision 0.66 [0.62, 0.69] at onset, nine held-out years

Lift 2.5 over the base rate; beats every trivial rule. Long Island Sound with the same recipe: AUC 0.875, but precision 0.14 at 0.875 recall. The ranking skill is real in both bays. The precision is not.

7 · Result 2: why the Sound is capped

11 × 8 in figure · 22 pt caption
Figurefig_lr_geometry.png, bottom two panels only (densities along the decision axis, and stacked counts). Re-export those two panels side by side at 11 × 5.5 in; the scatter panels are for the paper, not the board.
Same separability, ten times rarer

Project every test day onto the model's own decision axis. The bloom and no-bloom classes overlap by 0.44 in the Sound and 0.52 in Narragansett: the Sound separates better. But blooms are 3.6 % of onset days there against 35 %, so any cut that catches most Sound blooms also catches hundreds of quiet days. Precision 0.11 vs 0.64 from the same geometry.

9 · Result 3: it transfers

11 × 7.5 in figure · 22 pt caption
Figurenar_fig11_coverage_map.png (world map, every site colored by onset lift).
One bay's model, 74 sites, no retraining

Exported once, chlorophyll rescaled to each site's own distribution. Median lift 1.58, AUC 0.74; the confidence interval clears 1.0 at 67 sites across the Gulf of Mexico, California, the Great Lakes, Alaska, British Columbia and the Pacific islands. Refitting on a site's own data did not beat it at 8 of 11 tries.

10 · Decision value

11 × 7.5 in figure · 22 pt caption
Figurefig_decision_value.png (visits per confirmed bloom, calendar vs alert-directed, both bays).
What a manager gets: half the boat trips per bloom found

At eight station visits a month, one confirmed bloom costs 15.4 calendar visits in the Sound and 8.3 alert-directed; 3.8 versus 1.4 in Narragansett. The forecast is a triage tool, and that is the unit to sell it in.

11 · The sentence

24 × 4.5 in · 36 pt, orange rule above
In a bloom-rare system, no sampling cadence and no model class produces high-precision alerts, because precision is bounded by rarity. The actionable quantity is lift over the calendar, and it is 2 to 3× in both bays.

13 · Prospective test

12 × 7 in · 20 pt + results box
Forecasts issued before the outcome is known

Protocol frozen 2026-09-06 (version 1.2): one model, threshold 0.50, 20 live stations in four groups, per-station bloom definition fixed in advance, expected lift band written down per group. Weekly forecasts logged to a public ledger.

Results box: leave a 12 × 3 in white space for the fall-season pooled lift per group, filled in January. Until then the box reads "n = __ forecasts, __ positives, lift __ [__, __]".

First real issuance waits on Form 1A sign-off. If the season is short, show the dry-run health check (10 of 20 feeds live) and say so.

14 · Conclusions

12 × 8.5 in · 22 pt
  • Bloom onset is forecastable a week ahead from a station's own record; ranking skill is 0.81–0.88 AUC in both bays.
  • Alert precision is set by bloom rarity, not by model, features, data volume or cadence. Every one of those was tested and excluded.
  • The Sound's blooms became rare in the lab record in 2014; satellite chlorophyll shows no such step, so the record, not the water, is the likelier cause. Under review with CT DEEP.
  • The precursor signature exports: 67 of 74 sites worldwide gain from a model that never saw them.
  • The right unit for a manager is visits per confirmed bloom, and the forecast roughly halves it.

15 · Limitations

12 × 5 in · 18 pt
  • Sonde fluorescence reads 1.3–1.6× above lab chlorophyll; both bays use the same 10 µg/L cut.
  • Findings are correlational: low oxygen marks bloom-prone water, it does not cause blooms.
  • Sound decision-value numbers rest on 48 blooms and carry wide intervals.
  • Nothing is prospective until the ledger fills.
