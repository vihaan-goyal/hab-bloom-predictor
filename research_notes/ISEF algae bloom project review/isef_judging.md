# How Regeneron ISEF Is Judged (2026-2027 season), with ENEV, Team, Rules and ML Specifics

Researched 2026-09-27. VERIFIED = read on the linked page this session. INFERRED = my reasoning from verified material; not stated by a source. Society for Science pages fetched were already labelled for the 2026-2027 competition year / ISEF 2027 unless noted.

## 1. What are the official ISEF judging criteria and weights (science vs engineering tracks)?

### Takeaway
Both tracks are scored out of 100. Presentation is worth 35 (poster 10, interview 25), Creativity & Potential Impact 20, Execution 20, Design & Methodology 15, and Research Question/Problem 10. The two tracks differ only in sections I-III: science asks for a testable question with variables and controls, while engineering asks for a practical need with criteria and constraints, alternatives explored, and a prototype tested under multiple conditions. The interview alone, at 25 points, outweighs every other single line.

### Cited Findings
- VERIFIED. Science track: I. Research Question 10 (clear, focused purpose; says what it adds to the field; testable by scientific methods). II. Design & Methodology 15 ("well designed plan and data collection methods"; variables and controls defined, appropriate and complete). III. Execution: Data Collection, Analysis & Interpretation 20 (systematic collection; reproducibility of results; "appropriate application of mathematical and statistical methods"; enough data to support the conclusions). IV. Creativity & Potential Impact 20. V. Presentation 35. — [Society for Science, Grand Award Judging Criteria](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/)
- VERIFIED. Engineering track: I. Research Problem 10 ("description of a practical need or problem to be solved"; criteria for the proposed solution; constraints explained). II. Design & Methodology 15 (alternatives explored; solution identified; prototype or model developed). III. Execution: Construction and Testing 20 (the prototype shows the intended design; "tested in multiple conditions/trials"; "demonstrates engineering skill and completeness"). IV and V are the same as the science track. — [Grand Award Judging Criteria](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/)
- VERIFIED. Presentation 35 = Poster 10 (logical organization, clear graphics and legends, supporting documentation) + Interview 25. The interview is scored on "clear, concise, thoughtful responses to questions", understanding of the basic science, understanding of how to interpret the results and their limitations, how independently the student did the work, recognition of the potential impact, quality of ideas for further research, and, for teams, "contributions to and understanding of project by all members". — [Grand Award Judging Criteria](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/)
- VERIFIED. The Creativity guidance stresses "imagination and inventiveness" and "different perspectives that open up new possibilities". Creativity can be shown in any of criteria I-III, and impact can be in the field, technology, the economy, the environment or society. — [Grand Award Judging Criteria](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/)
- VERIFIED. How judges assess independence: for work done at a lab or institution, they use Form 1C. For work done at home or school, they assess how much mentoring the student received. — [Grand Award Judging Criteria](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/)
- VERIFIED. Judging logistics for ISEF 2027: interviews are in 15-minute periods on Wednesday, May 12, 2027, from 9:30 a.m. Category winners are decided in judge caucuses. More than 1,000 Grand Award judges are needed across 22 categories. A judge needs 6+ years of related professional experience beyond a bachelor's degree, a PhD/MD or equivalent, or 4+ years of doctoral research. K-12 teachers and fair directors cannot judge. — [Society for Science, Grand Award Judging](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/)
- VERIFIED. ISEF 2026 category award amounts: First $6,000, Second $2,400, Third $1,200, Fourth $600. — [ISEF 2026 Full Awards](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-2026-full-awards/)
- Secondary claim with a conflict: Lumiere Education says "Engineering Goals" is worth 30 points for individual projects and 25 for team projects. — [Lumiere](https://www.lumiere-education.com/post/isef-the-ultimate-guide). The official criteria page gives no such individual/team split, so treat this as outdated or wrong. [Official criteria](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/)
- Secondary: "each entry is judged at least four times". — [sciencefair.io](https://www.sciencefair.io/blog/judges-look-for-winning-isef-project), via a search snippet; not confirmed on an official page.

### Inferences
- INFERRED: The project mixes a forecasting model (science: hypothesis, AUC, lift) with a clay-and-magnet retrieval device (engineering). ENEV is an engineering category, but the rubric depends on the track the student picks, not the category. If the pitch is engineering, the device and alert system need stated criteria and constraints (cost, recovery fraction, lead time, false-alarm budget), alternatives that were considered, and testing across multiple conditions and trials.
- INFERRED: Pre-registered predictions and bootstrap confidence intervals fit criterion III ("appropriate statistical methods", "reproducibility") directly.
- INFERRED: Being honest about limitations is scored explicitly in the interview line. The project's label-rebuild story (sensor vs lab chlorophyll, and precision falling from 0.50 to 0.32) is an asset in the interview, not a weakness, as long as the student explains it themselves.

### Gaps
- There is no official public score-to-award mapping. Society for Science does not publish how caucuses turn scores into places.
- The "judged at least four times" figure was not confirmed on a primary source.

## 2. What do ENEV and environmental judges value (real-world impact, field validation, feasibility, cost, scale-up)?

### Takeaway
ENEV is defined as engineering processes or infrastructure that solve problems in water supply, waste disposal or pollution control. The subcategories include Bioremediation, Pollution Control and Water Resources Management. The 2026 winners at the top of environmental categories built working field hardware or treatment materials with a clear deployment story. There are no published ENEV-specific criteria, so impact and feasibility are scored through the general "Creativity & Potential Impact" line and the engineering Execution line.

### Cited Findings
- VERIFIED. ENEV "includes studies that engineer or develop processes and infrastructure to solve environmental problems in the supply of water, the disposal of waste, or the control of pollution". Subcategories: Bioremediation, Land Reclamation, Pollution Control, Recycling and Waste Management, Water Resources Management. — [Society for Science, Environmental Engineering](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/categories-and-subcategories/environmental-engineering/)
- VERIFIED. ISEF 2026 ENEV First Awards: Evan Budz (Canada), in-situ microplastic detection using holographic imaging and AI on an autonomous bionic sea turtle, which also won the Gordon E. Moore Award; and Bennett Huang & Jason Pan (Virginia), a team project on a robotic precision-spraying system for targeted pest control. A Regeneron Young Scientist Award went to Lakshmi Agrawal (Washington) for stormwater remediation with nanocellulose "hydrosponges". EAEV First Awards included Tina Jin (Massachusetts), sustainable bone filtration for water treatment. — [ISEF 2026 Full Awards](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-2026-full-awards/)
- VERIFIED. Directly comparable 2026 projects:
  - Prayrona Choudhury (Hanford HS, WA) took EAEV 2nd place for "AquaShift: Synchronized Chemical Equilibrium Convergence as a Leading Indicator of Phycocyanin-Threshold Cyanobacterial Bloom Onset Across Contrasting Freshwater Systems". — [Richland SD news](https://www.rsd.edu/news-announcements/news-details/~board/district-news/post/rsd-students-earn-international-recognition-at-regeneron-isef); [ISEF 2026 Full Awards](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-2026-full-awards/)
  - Srihaan Seelam (NY) won an award for ML prediction of global coral bleaching. — [ISEF 2026 Full Awards](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-2026-full-awards/)
- VERIFIED. 2026 ENEV special-award winners (Aramco, KACST, National Taiwan Science Education Center) were mostly capture, sorbent or valorization devices, for example ENEV038 "Energy-Efficient CO2 Capture System" and ENEV050 "Functionalized Bio-Composite Sorbent". — [ISEF 2026 Special Awards](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-2026-special-awards-ceremony/)

### Inferences
- INFERRED: The pattern across 2026 environmental winners is working physical systems deployed in or tested against real field samples, with a quantified performance metric. A forecast-only project competes in EAEV against entries like AquaShift. A forecast plus a physical retrieval device with a measured recovery fraction fits the ENEV "pollution control / water resources management" profile better.
- INFERRED: Field validation, cost per unit and a credible deployment partner (CT DEEP) are the natural answers to the rubric's "impact on environment/society". Judges with 6+ years of professional experience will ask about scale-up and cost.

### Gaps
- There is no official ENEV-specific rubric. No public statements from ENEV judges were found this session.

## 3. What do judges and past winners say separates Grand Award winners from finalists?

### Takeaway
Across official and secondary sources the message is the same. The interview (25 points) decides outcomes. Judges test whether the student owns and understands the work, including limitations, independence and next steps. Honest "I don't know, but here is how I'd find out" answers score better than bluffing.

### Cited Findings
- VERIFIED (official). The interview is scored on understanding of the basic science, interpretation, limitations, independence, impact and future research ideas. — [Grand Award Judging Criteria](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/)
- Secondary (coaching sites, not judges on record):
  - Judges ask what you personally did, why you chose the method, what the limitations are, and what comes next. If you cannot answer, say so and explain how you would find out. Judges score honesty above a confident guess that collapses on follow-up. — [YRI Science](https://www.yriscience.com/blog/how-to-win-isef-interview) (via search summary)
  - Top prizes often go to projects that "look simpler on the surface but are far deeper underneath". Many finalists over-invest in the poster and under-prepare for the conversation. — [sciencefair.io](https://www.sciencefair.io/blog/judges-look-for-winning-isef-project) (via search summary)
  - Prepare 1-, 3- and 10-minute versions. Don't memorize a script, and practise on non-scientists. — [Science Buddies judging tips](https://www.sciencebuddies.org/science-fair-projects/competitions/judging-tips-for-top-science-competitions) (via search summary; direct fetch returned 403)
- VERIFIED. For continuation projects, judges "focus ONLY on the current year's work" and use Form 7 to see what was done this year. The rules FAQ adds: "Judges like to see exactly what you have done this year, what you learned from your previous work, how last year's study led to this one." — [Criteria](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/); [ISEF Rules FAQ](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/faq/)

### Inferences
- INFERRED: Common reasons strong projects lose:
  - a team member can't explain another member's part;
  - the student can't explain code or statistics they used (a real risk given heavy AI-assisted coding);
  - claims go beyond the evidence, for example "prevents blooms";
  - the student can't state the baseline or the limitations;
  - the student can't separate this year's work from earlier work.

### Gaps
- There are no first-hand, attributable Grand Award judge interviews on record (Science News Explores / Science Buddies pages were blocked or not found). The "what separates winners" advice here is mostly from coaching firms with a commercial interest. Weight it accordingly.

## 4. Rules for a team project using non-toxic algae cultures, oysters, H2O2/CaO2 and state agency data; continuation and AI rules

### Takeaway
Nothing in the planned design is prohibited, but several items need forms and pre-approval:
- Algae and cyanobacteria cultures: cyanobacteria are on the potentially hazardous microorganisms list, so SRC pre-approval, Form 6A and a lab setting (not home) are needed if they are cultured.
- Peroxides: Form 3 risk assessment, SDS and EPA-compliant disposal.
- Oysters: invertebrates are not covered by the vertebrate rules.
- Students may NOT collect water during an active HAB.
- The team must be 3 or fewer members and fixed once it competes.
- Research counts only within 12 continuous months, starting no earlier than January 2026 (for ISEF 2027).
- Generative-AI use must be logged and cited. AI may never write the research plan, abstract, poster or conclusions.

### Cited Findings
- VERIFIED (2026-2027 rules). Teams have "no more than three members". Each member submits a Form 1B, and the team jointly submits Form 1, 1A, the Research Plan and the abstract. Once a project has competed at any fair, membership cannot change and the project cannot switch between individual and team. — [Rules for All Projects](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/rules-for-all-projects/)
- VERIFIED. Teams and individuals are judged together "only on basis of quality". All members must show significant contributions and understanding. — [Grand Award Judging Criteria](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/)
- VERIFIED. Continuation projects:
  - Form 7 is required and must be displayed at the booth.
  - Judging covers only work "over 12 continuous months", starting no earlier than January 2026 and ending May 2027.
  - "Repetition of previous experimentation with the same methodology and research question, even with an increased sample size, is an example of an unacceptable continuation."
  - Boards may show earlier conclusions but not earlier raw data.
  - [Rules for All Projects](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/rules-for-all-projects/)
- VERIFIED. "The start date of your project is when you begin to collect data"; literature review and study design come before it. — [ISEF Rules FAQ](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/faq/)
- VERIFIED. The Research Plan (with Form 1A) must be written before experimentation. It includes the rationale, question/hypothesis/engineering goal, materials, procedures, risk assessment, data analysis and bibliography. — [Rules for All Projects](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/rules-for-all-projects/)
- VERIFIED. Biological agents:
  - Cyanobacteria are listed as potentially hazardous microorganisms that need prior approval.
  - Exempt: "studies involving water or soil microbes not concentrated in media conducive to their microbial growth", and studies with protists (Form 3 only).
  - Culturing potentially hazardous agents at home is prohibited.
  - Students cannot collect samples during an HAB. "Qualified personnel may collect water samples during an HAB and provide the student with the samples for testing."
  - Releasing non-native, genetically altered or invasive species into the environment is prohibited.
  - A Qualified Scientist is required for BSL-2 work.
  - [Potentially Hazardous Biological Agents](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/potentially-hazardous-biological-agents/)
- VERIFIED. Vertebrate rules cover "all nonhuman vertebrates (including fish)" and cephalopods. Other invertebrates, such as oysters, are not covered. — [Vertebrate Animals rules](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/vertebrate-animals/)
- VERIFIED. Hazardous chemicals:
  - The risk assessment must be done with a Direct Supervisor or Qualified Scientist before experimentation.
  - Students must read the SDS for each chemical.
  - Disposal must be described "in sufficient detail to ensure compliance with EPA Guidelines", following local, state and federal rules.
  - Carcinogens, teratogens and reproductive toxins must be used at a Regulated Research Institution or in a restricted fume hood.
  - Household equipment cannot be reused.
  - Form 3 is required.
  - [Hazardous Chemicals, Activities or Devices](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/hazardous-chemicals-activities-or-devices/)
- VERIFIED. IRB/SRC approval is required before experimentation for human participants, vertebrates, and potentially hazardous biological agents or tissues. A Qualified Scientist is required for BSL-2 and DEA-controlled substances. — [Rules for All Projects](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/rules-for-all-projects/)
- VERIFIED. Society for Science generative-AI table (Oct 2025; applies to ISEF 2026 rules):
  - Using AI to write initial code is acceptable "only with explicit citation stating which portions of the code were AI generated and with a log of the prompts".
  - Using AI to pick statistical tests is acceptable with a prompt log, but "interpretation of data must be done by the student".
  - Using AI to initially write the research plan, abstract, paper or poster is "Never acceptable".
  - Using AI to produce conclusions or future steps is "Never acceptable".
  - Using AI to produce a starter bibliography or supporting citations is "Never acceptable".
  - Grammar and syntax polishing of a student-written abstract is fine.
  - Affiliated fairs may be stricter.
  - [Generative AI Use Table (PDF)](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2026/Rules/Generative-AI-Use-Table.pdf)

### Inferences
- INFERRED: The earlier forecasting work was done largely before or around 2026. If it is shown, it must be framed as a continuation with Form 7, and the 2026-27 year must add a new variable or a new line of work, such as the device bench tests or the lab-label rebuild. Rerunning the same model on more data does not count.
- INFERRED: Using CT DEEP public monitoring data needs no IRB/SRC biological approval, since no humans or vertebrates are involved. Still cite the source and the data agreement.
- INFERRED: Non-toxic algae strains from a reputable supplier (not cyanobacteria) would likely sit in the lower-risk protist/algae track. Culturing cyanobacteria, even non-toxic ones, triggers pre-approval and a lab setting. Confirm with the local SRC (CSEF) before starting.
- INFERRED: Calcium peroxide and hydrogen peroxide added to algae or oyster tanks need a disposal plan (neutralizing or diluting the peroxide) that is written into the Research Plan. No field release into Long Island Sound without agency permits.
- INFERRED: Given the project's heavy use of Claude for code, the team needs a prompt log and a statement of which code was AI-generated. The interview will test whether each student can explain that code.

### Gaps
- The biological-agents page did not say explicitly whether eukaryotic microalgae cultures count as "protists". Ask the SRC.
- No explicit ISEF rule on using government datasets was found. It is not regulated as far as found.
- The CSEF (Connecticut) affiliated-fair rules and dates were not checked.

## 5. How are team projects and ML/data-science projects evaluated?

### Takeaway
Team projects use the same 100-point rubric. The only extra check is that every member shows contribution and understanding. ISEF publishes no ML-specific criteria. ML work is scored through criterion III ("appropriate statistical methods", reproducibility) and the interview. The ML-methods literature names the checks expert judges will probe: data leakage (preprocessing outside CV folds, random splits on time series), missing baselines, and test-set reuse.

### Cited Findings
- VERIFIED. Team projects are judged together with individual projects on quality alone. The interview line includes "contributions to and understanding of project by all members". — [Grand Award Judging Criteria](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/)
- VERIFIED. The ML-pitfalls guide for academic researchers covers splitting data correctly, avoiding test-set leakage, baselines and fair comparisons. — [Lones, "How to avoid machine learning pitfalls" (arXiv 2108.02497)](https://arxiv.org/html/2108.02497v5)
- VERIFIED. Leakage is defined as information about the holdout set being available during training. In k-fold CV, preprocessing must happen inside each fold. — [MachineLearningMastery](https://machinelearningmastery.com/data-preparation-without-data-leakage/). For weather and climate ML, time-ordered and grouped validation are preferred over random k-fold. — [ECMWF slides, Kuehnert](https://events.ecmwf.int/event/466/contributions/4981/attachments/2833/5573/01_Kuehnert_ML%20Validation%20and%20Data%20Leakage.pdf)
- VERIFIED. 2026 examples show ML environmental projects do win: the AI-based microplastic detection project (Budz) won the Gordon E. Moore Award, and ML coral-bleaching prediction (Seelam) won an award. — [ISEF 2026 Full Awards](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-2026-full-awards/)

### Inferences
- INFERRED: Expect judges to ask:
  - What is your baseline? (climatology or persistence; this project already has lift against climatology)
  - Is the split temporal? (the test set is 2023-2025, so yes)
  - Was any feature built using future information? (rolling-window alignment)
  - How many events are in the test set? (the S4 check has only 15 events)
  - Why AUC and not precision/recall at an operating threshold?
- INFERRED: The project's existing practices are strong material for Execution and Creativity if presented clearly:
  - pre-registration;
  - the two negative NN results;
  - the relabel after the instrument audit;
  - confidence intervals.
- INFERRED: For team projects, split ownership clearly, for example Student A the model and Student B the device, but both must be able to answer questions about both.

### Gaps
- There is no public statement from ISEF (SOFT/ROBO/ENEV) judges about ML-specific red flags. The expectations above come from the general ML-methods literature.

## 6. Special awards relevant to water and algal-bloom projects

### Takeaway
The special-award lineup changes every year. NOAA did not give a Special Award at ISEF 2026. The American Meteorological Society considers atmospheric, oceanic and hydrologic projects. The 2026 environmental special awards came from Aramco, KACST, Taiwan NTSEC and others, alongside AI (AAAI, ACM) and math (AMS) awards. The U.S. Stockholm Junior Water Prize is a separate path (state, then national, then international), with an April 15 deadline and teams of at most two.

### Cited Findings
- VERIFIED. "NOAA did not offer a Special Award at the 2026 Regeneron ISEF", although NOAA has historically been a Special Award Organization offering cash awards. — [NOAA ISEF page](https://www.noaa.gov/office-education/outreach-communication/international-science-and-engineering-fair) (via search snippet; the winners page returned 403)
- VERIFIED (snippet). The American Meteorological Society gives certificates of Outstanding Achievement and considers "only projects relating to atmospheric and related oceanic and hydrologic sciences". — [Society for Science SAO listing / search result](https://www.societyforscience.org/get-involved/special-award-organizations/)
- VERIFIED. The 2026 special awards totalled more than $6 million and included Aramco (energy and environmental stewardship; ENEV038, ENEV068), KACST (ENEV028), NTSEC (ENEV050), ACM, AAAI, AMS, and the Patent and Trademark Office Society. No EPA, AGU, ASA or SJWP award appeared in the 2026 list. — [ISEF 2026 Special Awards](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-2026-special-awards-ceremony/)
- VERIFIED. The current SAO page lists DataClassroom and COMAP (statistics and modelling), AMS, Sigma Xi, PTOS, ACS and AAAI. The American Statistical Association was not found on it. — [Special Award Organizations](https://www.societyforscience.org/get-involved/special-award-organizations/)
- VERIFIED (2026 cycle page). U.S. Stockholm Junior Water Prize (run by the Water Environment Federation):
  - Eligibility: grades 9-12, age 15+ by August 1, teams of up to 2 students.
  - Scope: water quality, water resources or treatment topics, with "scientifically accepted methodologies … including statistical analysis".
  - Deadline for the state level: April 15; state winners are named in May; national virtual judging is in May; the top 20 compete in person in June (Chicago in 2026).
  - Judging covers relevance, creativity, methodology, subject knowledge, practical skills and the report. The research paper counts much more than the poster.
  - [WEF SJWP Eligibility](https://www.wef.org/membership--community/students--young-professionals/sjwp/sjwp-eligibility-and-entry-requirements/)
  - A 2027 deadline of April 15, 2027 is reported by [Scholarships.com](https://www.scholarships.com/scholarships/u-s-stockholm-junior-water-prize) (secondary).

### Inferences
- INFERRED: The SJWP 2-person team cap is stricter than ISEF's 3. A 3-person ISEF team cannot enter SJWP as-is.
- INFERRED: Because SJWP weights the written paper heavily, a strong paper is a second path that doesn't depend on booth judging.
- INFERRED: Watch the ISEF 2027 SAO list (usually published in spring) for AMS, any NOAA return, AAAI/ACM (the ML side) and environmental sponsors. Do not plan around NOAA or EPA being there.

### Gaps
- The full ISEF 2027 Special Award Organization list is not yet published or was not found.
- There is no confirmation of ASA, AGU or EPA participation in 2027.
- The AMS criteria were seen only as a search snippet.
