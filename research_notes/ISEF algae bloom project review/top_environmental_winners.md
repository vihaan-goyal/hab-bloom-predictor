# Top Regeneron ISEF Environmental Winners (ENEV / EAEV), 2018-2026

Scope note: VERIFIED = stated on the cited page (fetched 2026-09-27). INFERRED = my reading, not stated by a source. "Our project" = ML bloom forecast for Long Island Sound (CT DEEP, ~30 yr, S1 lab label, test AUC 0.804) plus an Arduino forecast-triggered treatment loop bench-tested in tanks.

## Who won the top awards in ENEV/EAEV each year 2018-2026, and what did they do?

### Takeaway
Environmental and water projects have won top awards often: the Gordon E. Moore Award went to water-pollution devices in 2024 (ultrasonic microplastic filter) and 2026 (AI microplastic-sensing robot turtle). A 2026 Young Scientist Award went to a tire-pollutant (6PPD-quinone) sponge. Nearly every top environmental winner is a **physical device or material with a measured removal or detection result**, not a pure analysis.

### Cited Findings

**Top overall awards that went to environmental, water or pollution projects**
- 2026, Gordon E. Moore Award ($50,000) and ENEV category winner: **Evan Budz**, 15, Burlington, Ontario. "In Situ Microplastic Detection Using Holographic Imaging and AI on an Autonomous Bionic Sea Turtle." He turned his earlier self-propelled turtle robot into a real-time microplastic detector with a 3D holographic camera plus AI. It detects particles "smaller than blood cells" with 94% accuracy and was used to monitor microplastics across 10 bodies of water. VERIFIED: [SfS 2026 top awards](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-2026-grand-awards/), [Youth Science Canada](https://youthscience.ca/team-canada-isef-2026-awards/)
- 2026, Regeneron Young Scientist Award ($75,000): **Lakshmi Agrawal**, 18, Bellevue, WA. "Nanocellulose hydrosponges for 6PPD-Q removal": plant-waste sponges that remove a tire pollutant that kills salmon from water with 80% efficiency, using 85% less production energy than alternatives. Framed around Puget Sound streams. VERIFIED: [SfS 2026](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-2026-grand-awards/)
- 2025, EAEV First Award: **Lakshmi Agrawal**, "A Novel Method of Water Purification: Optimizing Foam Fractionation for Effective Broad-Spectrum Removal of DBP Precursors in Chlorinated Drinking Water." So she made a two-year climb on water treatment, from category first place to a Young Scientist Award. VERIFIED: [SfS 2025 full awards](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-2025-full-awards/)
- 2025, Regeneron Young Scientist Award: **Siyaa Poddar**, Arizona College Prep HS. "Developing a Rapid, Colorimetric Metal Organic Framework (MOF) for Airborne Silica and Uranium Detection," a detector for hazardous airborne pollutants affecting Indigenous communities. VERIFIED: [SfS 2025 full awards](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-2025-full-awards/)
- 2025, Regeneron Young Scientist Award: **Benjamin Davis**, Bishop Feehan HS, MA. A single machine that combines pultrusion and extrusion to recycle PET-bottle waste and 3D-printing purge into filament. VERIFIED: [same](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-2025-full-awards/)
- 2024, Gordon E. Moore Award ($50,000) and EAEV First Award: **Justin Huang and Victoria Ou**, both 17, The Woodlands, TX. A prototype filter that uses ultrasonic (acoustic-force) waves to remove microplastics from water. It removed "between 84% and 94% of the suspended microplastic particles" in single-pass **laboratory** tests, and they were scaling it up. VERIFIED: [SfS 2024 top winners](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/high-schoolers-win-more-than-9-million-at-regeneron-isef-2024/)

## What patterns recur among winners?

### Takeaway
Top winners pair a **built artifact** (device, material, sensor, robot) with a **hard number** (removal %, accuracy %, fold change), a **named real-world victim or place** (salmon in Puget Sound, Indigenous communities, Oregon shellfish harvesters), and often a **multi-year build-up**. Field or real-environment testing separates the top-award tier from the category tier.

### Cited Findings
- Hard removal or detection numbers are the headline in every water winner: 84-94% microplastic removal (2024 Moore); 94% detection accuracy (2026 Moore); 80% 6PPD-Q removal with 85% less energy (2026 YSA). [2024](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/high-schoolers-win-more-than-9-million-at-regeneron-isef-2024/), [2026](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-2026-grand-awards/)
- Taking the device out of the lab: Budz tested across 10 bodies of water, and the Thai lacewing team reported field-test pest reduction. Huang and Ou won the Moore with **lab-only** single-pass tests plus a stated plan to scale. So field data helps but is not required. [2026](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-2026-grand-awards/), [2023](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/high-schoolers-win-nearly-9-million-at-regeneron-isef-2023/), [2024](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/high-schoolers-win-more-than-9-million-at-regeneron-isef-2024/)
- Multi-year iteration: Budz reused his earlier turtle robot. Agrawal won EAEV First in 2025 (foam fractionation), then a YSA in 2026 (6PPD-Q sponges). [2026](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-2026-grand-awards/), [2025](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-2025-full-awards/)
- Cost and efficiency framing is explicit: "low-cost" (2018 wildfire early warning; 2022 ElSa), "cost-effective, scalable" (2023 DAC), "affordable" (2021 sand filters; 2019 TiO2 filters), and 85% energy reduction (2026). [2018](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/intel-international-science-and-engineering-fair-2018-grand-award-winners/), [2021](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/2021-regeneron-isef-grand-awards/), [2023](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-full-awards-2023/)
- A magnetic or ferrofluid water-cleanup theme recurs at category-winning level: Katie Lu, 2019 EAEV Best of Category; Miriam Haddad, 2025 EAEV First; Raina Jain, 2018 ENEV Best of Category (magnetic Fe3O4 arsenic detection). [2019](https://www.societyforscience.org/blog/five-questions-with-isef-2019-best-of-category-in-earth-and-environmental-sciences-winner-katie-lu/), [2025](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-2025-full-awards/), [2018](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/intel-international-science-and-engineering-fair-2018-grand-award-winners/)
- A flocculation-based water project won ENEV First in 2021: Sahil Azad, natural flocculating proteins for sand filters. [2021](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/2021-regeneron-isef-grand-awards/)

### Inferences
- The difference between tiers seems to be that a top-award winner has a tangible, demo-able object with a single strong percentage, while category winners include pure analysis and modeling (Arctic rivers, landslide analytics, tectonic reconstruction). INFERRED
- Partnerships with agencies or communities are rarely stated in the press releases. They show up as framing (Puget Sound salmon, Indigenous communities) rather than as named partners. Our CT DEEP data partnership would be unusual and worth stating explicitly. INFERRED
- Patents are not mentioned in any of the press releases I read. INFERRED: absence of evidence, not evidence they are absent.
- Comparison with our project: our magnetic-clay retrieval device fits the recurring ferrofluid/magnetic cleanup and flocculation lineage (Lu 2019, Haddad 2025, Azad 2021). The forecast-triggered loop sets it apart from all of these, since none of them couples a prediction to an action. The headline should be one hard number (e.g., recovery fraction, or the current lift in notes/S1_NUMBERS_SHEET.md; the earlier "lift 5.0" was withdrawn 2026-09-28) rather than several caveated metrics. INFERRED

### Gaps
- The press releases do not report judges' scoring rationale, so any claim about "why" a project won is inferred.
- I found no stated cost analyses or patents for the specific winners.

## Are there ML/forecasting environmental projects among top winners, and how were they validated?

### Takeaway
Yes, but ML wins at the top level mostly when it is **embodied in hardware or imaging** (Budz's AI turtle, Estrada's robotic camera, Puri's drone thermal ML). Pure tabular forecasting shows up at category-first level (2018 wildfire early warning, 2018 food-shortage satellite prediction) or in the special-award tier. The closest analog to our project is a harmful-algae biotoxin forecaster, which won special awards and a Davidson Fellowship but, as far as I verified, not a top award.

### Cited Findings
- **Lila Schweinfurth** (Oregon Episcopal School, Portland), ISEF 2021 EAEV008, "Developing a User-Friendly System for Predicting Harmful Levels of Marine Biotoxins." She used 20 years of Oregon shellfish biotoxin data (domoic acid, PSP) plus NASA MODIS-Aqua satellite data, with Gradient Boosting and Random Forest time-series models. Reported "accuracies ranging from 0.90 - 0.99 for up to 5 weeks in advance," with a public web app. VERIFIED: [ISEF project page](https://isef.net/project/eaev008---predicting-harmful-levels-of-marine-biotoxins), [Davidson Fellow 2021](https://www.davidsongifted.org/gifted-programs/fellows-scholarship/fellows/current-and-past-fellows/2021-fellows/lila-schweinfurth/), [NOAA 2021 awards](https://www.noaa.gov/education/news/noaa-award-winners-at-2021-regeneron-isef). Her Grand Award placing is **not verified**. She is listed in the 2021 full awards, but my fetch did not state her level. ([SfS 2021 full](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/2021-regeneron-isef-grand-awards/))
- Also in 2021 EAEV: Katherine McCarthy (EAEV052), nutrient concentrations that may cause harmful algal bloom events, and Conor Casey (EAEV076), ML to improve weather forecasting. Award levels are not verified. [SfS 2021 full](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/2021-regeneron-isef-grand-awards/)
- 2023 EAEV Second Award: **Uma Grover and Melinda Lin**, West Linn HS, OR, "Microscopic Menace: What Are the Effects of Cyanobacteria Blooms on Primary Productivity, Toxicity, and Microorganisms?" VERIFIED: [SfS 2023 full](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-full-awards-2023/)
- 2022 EAEV Third Award: **Eliana Juarez**, NM, ML urban ozone forecasting with policy recommendations. VERIFIED: [SfS 2022 full](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-full-awards-2022/)
- 2018 EAEV Best of Category: a multivariable wildfire early-warning system (Tremsin). 2018 EAEV First: satellite-based prediction of African food shortages (Petersen). VERIFIED: [SfS 2018](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/intel-international-science-and-engineering-fair-2018-grand-award-winners/)
- 2021 Moore: drought-stress prediction from images from a custom robotic camera (Estrada). 2022 Scripps: ElSa poacher detection at 91% accuracy (Puri). [2021](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/2021-regeneron-isef-top-winners/), [2022](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/2022-regeneron-isef-top-winners/)
- A strong AI + IoT wildfire sensor network (Honary, 2025) got only a Second Award in Embedded Systems. VERIFIED: [SfS 2025 full](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-2025-full-awards/)

### Inferences
- Schweinfurth is the direct precedent for a state-data HAB forecast plus public tool. Judges have already seen "20 years of state monitoring data + tree models + web app with 0.90-0.99 accuracy." Our project needs a clear increment beyond that: an honest skill metric (AUC with CI, lift, precision at a base rate of 6.3%), the label-rebuild story (sensor drift confirmed by DEEP), and, above all, the closed-loop actuation. Her "accuracy 0.90-0.99" likely reflects class imbalance. Our more honest metrics may look weaker unless the imbalance is explained up front. INFERRED
- Top-tier ML environmental winners validated with held-out accuracy on real sensor or imagery data from the field. None that I found was validated by an operational deployment with an agency. INFERRED

### Gaps
- I could not verify Schweinfurth's grand-award level or whether she won later ISEF awards.
- Validation details (test splits, baselines) are not given in the press releases for any ML winner.

## Any winners combining sensing + automated intervention (closed-loop systems)?

### Takeaway
There are few, and they are partial. The closest are 2025 ENEV First (XinYan Chen: monitoring plus catalytic breakdown of formaldehyde in one portable device), 2023 ENEV First (Aryan Mago: wearable VOC detection plus mitigation), and 2026 ENEV (Huang and Pan: ML robotic system for crop-protection spraying). I found no top winner that uses a **forecast** to trigger a water treatment.

### Cited Findings
- XinYan Chen, 2025 ENEV First: "A Digital and Portable Integrated Apparatus for Dynamic Monitoring and Highly Efficient Catalysis of Trace Formaldehyde Using Fe-Based TiO2 Single-nanoparticle Photocatalyst." VERIFIED: [SfS 2025](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-2025-full-awards/)
- Aryan Mago, 2023 ENEV First: "Novel Wearable Volatile Organic Compound Detection and Mitigation via Cumulative Solvatochromism and Dietary Modification." VERIFIED: [SfS 2023](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-full-awards-2023/)
- Bennett Huang and Jason Pan, 2026 ENEV category winner: "ML robotic system for dsRNA-MeJA crop protection." VERIFIED: [SfS 2026](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-2026-grand-awards/)
- Vasily Tremsin, 2018 EAEV Best of Category: "Multivariable Early-Warning System for Low-Cost Prevention of Wildfire Proliferation," which pairs warning with prevention. VERIFIED: [SfS 2018](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/intel-international-science-and-engineering-fair-2018-grand-award-winners/)
- Vedant Srinivas, 2022 ENEV First: AI detection of animals plus an automatic driver warning (detect, then act). VERIFIED: [SfS 2022](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-full-awards-2022/)

### Inferences
- A forecast → Arduino → dosing/treatment loop for HABs appears to be a genuinely unoccupied niche among 2018-2026 top environmental winners. That is a differentiation opportunity. The risk is that judges will want tank data that shows the loop improves an outcome, e.g., recovery fraction or chlorophyll reduction against a no-forecast control, reported as a single percentage like the Moore winners. INFERRED
- To match the pattern of winners, the bench loop needs (a) a quantitative end-to-end result, (b) a cost figure, and (c) a named stakeholder (CT DEEP, shellfish beds). INFERRED

### Gaps
- Press releases give too little mechanistic detail to tell whether Chen's or Mago's devices are truly automatic (sensor-triggered) or just co-located. The ISEF abstracts database would settle it but was not fetched.
